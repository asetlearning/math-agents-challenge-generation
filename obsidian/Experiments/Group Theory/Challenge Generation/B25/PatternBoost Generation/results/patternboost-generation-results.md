### Findings
1. **The number of local-search steps dominates.** s = 1 gives 20–54% success, s = 5 gives 78–98%, and s = 10 gives 96–100%, across all beams and pools.
2. **A bigger pool costs roughly linear time for little gain once steps ≥ 5.** For example, at w1.s10: 96% at p100 (96 s), 98% at p1000 (545 s), 96% at p5000 (2157 s). A bigger pool does cut the iterations to a match (3.04 → 1.10), but each iteration costs more.
3. **Beam width above 1 adds cost for marginal gain.** Only w5.s10 reaches 100%, at 3–20× the time of w1.s10.
4. **With a large search effort, the target is often already in the initial pool or reached at iteration 1** (avg iters < 1 at w5.s10). The task is easy partly because the targets come from the same distribution as the initial sample.

### Caveat: what this does and does not show
It shows that the loop **can steer** certified trivial words onto a specified target, reliably, at modest cost. It says **nothing about hardness.** The targets are generic random products of conjugates, which is exactly the "easy, generic" regime described in [[_synthesis-hard-instance-generation]].

## Earlier single-run observations (May 18–20, 2026; owner's notes)
These were runs against one target challenge each.
- **May 18.** Edit and Hamming scorers; one short reduced challenge; structured `[REL_i]`/`[CONJ_j]` tokens with no BPE.
  - The model emits valid factor-word encodings almost without failures.
  - The expanded lengths approach the target length.
  - **Every candidate is easily reducible to 1 by 2.5-reduction.**
  - Hamming is faster but leaves visible powers. Edit scrambles better but is still 2.5-reducible.
  - Local-search time grows linearly with challenge length.
- **May 19.** **Solved** one random challenge in 2 iterations (10 epochs, pool 5000, length 170, 6000 model samples). A longer challenge (length 203) took 3 iterations, with 15 candidates converging, at 5100 samples.
- **May 20.**
  - Small parameters with the edit scorer (beam 1, 3 steps, 1500 samples, pool 1000) took 6 iterations, each much faster.
  - **Hamming with the same parameters stalled**: the maximum score did not move for 38 iterations.
  - With large parameters (pool 5000, beam 1, 10 steps), Hamming also succeeded in 6 iterations.
- **Compressed words.** BPE generator compression worked poorly on random targets, since the vocabulary trained on random words is random apart from short tokens. Untested ideas: tokens derived from the target word itself, and a vocabulary built from 2.5-reduced words.

## Open problems the owner identified (from the notes)
- **Signal.** Hard examples are exponentially rare, so random starts give almost no reward signal. Proposed fixes: bias toward known hard words (challenges, 2.5-reduced KB rules), use more drastic exploration when sampling converges, and possibly add RL (SFT on high-power samples, then RL on reduction).
- **Reduction cost.** Putting reduction into scoring makes scoring too slow for practical local search.
- **Hardness score ideas:** van Kampen diagram area, which links to the `dehn_function` scorer; the ratio of factor count to expanded length; and seeding from 2.5-reduced KB rewrite rules as relators.
