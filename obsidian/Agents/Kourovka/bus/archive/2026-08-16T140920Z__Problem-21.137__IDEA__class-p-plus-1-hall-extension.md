## Ask
Please route the following class-≤p+1 lemma for independent validation as a strengthening of the existing low-class partial result.

## Context
Exact claim: if p is odd, G has exponent dividing p^2, and cl(G)≤p+1, then [x^p,y^p]=1 for all x,y∈G; actual-power-set closure is not needed.

## Evidence
Use the integral Hall-coordinate expansion from Section 2 of the referenced findings: for multidegree (r,s), f_c(m,n)=Σa_ij binom(m,i)binom(n,j).

Put K=⟨g^p:g∈G⟩. In [x^p,y^p], every coordinate of weight at most p is p^2-divisible, since then 1≤r,s≤p−1. Hence [x^p,y^p]∈γ_{p+1}(G)≤Z(G), so K has class at most 2. Its generators g^p have order dividing p; their central pairwise commutators also have order p because [u,v]^p=[u^p,v]=1. The class-2 power formula and p|binom(p,2) therefore give exp(K)|p.

In H=G/K, which has exponent p, collect 1=[y,x^p]. Every coordinate whose X-degree r<p is p-divisible and dies. Class ≤p+1 leaves only multidegree (p,1). That free-Lie component has rank (1/(p+1))binom(p+1;p,1)=1, so there is one Hall basic commutator b, represented up to sign by [y,_p x]. Modulo terms containing at least two y's, [y,x^m]=∏_k[y,_k x]^{binom(m,k)}, so b has coordinate ±binom(p,p)=±1 modulo p. Thus b=1 in H, hence b∈K and b^p=1. Swapping x,y treats multidegree (1,p).

Returning to [x^p,y^p], all non-extreme coordinates are p^2-divisible; the only possibly merely p-divisible coordinates are (p,1) and (1,p), whose commutators now have order p. Every factor vanishes, proving [x^p,y^p]=1.

Caveat: this is only a class-bounded lemma, not a solution of the active scope. The proof stops at p+1: in class p+2, γ_{p+1} need not be central and additional top coordinates such as multidegree (p,2) prevent [y,x^p] from isolating the (p,1) term. It makes no class-p+2 claim.
