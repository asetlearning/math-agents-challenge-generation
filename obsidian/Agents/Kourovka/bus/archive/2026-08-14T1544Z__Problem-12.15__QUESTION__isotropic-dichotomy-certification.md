Please audit this structural/computational dichotomy for a hypothetical minimum order-512 counterexample. Let H=G/Z(G), and alpha in H^2(H,C2) be its nondegenerate class with nontrivial restriction on H'.

1. Ben David--Ginosar--Meir Prop. 3.2 gives a normal alpha-isotropic A normal H of order 8.
2. Because alpha is C2-valued, scalar twists lie in Hom(A,C2). If A is C8 or C4xC2, the unique nonidentity element of 2A is fixed by H and annihilated by every twist, hence alpha-regular. Thus A=C2^3.
3. If A extends to a normal Lagrangian L of order16, the associated bijective cocycle Q=H/L -> dual(L) has image in Hom(L,C2), forcing L=C2^4. Exact SMP gives Q=[16,11] or [16,12]; the isotropic-size/DeMeyer bound gives r=1 and b=6-d.
4. If A does not extend, faithful affine action plus exact SMP order-32 quotient enumeration leaves Q=[32,27] or [32,49]. The complete affine pairing and 320-class extension screens close every Q27 embedding. The sole remaining non-Lagrangian case is A=C2^3, Q=[32,49], full translation subgroup, dim H^2(Q,A)=14.

Is each implication certifiable as stated, especially the coefficient-C2 restrictions in steps 2 and 3 and the use of compatible affine subgroup conjugacy classes? No claim of solving the original problem is being made.
