# Verification — Kourovka 20.49 — subdirect lift profiles

## The claim

Let `G` be a finite least-order counterexample and let `M,N` be distinct minimal
normal subgroups.  With

```text
A=G/M,  B=G/N,  C=G/MN,
```

the submitted claim identifies `G` with `A x_C B`, proves
`exp(G)=lcm(exp(A),exp(B))`, and characterizes the existence of an
at-most-two-generated equal-exponent subgroup by compatible exponent profiles
over one common precomposition orbit of maps `F_2 -> C`.

The hand argument passes, subject to one wording repair: it is
`W_A intersect W_B != empty` that is stronger than necessary for success.
Consequently `W_A intersect W_B = empty` is weaker than the exact counterexample
obstruction.  Equations (4)--(7) in the submitted note already have the correct
logical content.

There is no `claim-checks/<claim-id>.json`.  That is consistent with the submission
being routed as a `PARTIAL_RESULT`, but it independently prevents treating this
note as a closure claim.

## Subclaims and what each method proves

### 1. Distinct minimal normal subgroups

Because `M cap N` is normal in `G` and is contained in each of the distinct
minimal normal subgroups, `M cap N=1`.  Normality also gives
`[M,N] <= M cap N`, hence `[M,N]=1`.

The reviewed least-counterexample argument makes `G` directly indecomposable.  If
`MN=G`, the two preceding equalities would give the internal direct product
`G=M x N`, a contradiction.  Hence `MN<G`; since `MN` is nontrivial, `C` is a
nontrivial proper quotient.  Direct indecomposability is needed only for this last
nontriviality statement, not for the fibre-product calculation itself.

### 2. The canonical fibre product

Let `alpha:A->C` and `beta:B->C` be the natural quotient maps.  The map

```text
gamma:G -> A x_C B,    g |-> (gM,gN)
```

has kernel `M cap N=1`, so it is injective.  Moreover

```text
|A x_C B| = |A||B|/|C|
            = (|G|/|M|)(|G|/|N|) / (|G|/|MN|)
            = |G|,
```

where `|MN|=|M||N|` follows from the trivial intersection.  Therefore `gamma` is
onto the full fibre product, not merely onto an unspecified subdirect subgroup.

Put `e_A=exp(A)` and `e_B=exp(B)`.  Both are quotient exponents and divide
`e=exp(G)`.  Conversely the embedding `G <= A x B` makes `e` divide
`exp(A x B)=lcm(e_A,e_B)`.  Hence

```text
exp(G)=lcm(e_A,e_B).
```

No element-order maximum is substituted for group exponent here.

### 3. Compatible free-group maps and image exponents

Let `F=F(s,t)`.  For homomorphisms `phi:F->A` and `psi:F->B`, the map

```text
rho(w)=(phi(w),psi(w))
```

lands in `A x_C B` exactly when `alpha phi=beta psi`.  This is an exact
correspondence, by the defining universal property of the fibre product.

Write `H=rho(F)`, `H_A=phi(F)`, and `H_B=psi(F)`.  Projection makes `H_A,H_B`
quotients of `H`, so both projected exponents divide `exp(H)`.  On the other
hand `H<=H_A x H_B`, so `exp(H)` divides their lcm.  Therefore

```text
exp(H)=lcm(exp(H_A),exp(H_B)).
```

This proof does not require `phi` to be onto `A`, `psi` to be onto `B`, or `rho`
to be onto `G`.

### 4. The precise orbit action

For every target group `X`, use precomposition on `Hom(F,X)`: explicitly, for
`theta in Aut(F)`, write

```text
u^theta := u o theta.
```

If `v=u^theta`, then replacing a lift `phi` of `u` by
`phi^theta=phi o theta` makes its base map equal to `v` because

```text
alpha(phi^theta)=(alpha phi)^theta=u^theta=v.
```

Since `theta(F)=F`, `phi^theta(F)=phi(F)`.  Thus the projected subgroup and its
exponent are unchanged.  This is the same precomposition action upstairs and
downstairs; no postcomposition action and no independent change of the target
group is being used.

The phrase “Nielsen orbit” here is legitimate for all ordered pairs, including
non-generating pairs in `C`: it means precisely the `Aut(F)` precomposition orbit.

### 5. Exact attainable-profile iff

For clarity, first fix a base map `u:F->C` and define

```text
P_A(u)={exp(phi(F)) : alpha phi=u},
P_B(u)={exp(psi(F)) : beta psi=u}.
```

These sets are nonempty: choose arbitrary lifts in `A` or `B` of `u(s),u(t)` and
use freeness of `F`.  Precomposition gives
`P_A(u^theta)=P_A(u)` and similarly for `B`.  Thus for an orbit `O`, the submitted
sets `E_A(O),E_B(O)` are well defined.

There is no false mixing when the two exponents are attained over different maps
in the same orbit.  If `r` is attained over `u` and `s` over
`v=u^theta`, precompose the `A`-lift by `theta`; it now lies over `v` and retains
exponent `r`, so it is compatible with the chosen `B`-lift.

Consequently

```text
G has an at-most-two-generated H with exp(H)=e

iff

there exist an orbit O, r in E_A(O), and s in E_B(O)
with lcm(r,s)=e.                                      (*)
```

For the forward implication, choose a map `rho:F->G` onto the eligible subgroup;
its two projections have exactly the same base map.  For the reverse implication,
align the two base maps by the preceding precomposition and apply the compatible
map and exponent identities.  This proves both directions and includes cyclic and
trivial images by allowing a redundant or identity generator.

### 6. What minimality does and does not supply

Since `A` and `B` are smaller finite groups, least-order minimality supplies
at-most-two-generated subgroups of exponents `e_A` and `e_B`, respectively.  It
therefore proves

```text
W_A={O:e_A in E_A(O)} != empty,
W_B={O:e_B in E_B(O)} != empty.
```

It does not make either factor map surjective, and it does not identify the two
orbits.  A common orbit in `W_A intersect W_B` would be sufficient, by the ambient
exponent identity, but is not necessary: projected subgroups can carry
complementary full prime-power parts without either having the whole factor
exponent.  Precisely:

- `W_A intersect W_B != empty` is stronger than necessary for success;
- a counterexample must have `W_A intersect W_B=empty`, but that condition alone
  is weaker than the exact obstruction; and
- the exact obstruction is that every `O,r,s` in `(*)` has
  `lcm(r,s)<e`.

Because `r,s` divide `e`, this last condition is equivalent to: for every such
triple there is a prime `p|e`, depending on the triple, for which

```text
max(v_p(r),v_p(s)) < v_p(e).
```

Thus both projections miss the same maximal `p`-power for that compatible choice.

### 7. At most two generators versus epimorphic pairs

The exact criterion `(*)` ranges over all maps `F->A` and `F->B`.  A full-exponent
subgroup of a factor need not be the whole factor, so replacing `Hom` by
`Epi(F,A)` or `Epi(F,B)` would make the criterion false as stated.

There is one valid epimorphism restriction in the least-counterexample setting.
After compatibility, let `u:F->C` be the common base map and `D=u(F)`.  For the
natural map `q:G->C`, the subgroup `H=rho(F)` lies in `q^{-1}(D)`.  If `D<C`, this
inverse image is a proper subgroup of `G`; proper-subgroup exponent drop gives

```text
exp(H) divides exp(q^{-1}(D)) < exp(G).
```

Therefore a profile satisfying `(*)` must have `u` epimorphic onto `C`.  This says
nothing about `phi` being onto `A` or `psi` being onto `B`.  If `d(C)>2`, no such
base orbit exists; that leaves this proof route stuck and does not itself
contradict the hypothetical counterexample.

## Verdict

**The exact conditional fibre-product/profile reduction is upheld by the hand
audit, with the one logical wording repair stated above.  Overall status remains
`status/conjectured`; `active_assignment_answered: no`.**

The canonical fibre-product description, both exponent-lcm identities, the
compatible-`F_2` correspondence, the explicit precomposition-orbit alignment, and
the full attainable-profile iff all pass.  The submission also correctly stops at
separate nonempty factor-profile sets and does not infer a common orbit or
monolithicity.

## Why this verdict

Every step of the conditional equivalence has a direct hand proof, and the use of
arbitrary `F_2` maps correctly captures subgroups generated by at most two
elements.  But the source's universal quantifier and target-conclusion rows are
unproved.  There is no common-profile existence theorem, no counterexample, and no
claim-check file for an active-scope claim.  Protocol therefore forbids promotion
of the active assignment.

## What is NOT established

- No orbit `O` satisfying the lcm condition is proved to exist.
- Minimality does not force `W_A intersect W_B` to be nonempty.
- Full exponent of a projected subgroup does not make it the whole factor.
- Only the common base map may be required to be epimorphic, and only after using
  proper-subgroup exponent drop.
- It is not proved that `d(C)<=2`; the case `d(C)>2` is an obstruction to this
  route, not a contradiction.
- Distinct minimal normal subgroups are not excluded, so monolithicity is not
  proved.
- The universal revision-1 target remains open.

## What would upgrade it

The proof direction needs a new theorem showing that the least-counterexample
hypotheses force some epimorphism orbit `O` and attainable exponents
`r in E_A(O)`, `s in E_B(O)` with `lcm(r,s)=exp(G)`, or another argument excluding
two distinct minimal normal subgroups.  Closing the active assignment still
requires a universal gap-free proof or an explicit finite counterexample with a
complete all-pairs certificate.
