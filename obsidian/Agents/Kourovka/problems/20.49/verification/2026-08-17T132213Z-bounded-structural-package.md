### 2. Least-order counterexample package

Assume a counterexample exists and choose `G` of least order.

**Nonsoluble.** This is immediate from the source-supplied affirmative result for
soluble groups.

**Proper-subgroup exponent drop.** If `L<G` and `exp(L)=exp(G)`, then minimality
applied to the smaller finite group `L` gives `H=<x,y><=L` with
`exp(H)=exp(L)=exp(G)`, contradicting that `G` is a counterexample. Since subgroup
exponents divide the ambient exponent, the inequality is a genuine strict drop.

**Proper-quotient exponent drop.** Let `1!=N normal G` with `G/N` a proper quotient.
If `exp(G/N)=exp(G)`, minimality gives a subgroup of `G/N` generated by `xN,yN`
and having exponent `exp(G/N)`. The image of `<x,y>` in `G/N` is that subgroup, so
its exponent divides `exp(<x,y>)`. But `exp(<x,y>)` divides `exp(G)`. Equality on
the two outside terms forces `exp(<x,y>)=exp(G)`, a contradiction.

**Exactly three generators.** A counterexample is not at most two-generated because
`H=G` is allowed. The source-known three-generator theorem gives a three-generated
subgroup `L` of exponent `exp(G)`. Proper-subgroup exponent drop forces `L=G`.
Therefore `d(G)=3`.

**Direct indecomposability.** If `G=A x B` nontrivially, minimality gives
`A0=<a1,a2><=A` with `exp(A0)=exp(A)` and `B0=<b1,b2><=B` with
`exp(B0)=exp(B)`. Then

```text
H = <(a1,b1),(a2,b2)>
```

projects onto both `A0` and `B0`. Hence `exp(A)` and `exp(B)` both divide
`exp(H)`, while `exp(H)` divides
`exp(A x B)=lcm(exp(A),exp(B))`. Equality follows, contradicting the choice of
`G`.

All five reductions pass the hand audit. They are necessary conditions only; they
neither construct nor exclude a counterexample.

### 3. Order-30 obstruction to monolithicity

For

```text
D=<r,s | r^15=s^2=1, srs=r^-1>,
```

rotations have order dividing 15 and reflections have order 2, so `exp(D)=30`.
Every subgroup is cyclic inside `<r>` or dihedral over a rotation subgroup. A proper
dihedral subgroup has rotation order `1`, `3`, or `5`, hence exponent `2`, `6`, or
`10`; a proper cyclic subgroup has exponent dividing 15. Every proper normal
subgroup lies in `<r>` because the normal closure of a reflection is `D`. The three
nontrivial proper normal rotation subgroups have orders 3, 5, and 15, and their
quotients have exponents 10, 6, and 2.

Every proper normal subgroup is odd-order, so two proper normal direct factors
cannot multiply to `D`. Thus `D` is directly indecomposable. The prime-order normal
subgroups `<r^5>` and `<r^3>` are distinct minimal normal subgroups of orders 3 and
5. The independent 30-element checker reproduces all 28 subgroups, all five normal
subgroups, both minimal normal orders, all proper-subgroup exponents, the three
quotient exponents, and the absence of a direct-factor pair.

This exact control refutes only the implication

```text
subgroup exponent-critical + quotient exponent-critical + directly indecomposable
    => monolithic.
```

It does not address whether adding nonsolubility and `d(G)=3` forces monolithicity.
The group `D` itself is soluble and two-generated, hence not an in-scope
counterexample.

### 4. At least three exponent primes and exact three-prime defects

For each prime `p` in `pi(exp(G))`, a maximal `p`-part in the least common multiple
is attained by some element order; taking its prime-to-`p` power gives a pure
`p`-element `t_p` of order equal to the full `p`-part of `exp(G)`.

In a counterexample, every pair has a nonempty defect set. A pair containing `t_p`
cannot be defective at `p`. Thus for distinct primes `p,q`, the pair `(t_p,t_q)`
can be defective only at a third exponent prime. This proves that a counterexample
has at least three distinct exponent primes.

If there are exactly three primes `p,q,r`, choose pure maximal elements `x,y,z`.
The subgroup `<x,y,z>` contains the full prime-power part at all three primes, so
its exponent is `exp(G)`. In a least counterexample it therefore equals `G`; since
`d(G)=3`, the triple is irredundant. The pair `<x,y>` already has the full `p`- and
`q`-parts, and its defect set is nonempty, so its defect is exactly `{r}`. Cyclically,

```text
Delta(x,y)={r},  Delta(y,z)={p},  Delta(z,x)={q}.
```

This passes exactly in the stated prime-pure, exactly-three-prime setting. It is not
a claim about arbitrary generating triples.

### 5. Nielsen non-invariance control

The finite abelian coordinate checker gives order 9,240 and exponent 2,310 for

```text
A=C2^3 x C3 x C5 x C7 x C11.
```

It exhaustively generates `A` from both `T=(x,y,z)` and
`T'=(x+y,y,z)`. The `C2^3` projections are bases, so both triples are irredundant.
Their pair defects are exactly

```text
T:  xy->{11}, xz->{7}, yz->{5};
T': (x+y)y->{11}, (x+y)z->{3}, yz->{5}.
```

Replacing `x` by `x+y` is a Nielsen move, so the label on the moving edge changes
from 7 to 3. This exact control proves non-invariance of the submitted unrestricted
edge labels. It has five exponent primes and the moved element is not prime-pure;
therefore it does not contradict the exact three-prime prime-pure lemma above, and
it does not rule out every possible refined invariant.

### 6. Abelian minimal-normal quotient drop

Let `N` be an abelian minimal normal subgroup of a least counterexample. Since a
finite abelian characteristically simple group is elementary abelian, `N` is an
elementary abelian `p`-group. Let `p^a` be the `p`-part of `exp(G)` and `p^b` the
`p`-part of `exp(G/N)`.

For `q!=p`, a pure maximal `q`-element has trivial intersection with `N`, so its
image has the same order. Every non-`p` exponent part is therefore preserved in the
quotient. Proper-quotient criticality then forces a strict drop at `p`, so `b<a`.

For every `g in G`, raising `g` to `exp(G/N)` lands in `N`, whose exponent is `p`.
Thus `exp(G)` divides `p exp(G/N)`, and hence `a<=b+1`. Combining the inequalities
gives `b=a-1`. A maximal `p`-element `g` has
`1!=g^(p^(a-1)) in N`. The one-step quotient-drop claim passes.

### 7. Split norm and full Jordan block

In a split extension `G=N semidirect Q`, choose a maximal `p`-element `g=nu` of
order `p^a`. Its image `u` cannot have order below `p^(a-1)`, because `N` has
exponent `p`; the quotient bound gives equality. Put `m=p^(a-1)`. Then

```text
(nu)^m = T_u(n),             T_u=1+u+...+u^(m-1).
```

The lift has order `pm=p^a` exactly when `T_u(n)` is nonzero. In characteristic
`p`, for `a>=2`, writing `X=u-1` gives

```text
u^m-1=X^m,  T_u=(u^m-1)/(u-1)=X^(m-1).
```

Thus nonzero norm is equivalent to `X^(m-1)!=0`. Since `X^m=0`, the restriction of
`u` has a Jordan block of exactly length `m`, and `dim_Fp(N)>=m`. For `a=1`, this is
the trivial length-one case `m=1`, `T_u=1`. This necessary split-layer claim passes;
it is not sufficient to produce a complementary partner.

### 8. Explicit exponent-900 no-partner pattern

For `B=C25 semidirect C4` with action `a -> a^7`, `7^2=-1 mod 25` and the action has
order 4. The exact 100-element table has element-order distribution

```text
1:1, 2:25, 4:50, 5:4, 25:20.
```

Hence `exp(B)=100`, but no element, and therefore no cyclic subgroup, realizes both
the 4- and 25-parts.

The deleted permutation module

```text
W={(t1,t2,t3,t4) in F3^4 : t1+t2+t3+t4=0}
```

has dimension three. The permutation form restricts nondegenerately because the
constant vector has norm `4=1` in `F3`. There is no invariant line: every
one-dimensional `F3`-character of `A4` is trivial, while `W` has no nonzero fixed
constant vector. An invariant plane would have an invariant orthogonal-complement
line. Thus `W` is irreducible. The exact checker independently enumerates all 28
subspaces and finds no nonzero proper `A4`-invariant one.

For `u=(123)`, `n=(1,0,0,-1)`, the checker obtains

```text
T_u(n)=(1,1,1,0)!=0,
```

and the successive image sizes of `u-1` are 9, 3, 1. Hence `u` has one length-three
Jordan block and `x=(n;u,1)` has order 9. With the submitted `y` and `z`, the exact
coordinate computation obtains generator orders `(9,4,25)`, generated-group order
32,400, and exponent 900. It also obtains

```text
|<x,y>|=1296, exp(<x,y>)=36,  defect={5};
|<x,z>|=225,  exp(<x,z>)=225, defect={2};
|<y,z>|=100,  exp(<y,z>)=100, defect={3}.
```

There is one repair needed in the submitted prose. Merely saying that the kernel of
`G0 -> B` has 2-exponent two would not, for an arbitrary extension, exclude an
order-four lift of an involution. Here the construction additionally says that `B`
acts trivially on `W` and commutes with `A4`. Therefore

```text
G0 = (W semidirect A4) x B.
```

The first factor has exponent 18: it has no 5-part and only a single factor of 2.
For any `w`, the `B`-projection of `<x,w>` is the cyclic group generated by the
single `B`-coordinate of `w`. A full-exponent partner would consequently require
that coordinate to have order divisible by both 4 and 25. The exhaustive table of
all 100 `B`-coordinates shows that none does. This repaired direct-product argument
covers all 32,400 choices of `w` and establishes

```text
exp(<x,w>)<900 for every w in G0.
```

The pattern is nevertheless soluble: `W`, `A4`, and `B` are soluble and solubility
is closed under extensions and direct products. It is a control showing that the
norm/Jordan condition does not force a partner for this fixed maximal 3-element,
not a counterexample to the source problem.

## Why this verdict

The scope matrix fails the universal quantifier and target-conclusion rows. The
SG255 claim has one claimant computation and one independent text parser, not two
independent group computations. The hand reductions do not imply existence or
nonexistence of a counterexample. The controls deliberately satisfy only selected
local hypotheses and are soluble. Protocol therefore forbids `status/replicated`
for SG255 and forbids `active_assignment_answered: yes` for the package.

## What is NOT established

- The 14 GAP element coordinates, element orders, subgroup sizes, solvability flags,
  and exponent equalities have not been independently reconstructed.
- No assertion is made about groups of order at least 256.
- The source-known soluble and three-generator theorems were used as source inputs;
  this blind, no-web audit did not perform a new literature verification of them.
- A full least counterexample has not been shown monolithic; the order-30 control
  leaves open whether nonsolubility together with `d(G)=3` changes the conclusion.
- The Nielsen control does not invalidate the exact three-prime prime-pure defect
  lemma and does not rule out a different invariant.
- The abelian-chief argument neither proves that a least counterexample has an
  abelian minimal normal subgroup nor handles a nonsplit layer with the norm formula.
- The norm/Jordan condition does not force a mixed complementary partner.
- The exponent-900 group is not a counterexample; it only blocks a fixed-element
  partner inference.
- The universal target in revision 1 remains open.

## What would upgrade it

A bounded, hash-matched Lead lease allowing Validator to reconstruct all 14
`SmallGroup(n,i)` objects and independently verify the persisted pairs would be the
minimum path toward `status/replicated` for the SG255 bounded claim. Upgrading the
active assignment itself would require either a universal gap-free proof or one
explicit finite counterexample with a complete all-pairs certificate satisfying
every canonical constraint row.
