minimal normal subgroups `M,N`.  Put

```text
A=G/M,  B=G/N,  C=G/MN,
alpha:A->C,  beta:B->C,  q:G->C.
```

Starting from the reviewed identification `G=A x_C B`, it claims an exact
prime-defect description for the inverse image of any at-most-two-generated
full-exponent subgroup `D<=C`, a universal support-transversal generation
statement, and a residual dichotomy according as `d(C)=3` or `d(C)<=2`.

The bounded structural package passes after the precision corrections below.

source question and does not answer the active assignment.

## Subclaims and what each method proves

### 1. Least-counterexample facts used by the package

Write `e=exp(G)`.  Every proper subgroup `U<G` has `exp(U)<e`: otherwise
minimality applied to the smaller finite group `U` supplies an at-most-two-
generated subgroup of exponent `exp(U)=e`, contradicting that `G` is a
counterexample.

Distinct minimal normal subgroups have

```text
M cap N=1,  [M,N]=1.
```

The first equality follows because the intersection is normal in `G` and is
proper in each of the two distinct minimal normal subgroups; the second follows
from `[M,N]<=M cap N`.

In particular, the map `G->A x B` is injective.  Since quotient exponents divide
`e` while a subgroup exponent divides the exponent of its ambient direct
product,

```text
exp(G)=lcm(exp(A),exp(B)).                       (0)
```

The common quotient `C` is smaller than `G`.  Minimality therefore supplies an
at-most-two-generated `D<=C` with `exp(D)=exp(C)`.

### 2. Exact preimages and the exponent identity

For such a fixed `D`, define

```text
A_D=alpha^{-1}(D),  B_D=beta^{-1}(D),  P_D=q^{-1}(D).
```

Restriction of the canonical fibre-product identification gives

```text
P_D=A_D x_D B_D.                                (1a)
```

Equivalently, the two projections of `P_D` are onto `A_D` and `B_D`, with

```text
A_D=P_D/M,  B_D=P_D/N.
```

Hence the two projected exponents divide `exp(P_D)`.  Conversely `P_D` embeds
in `A_D x B_D`, so its exponent divides the lcm of their exponents.  Therefore

```text
exp(P_D)=lcm(exp(A_D),exp(B_D)).                 (1b)
```

This proves the submitted equation (1); it does not use a maximum-element-order
substitute for group exponent.

### 3. Proper-base defect and its precise divisibility content

For every prime `p`, put

```text
g_p=v_p(e),  c_p=v_p(exp(C)),  s_p(D)=v_p(exp(P_D)),
S(D)={p:s_p(D)<g_p}.
```

If `D<C`, then `P_D<G`, so the proper-subgroup fact gives
`exp(P_D)<e`.  Since `exp(P_D)` divides `e`, strict inequality is equivalent to
at least one strict prime-adic inequality; thus `S(D)` is nonempty.

For `p in S(D)`, the equality `exp(D)=exp(C)` and the quotient
`P_D->D` give

```text
c_p=v_p(exp(D)) <= s_p(D) < g_p.
```

Also `M,N<=P_D`, so

```text
v_p(exp(M)) <= s_p(D) < g_p,
v_p(exp(N)) <= s_p(D) < g_p.                   (2)
```

Thus no top `p`-height is present in the base or in either minimal normal
subgroup.  By (0), at least one of `A,B` nevertheless has exponent of
`p`-height `g_p`.  In that precise sense the ambient top height is created in
at least one of the two extensions.  This does **not** say that both factor
extensions carry the top height.

The notation `S(D)` is fixed only after `D` is fixed; no argument shows that
different eligible subgroups `D` have the same defect set.

### 4. Exact support equivalence

Interpret “an element has `p`-height `g_p`” as
`v_p(|x|)=g_p`, and define

```text
Sigma_{A,p}={c in C: some a in A has alpha(a)=c and v_p(|a|)=g_p},
Sigma_{B,p}={c in C: some b in B has beta(b)=c and v_p(|b|)=g_p},
Sigma_p=Sigma_{A,p} union Sigma_{B,p}.
```

Because `A` and `B` are finite,

```text
v_p(exp(A_D))=g_p  iff  D cap Sigma_{A,p} is nonempty,
v_p(exp(B_D))=g_p  iff  D cap Sigma_{B,p} is nonempty.
```

Combining these equivalences with (1b) gives, with both directions proved,

```text
p in S(D)  iff  D cap Sigma_p is empty.         (3)
```

For every `p in S(D)`, the set `Sigma_p` is nonempty by (0) and the fact that
the `p`-valuation of a finite-group exponent is attained by an element order.

Each `Sigma_{A,p}` is closed under conjugacy in `C`: lift a conjugating element
of `C` to `A` and conjugate the witnessing lift.  It is also closed under
taking `k`-th powers when `p` does not divide `k`, because
`v_p(|a^k|)=v_p(|a|)`.  The same proof applies to `Sigma_{B,p}` and hence to
their union.

The exact safe wording is “closed under such powers.”  A `k`-th power map need
not be bijective on `C` when `k` is only assumed coprime to `p`, so no reverse
setwise-invariance assertion follows.

### 5. The universal support-transversal quantifier

Fix `D<C`.  For **every** simultaneous choice

```text
c_p in Sigma_p  for each p in S(D),
```

let

```text
L=<D, c_p (p in S(D))>.
```

For `p notin S(D)`, the subgroup `P_D<=P_L` already has top `p`-height `g_p`.
For `p in S(D)`, the chosen `c_p` has a top-height lift in at least one of
`A_L,B_L`; because `P_L` projects onto both preimages, `P_L` also has top
`p`-height.  Thus every prime valuation of `exp(P_L)` equals that of `e`, and

```text
exp(P_L)=e.
```

Proper-subgroup exponent drop forces `P_L=G`, and applying `q` gives

```text
C=<D, c_p (p in S(D))>.                         (4)
```

Nothing in this proof selected a favourable transversal.  It proves the stated
universal quantifier over all choices, including choices in which one element
supports more than one defect prime.  It does not say that the displayed
generating set for `C` has size at most two.

Since `P_D<G`, minimality separately supplies an at-most-two-generated
`K_D<=P_D` with

```text
exp(K_D)=exp(P_D)<e.
```

Its base image is contained in `D<C` and hence is not epimorphic onto `C`.

### 6. Primary carrier refinement and the correct kernels

Let `p in S(D)`.  By (0), suppose first that `A` has exponent of `p`-height
`g_p`.  Choose an element whose order has that height and take its primary
part; this gives a `p`-element `x in A` of exact order `p^{g_p}`.  Its image is
a `p`-element of order `p^t`.  Equation (2) and the base bound give

```text
0 < t < g_p.
```

The upper inequality follows from `c_p<g_p`.  If `t=0`, then `x` would lie in
the kernel and put top height in `N`, contradicting (2).

The literal kernel is

```text
ker(alpha)=MN/M,
```

which is canonically isomorphic to `N` through `n |-> nM` because
`M cap N=1`.  Therefore the precise statement is

```text
x^{p^t} in MN/M = ker(alpha),
|x^{p^t}|=p^{g_p-t}.                            (5A)
```

It is harmless to write “in `N`” only after explicitly adopting this canonical
identification.  In the other factor the roles cross:

```text
ker(beta)=MN/N is canonically isomorphic to M,  (5B)
```

not to `N`.

Thus a full-height `p`-element may always be chosen **upstairs**, and its base
carrier is a nontrivial `p`-element of strictly lower height.  This does not say
that an arbitrary preassigned mixed-order `c in Sigma_p` remains fixed when its
witness is replaced by its primary part: the base element can change to a
power `c^m` with `p` not dividing `m`.  Such a power is still in `Sigma_p` by
the closure just proved, so one may choose a primary carrier for each defect
prime and then apply the universal statement (4).

### 7. The generator-rank dichotomy

The known three-generator theorem supplies a subgroup `J<=G`, generated by at
most three elements, with `exp(J)=e`.  Proper-subgroup exponent drop forces
`J=G`.  Since a counterexample cannot itself be generated by at most two
elements,

```text
d(G)=3,  hence d(C)<=3.                          (6)
```

There are exactly two rank branches.

If `d(C)=3`, every at-most-two-generated full-exponent `D<=C` is proper, so the
fixed-`D` conclusions (2)--(5) apply.  No epimorphism `F_2->C` exists.  This is
an obstruction to the reviewed lift-profile route; it is not a contradiction,
does not show that such a `C` cannot occur, and does not turn (4) into a
two-generator presentation of `C`.

If `d(C)<=2`, take `D=C`.  Then `P_C=G`, so there is no proper-preimage defect.
For an epimorphic precomposition orbit `O` and attainable projected exponents

```text
r in E_A(O),  s in E_B(O),
```

define

```text
L_A(r)={p:v_p(r)<g_p},
L_B(s)={p:v_p(s)<g_p}.
```

If `c_p=g_p`, every factor image over an epimorphic base map maps onto `C`, so
its exponent is divisible by `exp(C)` and cannot lose `p`.  Hence both loss
sets lie in `{p:c_p<g_p}`.

Since `r,s` divide `e`, primewise lcm valuation gives the exact equivalence

```text
lcm(r,s)=e  iff  L_A(r) cap L_B(s) is empty.     (7)
```

Combining (7) with the reviewed orbit-alignment criterion and the fact that a
successful base map in a least counterexample would have to be epimorphic, the
residual counterexample condition is exactly

```text
for every epimorphic orbit O,
for every r in E_A(O),
for every s in E_B(O),
L_A(r) cap L_B(s) is nonempty.                  (8)
```

The three universal quantifiers in (8) are essential.  Minimality only gives
full-factor witnesses above some, possibly non-epimorphic and different, base
maps.  It gives no full-factor value in an epimorphic orbit and no closure of
attainable valuation profiles under coordinatewise maximum.

## Verdict

**PASS_WITH_CORRECTION for the bounded conditional structural partial.**

The exponent identity, nonempty proper-base defect, base/kernel divisibility,
support equivalence, universal support-transversal generation statement, primary
carrier existence, and the rank/loss-profile dichotomy all pass.  The required
repairs are:

1. write the kernels literally as `MN/M` in `A` and `MN/N` in `B`, identifying
   them with `N` and `M`, respectively, only after saying so;
2. distinguish a full-height `p`-element upstairs from its lower-height base
   carrier, and do not claim that primary purification fixes an arbitrary
   mixed-order support element;
3. read power stability as forward closure under `p`-coprime powers, not as
   bijective setwise invariance; and
4. treat `S(D)` as depending on the fixed choice of `D`.

These corrections do not invalidate equations (1)--(5) or the exact bounded
dichotomy.  Overall status remains `status/conjectured`, and
`active_assignment_answered: no`.

## Why this verdict

Every quantified implication in the fixed-`D` package has a direct proof from
surjective projections, exponent divisibility, and proper-subgroup exponent
drop.  In particular, the support-transversal statement really is universal,
not existential.  The carrier argument is valid after using the exact crosswise
kernels.  The loss-profile statement is an exact reformulation of the reviewed
compatible-profile criterion in the branch `d(C)<=2`.

The source's universal quantifier is not addressed: one branch records a base-
rank obstruction and the other records a cross-intersecting profile obstruction,
but neither obstruction is excluded.
