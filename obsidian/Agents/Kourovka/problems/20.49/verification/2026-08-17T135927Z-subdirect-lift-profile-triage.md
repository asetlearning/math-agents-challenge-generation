# Verification triage — Kourovka 20.49

## Claim restated

Conditionally on a finite least-order counterexample `G` having distinct minimal
normal subgroups `M,N`, put `A=G/M`, `B=G/N`, and `C=G/MN`.  The claim is that
`G` is canonically `A x_C B`, that its exponent is the lcm of the factor
exponents, and that `G` has an at-most-two-generated subgroup of exponent
`exp(G)` exactly when one `Aut(F_2)`-orbit of base maps `F_2 -> C` supports
attainable projected exponents whose lcm is `exp(G)`.  This is a conditional
partial reduction, not a proof of the active universal assertion.

## Subclaims to check

1. Distinct minimal normal subgroups have trivial intersection and centralize
   one another.
2. The map `g |-> (gM,gN)` identifies `G` with the full fibre product
   `G/M x_(G/MN) G/N`.
3. `exp(G)=lcm(exp(G/M),exp(G/N))`.
4. Compatible maps from `F_2` to the two quotients correspond exactly to maps
   from `F_2` to the fibre product.
5. The exponent of such an image is the lcm of the two projected image
   exponents.
6. The precise precomposition action of `Aut(F_2)` permits two base maps in one
   orbit to be made equal and preserves each generated image subgroup.
7. The attainable-profile criterion is an iff and includes cyclic and trivial
   images, not just epimorphic ordered pairs.
8. Minimality gives separate nonempty full-factor profile sets but does not give
   their intersection; moreover full factor exponents are sufficient, not
   necessary, because complementary prime powers may occur.
9. Under proper-subgroup exponent drop, a base image properly contained in `C`
   cannot yield exponent `exp(G)`; this restriction concerns the base map only.
10. None of these claims proves monolithicity or the universal target.
