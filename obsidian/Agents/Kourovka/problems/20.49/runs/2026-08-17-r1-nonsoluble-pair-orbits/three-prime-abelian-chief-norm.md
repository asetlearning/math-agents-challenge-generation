# Three-prime split abelian-chief norm gate

## Exact norm constraint

Let `G=N semidirect Q`, where `N` is a minimal abelian normal `p`-subgroup of a
least counterexample. Then `N` is an irreducible elementary abelian `F_p Q`-module.
Write `p^a` for the `p`-part of `exp(G)`. The quotient reduction gives

```text
the p-part of exp(Q) = p^(a-1).
```

If `u in Q` is a `p`-element of order `m=p^(a-1)` and `n in N`, then, in additive
notation on `N`,

```text
(n u)^m = T_u(n),
T_u = 1 + u + ... + u^(m-1).
```

Thus a lift `nu` has order `p^a` exactly when `T_u(n) != 0`. For `a>=2`, in
characteristic `p`,

```text
T_u = (u-1)^(m-1).
```

Consequently a split abelian layer that raises the maximal `p`-power must admit a
`p`-element acting on `N` with a Jordan block of full length `m`; in particular
`dim_Fp(N) >= p^(a-1)`. This is stronger than merely requiring a fixed vector.

For the prime-pure generating triple `(x,y,z)` with `x=nu` of order `p^a`, a
single partner `w` would settle the exponent condition if the subgroup
`<u, wN>` in `Q` realizes the maximal `q`- and `r`-parts. The norm condition alone
does not force such a partner.

## Explicit compatible failure pattern

The following entirely explicit split extension satisfies the three-prime,
irreducible-layer, nonzero-norm, and distinct-pair-defect local constraints, but its
maximal `p`-element has no single partner realizing both complementary prime powers.

Take `p=3`, and let

```text
B = <a,c | a^25=c^4=1, c a c^(-1)=a^7> = C25 semidirect C4.
```

The unit `7 mod 25` has order 4 (`7^2=-1 mod 25`), and every element of `B`
has order dividing 25 or 4. In particular no cyclic subgroup of `B` has exponent
divisible by both 25 and 4.

Let

```text
W = {(t1,t2,t3,t4) in F3^4 : t1+t2+t3+t4=0}.
```

Let `A4` act on `W` by permuting coordinates, let `B` act trivially, put
`Q=A4 x B`, and form

```text
G0 = W semidirect Q.
```

The module `W` is irreducible for `A4`: it has no invariant line (the only possible
one-dimensional character over `F3` is trivial, while the constant vector is not in
`W`), and the nondegenerate restricted permutation form rules out an invariant
2-space by orthogonal complementation. Hence `W` is a minimal normal subgroup of
`G0`.

Set

```text
u=(1 2 3),             v=(1 2)(3 4) in A4,
n=(1,0,0,-1) in W.
```

The element `u` has a single length-3 Jordan block on `W`, and explicitly

```text
T_u(n)=n+n^u+n^(u^2)=(1,1,1,0) != 0.
```

Therefore

```text
x=(n;u,1) has order 9,
y=(0;v,c) has order 4,
z=(0;1,a) has order 25.
```

The triple generates `G0`: `u,v` generate `A4`, `c,a` generate `B`, and the
nonzero element `x^3=T_u(n)` has `Q`-orbit spanning the irreducible module `W`.
Moreover

```text
exp(G0)=lcm(9,4,25)=900,
<x,y> misses the maximal 5-part 25,
<x,z> misses the maximal 2-part 4,
<y,z> misses the maximal 3-part 9.
```

Thus this is exactly the locally forced distinct-defect pattern.

Nevertheless, for every `w in G0`, the projection of `<x,w>` to `B` is the cyclic
group generated by the single `B`-coordinate of `w`, because `x` has trivial
`B`-coordinate. The kernel of `G0 -> B` has 2-exponent only 2 and has no 5-elements.
Hence realizing both the maximal 2-part 4 and maximal 5-part 25 would require that
one cyclic subgroup of `B` realize both. No such cyclic subgroup exists. Therefore

```text
exp(<x,w>) < 900 for every w in G0.
```

## Limitation and outcome

`G0` is soluble, so it is not a counterexample to Problem 20.49; by the source-known
soluble theorem it has some other full-exponent pair. Its role is an explicit
compatible module/action failure certificate: even an irreducible split abelian
chief layer with the exact nonzero norm, exactly three exponent primes, and the
prime-pure distinct-defect generating triple does not force the maximal `p`-element
to have a single complementary partner.

The route therefore needs an additional nonsolubility/almost-simple constraint on
`Q`; the norm gate alone cannot complete the argument.
