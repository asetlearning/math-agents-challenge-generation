# Post-prime-cover route assessment

## Scope fidelity

The active revision-1 target remains the universal assertion that every finite
group `G` has a subgroup generated by **at most two elements** whose group
exponent is **exactly** `exp(G)`.  The known soluble and three-generated clauses,
and equality of only a maximum element order, remain excluded.  The reviewed
prime-cover package is a conditional partial for a hypothetical least
counterexample with two distinct minimal normal subgroups; it is not the
universal theorem.

Existing inputs below are internally cited to the two Validator notes in the
request.  Every new deduction in this assessment is **general mathematical
knowledge / hand reasoning, unverified**, and would need Validator review before
reuse.

## Common obstruction

The exact fibre criterion has reached a compatibility obstruction, not an
exponent-computation obstruction.  Minimality supplies full-exponent subgroups
in smaller groups, but it does not put their generating pairs over one common
epimorphic map `F_2 -> C`.  Neither support conjugacy/power closure nor
cross-intersection of arbitrary attainable loss families supplies that missing
compatibility.

## Route comparison

### 1. `d(C)=3`: maximal overgroups of a full-exponent `D`

Fix the reviewed `D<C`, `S(D)`, and primary support subsets of `Sigma_p`.  A
precise next reformulation is

```text
for every maximal K of C with D<=K,
there is p in S(D) with K cap Sigma_p empty.       (M)
```

Indeed, if one such `K` met every support, a simultaneous transversal inside
`K` would contradict the reviewed universal generation statement; conversely,
(M) keeps every transversal out of every maximal overgroup of `D`.  Because the
supports are conjugacy-closed and have `p`-element primary carriers, the missing
support gives a `p`-derangement in the coset action on `C/K`, hence at most the
index constraint `p | [C:K]`.

This is an exact observable but not a target-facing bounded continuation.  It
does not lower `d(C)`: `D` may require two generators and one element outside a
maximal overgroup gives exactly three.  Advancing from (M) requires an unbounded
primitive-group/derangement classification or a new theorem forcing some
maximal overgroup to meet all supports.  No such starting lemma is in the
reviewed package.

### 2. `d(C)<=2`: primewise profiles in one epimorphic orbit

Put

```text
Delta_A={p:v_p(exp(A))<g_p},
Delta_B={p:v_p(exp(B))<g_p},
U={p:v_p(exp(C))<g_p}.
```

The reviewed exponent identity and strict quotient drop suggest the checkable
facts `Delta_A,Delta_B` nonempty, disjoint, and contained in `U`.  Even in the
smallest residual case

```text
U={p,q}, Delta_A={p}, Delta_B={q},
```

one epimorphic orbit `O` succeeds exactly when some `A`-lift over `O` attains the
top `q`-height and some `B`-lift over the same orbit attains the top `p`-height.
The counterexample condition merely orients every orbit:

```text
all A-lifts over O lose q, or all B-lifts over O lose p.
```

Without a theorem connecting these orientations across Nielsen orbits, this is
another exact encoding of the existing cross-intersection obstruction.  The
earlier split abelian-chief control already warns that one-sided failure of a
top-height lift can occur locally, so a norm or coordinatewise-max argument
would repeat a killed implication.  There is no bounded hand observable in the
current data whose positive or negative value forces a common orbit.

### 3. Frattini/chief-factor representation change

A genuinely different datum would attach to every minimal normal subgroup `L`
the quotient-loss set

```text
delta(L)={p:v_p(exp(G/L))<g_p}.
```

The first hand pass would only recover: `delta(L)` is nonempty; distinct minimal
normal subgroups have disjoint loss sets via the embedding into the product of
their quotients; and an abelian minimal normal `r`-group has singleton loss
`{r}` with the already-reviewed one-level drop.  Recording whether such a chief
factor is Frattini or complemented does not yet connect a quotient witness to a
two-generator full-exponent lift.  The natural bridge is a Gaschütz/crown-style
classification of a three-generator-critical quotient with explicit exponent
data.  No sourced theorem or self-contained bounded lemma of that strength is
available here, and the earlier abelian-chief/norm pass has already exhausted
the naive version.

## Recommendation

`PARK_RECOMMENDED` for revision 1.  The unused 106 active minutes are not worth
spending on another maximal-support or loss-profile reformulation.  Routes 1 and
2 have exact observables but no bounded implication toward the at-most-two-
generated equal-exponent target; route 3 is the right representation change in
principle but currently lacks its starting bridge theorem.

### Precise restart gate

Restart only on a **chief/crown representation route** after Lead supplies, or
the blind-run boundary is lifted to locate, a source-checked theorem with the
following usable output:

```text
From a finite nonsoluble G with d(G)=3 and strict exponent drop in every proper
subgroup and nontrivial proper quotient, construct a monolithic primitive or
crown-based quotient Q that still records each top-prime loss, with either an
explicit finite bound on the crown multiplicity or explicit two-generator lift
equations over its socle.
```

The theorem statement and its hypotheses must first pass Validator review.
On restart, the exact observable is the socle/chief-factor loss table together
with the lift equations.  Success is either monolithicity of the least
counterexample or a finite, explicitly parameterized residual family.  Kill the
route immediately if the available theorem controls only `d(Q)` but does not
retain exponent-loss or lift data; that would not face the active target.
