# Verification — Kourovka 12.15

## Verdict

## Line-by-line check

1. (R_x=N_x^2[N_x,G]) is (G)-normal. The quotient (N_x/R_x) is central elementary abelian and is generated by the single image (xR_x), since all conjugates of (x) have that image.
2. The quotient is nontrivial: a maximal proper (G)-invariant (L<N_x) gives a minimal normal subgroup (N_x/L) of the finite 2-group (G/L), hence a central subgroup of order 2; therefore (N_x^2[N_x,G]\le L). This also proves (x\notin R_x) and (|N_x:R_x|=2).
3. If (y\in N_x\setminus R_x), then (\langle y\rangle^G R_x=N_x). A maximal invariant subgroup containing a hypothetical proper (\langle y\rangle^G) would also contain (R_x), a contradiction. Thus (y) and (x) have the same normal closure and are conjugate. Conversely every conjugate of (x) lies in (xR_x). Hence (x^G=N_x\setminus R_x=xR_x).
4. With ([a,b]=a^{-1}a^b), left multiplication by (x^{-1}) gives (D_G(x)=R_x). The consequences (x^2\in R_x), (|x^G|=|N_x|/2), elementary-abelian (G/G'), and elementary-abelian (Z(G)) follow.
5. In the reverse intrinsic characterization, (a^H=aD_H(a)) is conjugacy-invariant. If (D_H(a)) is a subgroup, the coset calculation in the log correctly forces (D_H(a)\lhd H). The order comparison rules out (a\in D_H(a)) for (a\ne1), and (a^2\in D_H(a)) then yields (N_a\setminus D_H(a)=a^H). This proves the stated equivalence.
6. For (K\lhd H), the exact value-set identity is
   
   \[
   D_{H/K}(aK)=\{[a,h]K:h\in H\}=D_H(a)K/K.
   \]
   
   Because (D_H(a)) is a subgroup containing (a^2), its image is a subgroup containing ((aK)^2). This avoids the invalid shortcut of lifting equality of normal closures, so quotient inheritance is sound.
7. For a least counterexample, every nontrivial normal quotient is metabelian, hence (G''\le K) for every nontrivial (K\lhd G). Therefore (G'') is the unique minimal normal subgroup, is central of order 2, and the elementary-abelian-center conclusion forces (Z(G)=G''\cong C_2). For noncentral (a), the nontrivial normal subgroup (D_G(a)) contains this unique minimal normal subgroup, giving (az\in a^G). Each deduction is valid.

For precision, the standalone statement should define (N_x^2) as the subgroup generated by squares of elements of (N_x).

## What is NOT established

The proof does not exclude a central extension with (Z(G)=G''=C_2), metabelian (G/Z(G)), and (az\in a^G) for every noncentral (a). Thus it does not prove (G''=1) and does not answer the active assignment.
