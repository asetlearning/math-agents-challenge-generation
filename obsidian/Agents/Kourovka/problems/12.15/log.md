## Statement in my own words

Let `G` be any finite group of order a power of 2. Assume that for every ordered pair `x,y in G`, equality of the normal subgroups generated by `x` and `y` implies that `x` and `y` lie in the same `G`-conjugacy class. Equivalently, `G` has the Strong Magnus Property. Determine whether this forces `[G,G]` to be abelian, equivalently `G''=1`.

The hypothesis concerns all elements, including `1`, and is one-way: equal normal closures must imply conjugacy. The conclusion is metabelianity of `G`, not abelianity of `G`.

## 2026-08-14T13:29Z — minimal-counterexample reduction

Let `G` be a counterexample of minimum order and set `Z0=G''`. The following reduction appears rigorous, conditional only on the published quotient-closure lemma for finite SMP groups (Garonzi–Marion 2023, Lemma 2.2):

1. `G/N` is SMP for every normal `N`. If `N` is nontrivial, minimality makes `G/N` metabelian, hence `(G/N)''=G''N/N=1`, so `G'' <= N`.
2. Therefore `Z0=G''` is the unique minimal normal subgroup of `G`. In a finite 2-group every nontrivial normal subgroup meets the center nontrivially. Thus `Z0 <= Z(G)`. Every subgroup of central `Z0` is normal, so minimality forces `|Z0|=2`; write `Z0=<z>`.
3. For every `x` other than `1,z`, both normal closures `<x>^G` and `<xz>^G` are nontrivial and hence contain `z`. Since `xz` belongs to `<x>^G` and `x=(xz)z` belongs to `<xz>^G`, the closures are equal. SMP forces `x` conjugate to `xz`.
4. If `c` were central outside `<z>`, then `c` and `cz` would be conjugate central elements and hence equal, impossible. Thus `Z(G)=G''=<z>`.
5. Consequently `(G,Z(G))` is a central Camina pair: for every `x` outside the center, the whole coset `xZ(G)={x,xz}` lies in `x^G`. Equivalently `z in [x,G]` for every noncentral `x`.
6. Character consequence: choose an irreducible character `chi` with `z` outside its kernel. Any nontrivial character kernel would contain the unique minimal normal subgroup `<z>`, so `chi` is faithful. By Schur's lemma `z` acts as `-I`. Since `x` and `xz` are conjugate off the center, `chi(x)=chi(xz)=-chi(x)`, hence `chi` vanishes on `G-Z(G)`. Character orthogonality gives `chi(1)^2=|G:Z(G)|=|G|/2`.

Thus any minimal counterexample must be a 2-group of central type with cyclic center of order 2, `(G,Z(G))` a Camina pair, and `G''=Z(G)`. This is substantially narrower than an arbitrary finite 2-group, but it is not yet a contradiction: the literature on central Camina pairs explicitly allows `Z(G)<G'`, and groups of central type with center of prime order can have complicated central quotients. The missing step is to rule out `G''=Z(G)` under these conditions or show directly that SMP fails in every such group.

Literature searches for `"Camina pair" "G''" "Z(G)"`, `"central Camina pair" derived length`, and variants found Mark L. Lewis, *p-Group Camina pairs*, arXiv:1201.3875, which studies exactly `(G,Z(G))` Camina pairs but whose visible theorem statements give size bounds, not the needed metabelian conclusion. No source located so far closes the missing step.

Two further consequences of SMP, useful as cheap filters, are:

- `G` is a rational group: if `k` is coprime to the order of `x`, then `<x>=<x^k>`, so their normal closures agree and SMP makes `x` conjugate to `x^k`. Thus every generator of every cyclic subgroup is conjugate.
- If `|x|>=4`, then `<x^2>^G` is a proper subgroup of `<x>^G`. Inclusion is automatic; equality would make `x^2` conjugate to `x` by SMP, impossible because their orders differ. Hence the normal closures of successive squares form a strict chain until the identity.

Thus a complete catalogue of metabelian SMP groups of order 256 is a meaningful structural precursor, unlike the withdrawn direct counterexample scan at order 256. ## 2026-08-14T13:37:56Z — derived-exponent lemma for metabelian SMP 2-groups

I obtained the following structural filter.

**Lemma.** If `H` is a finite metabelian SMP 2-group, then `exp(H') <= 8`.

Proof. Put `D=H'` and `A=H/D`. Since SMP passes to quotients, the abelian group `A` is SMP. In an abelian SMP group, `a` and `a^{-1}` generate the same normal closure and hence must be equal; therefore `A` is elementary abelian of exponent at most 2.

Write the abelian group `D` additively. Conjugation makes it a module over the commutative integral group ring `R=Z[A]`: the action factors through `A` because inner conjugation by elements of the abelian subgroup `D` is trivial on `D`. Fix `0 != x in D`, let `M=Rx=<x>^H`, and put `S=R/Ann_R(x)`. The map `S -> M`, `s |-> sx`, is an isomorphism of additive groups. An element `y=sx` generates the same `R`-module as `x` exactly when the image of `s` is a unit of the finite commutative ring `S`.

SMP now says every such `y` is conjugate to `x`. Conjugacy on `D` is precisely the action of `A`, so every unit of `S` is the image of an element of `A`; conversely every image of an element of `A` is a unit. Thus `S^times` is exactly the image of `A`, hence every unit of `S` has square 1. The additive group of `S` is the 2-group `M`, so the scalar `3` is a unit. Consequently `3^2=1` in `S`, hence `8=0` in `S` and `8x=0`. Since `x` was arbitrary, `exp(D)<=8`.

This is also consistent with Chebolu--Corry--Grimm--Hatfield, *Rings with an elementary abelian p-group of units*, arXiv:2112.03853, Theorem 1.1(2), whose finite commutative `Delta_2` classification includes characteristic 8 rings; therefore the elementary argument does **not** justify improving 8 to 4.

Applied to a minimum counterexample `G`, the quotient `H=G/Z(G)` is metabelian SMP and `H'=G'/Z(G)`, so `exp(G'/Z(G))<=8` and `exp(G')<=16`. For the first possible order `|G|=512`, this gives an inexpensive necessary filter on the order-256 quotient catalogue. It is a necessary condition only, not a construction of a central extension and not a proof that any surviving quotient can occur.

## 2026-08-14T13:51:45Z — DeMeyer central-type shape constraint

The projective degree-16 condition on the order-256 quotient has a useful group-theoretic translation. Apply F. R. DeMeyer, *Metabelian groups with an irreducible projective representation of large degree*, Pacific J. Math. 76 (1978), Theorem 1, to the central-type group `G` with the normal subgroup `N=G'` and `Z=Z(G)`. Its hypotheses hold because `G/N` is abelian and `N/Z` is abelian (`N'=G''=Z`). The theorem yields:

1. `N` is itself of central type and `N/Z(N)` is of symmetric type;
2. there is `N <= L normal G` such that `L/N` is of symmetric type and `[G:L]=[Z(N):Z]`.

Translate this to `H=G/Z`, `D=H'=N/Z`, and `B=Z(N)/Z <= D`. The subgroup `B` is `H`-invariant. Since `N'=Z` has order 2, every square in `N` is central in `N`, so

`D/B = N/Z(N) = C2^(2r)`

for some `r>=1`; the rank is even because this quotient is of symmetric type and nontrivial because `N` is nonabelian. If `|B|=2^b` and `|H:D|=2^d`, then DeMeyer's subgroup `L` gives `[G:L]=2^b` while `L/N` is an elementary abelian symmetric-type subgroup of `G/N=C2^d`. Hence `b<=d` and `d-b` is even.

At order 512, `|H|=2^8`, so `|D|=2^(8-d)` and `b=8-d-2r`. A cheap necessary quotient-only test is therefore the existence of an integer `r` such that

- `1 <= r <= floor(rank_2(D)/2)`;
- `b=8-d-2r` satisfies `0<=b<=d`.

This rank test deliberately forgets the additional requirement that the corresponding kernel `B` be `H`-invariant, so a pass is only necessary evidence. On a short exact-SMP list, the stronger check is to enumerate `H`-normal subgroups `B<=D` and require `D/B` to be elementary abelian of positive even rank together with the index balance above.

## 2026-08-14T13:55:34Z — cyclic-module size bound excludes 2-generator parents

The ring proof of the derived-exponent lemma gives a size bound as well. With `A=H/H'=C2^d`, `M=<x>^H`, and `S=Z[A]/Ann(x)`, the ring `S` is local with residue field `F2`: it is a quotient of the local group ring `(Z/2^e Z)[A]`, where `2^e=exp(M)`. Hence every nonunit lies in its maximal ideal and `|S^times|=|S|/2`. Since the earlier argument identifies `S^times` with the image of `A`,

`|<x>^H|=|S|=2|S^times| <= 2|A|=2^(d+1)`.

If a metabelian group `H` is generated by two elements modulo `H'`, then `H'` is a cyclic `Z[H/H']`-module generated by one basic commutator: the standard metabelian commutator identities express every conjugate commutator in the normal closure of that commutator. Thus, for a 2-generated metabelian SMP 2-group, `|H'|<=2^(2+1)=8`.

For an order-256 parent `H` of a hypothetical order-512 minimum counterexample, `|H:H'|=2^d` and `|H'|=2^(8-d)`. The case `d=2` would require `|H'|=64`, contradicting `|H'|<=8`. Therefore every possible parent has `3<=d<=6` (the upper bound comes from `H'` being noncyclic and hence of order at least 4). The catalogue script's parent tag will enforce `d>=3` explicitly.

## 2026-08-14T13:58:09Z — exponent and order-16 element filters on a parent

`exp(H)=exp(G/Z(G))<=16`.

There is a useful consistency check in the extremal case. In any SMP 2-group `H`, all generators of `<x>` are conjugate to `x`; consequently conjugation induces the full automorphism group on `<x>` and

`N_H(<x>)/C_H(x) ~= Aut(<x>)`.

If `|H|=256` and `|x|=16`, the right side has order 8. If an involution in the elementary abelian center `Z(H)` lies outside `<x>`, then `|C_H(x)|>=32`, forcing `N_H(<x>)=H`; hence `<x>` and `C_H(x)` are normal and `H/C_H(x) ~= Aut(C16) ~= C4 x C2`. But a quotient of an SMP group is SMP, whereas an abelian SMP group is elementary abelian. This is impossible. Thus any parent containing an element of order 16 must have `Z(H)=C2` and the unique central involution must be `x^8` for every such `x`.

## 2026-08-14T14:00:17Z — the six-generator parent has an elementary-abelian maximal subgroup

## 2026-08-14T14:12:52Z — the six-generator parent is impossible

The preceding elementary-maximal-subgroup condition can be sharpened to a contradiction. Suppose an order-512 minimum counterexample has `d=log_2|G:G'|=6`, put `K=G'`, and write `Z=Z(G)=K'=<z>`. Then `K` is `D8` or `Q8`. As above, for `C=C_G(K)` the conjugation image has order 8, `M=KC` has index 2 in `G`, `K cap C=Z`, and `[K,C]=1`. Also `C'<=K cap C=Z`, so `M'=Z`; moreover `M/Z` is elementary abelian.

The commutator map therefore defines an alternating bilinear form

`beta : M/Z x M/Z -> Z ~= F_2`.

Choose `t in G-M`. Conjugation by `t` induces an isometry `alpha` of `V=M/Z`. Since `t^2 in M` and inner automorphisms of the class-2 group `M` act trivially on `M/Z`, `alpha^2=1`. Put `N=alpha-1` (the same as `alpha+1` in characteristic 2). The displacement space `im(N)` is totally isotropic: if `u=Nx` and `v=Ny`, then `alpha(v)=v`, and invariance gives

`beta(x+u,v)=beta(alpha(x),alpha(v))=beta(x,v)`,

so `beta(u,v)=0`.

On the other hand, because `G=<M,t>`,

`G'/Z = <M', [M,t]>/Z = im(N)`.

Thus `im(N)=K/Z`. But the restriction of `beta` to `K/Z` is the commutator form of `K`; it is nondegenerate (and in particular nonzero) for both `D8` and `Q8`. This contradicts total isotropy. Therefore `d=6` cannot occur. Any order-256 SMP parent of a first order-512 counterexample must have `3<=d<=5`.
