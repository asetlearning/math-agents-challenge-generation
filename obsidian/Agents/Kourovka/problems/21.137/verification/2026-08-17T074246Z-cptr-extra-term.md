## Conventions reconstructed before checking any equation

Let the kernel coordinates be

`K=A x F_p`,

with `p` odd, alternating bilinear form `beta`, and multiplication

`(a,z)(b,w)=(a+b,z+w+(1/2)beta(a,b))`.

Then

`(a,z)^(-1)=(-a,-z)`

and, for the usual right-conjugation notation `y^x=x^(-1)yx`,

`(a,z)^(eta,e)=(a,z+beta(a,eta))`.                       `(V1)`

Choose normalized right-hand section representatives with

`x_a x_b=x_(ab)u_(a,b)`,

`u_(a,b)=(r_(a,b),s_(a,b))`,

and define the right action

`alpha_t(k)=k^(x_t)`,

`alpha_t(a,z)=(L_t a,z+ell_t(a))`.

Thus `L_t` preserves `beta`, and `ell_t` is linear.  Put

`D_t=L_t-I`, `E_m=I+L_t+...+L_t^(m-1)` with `E_0=0`,

`N_t=D_t^(p-1)`, and `M_t=D_t^(p-2)`.

For an order-`p` quotient element `t`, inner conjugation by `x_t^p` is
trivial on the `A` coordinate, so `L_t^p=I` and `D_t^p=0`.

These conventions reproduce every action direction used below.  Reversing the
section-factor convention would reverse several formulas; no such reversal is
made here.

## Normalized bar boundary and the cycle

With trivial coefficients, the normalized inhomogeneous differential is

`partial[a|b|c]=[b|c]-[ab|c]+[a|bc]-[a|b]`.              `(V2)`

For commuting order-`p` elements `h,j`, set

`Z(h,j)=sum_(i=1)^(p-1)([j|h^i|h]-[h^i|j|h]+[h^i|h|j])`.

Expanding the `i`-th parenthesis gives, before summing,

`[j|h^(i+1)]-[j|h^i]-[j|h]`

` +[h^i|j]+[h|j]-[h^(i+1)|j]`.                           `(V3)`

The terms suppressed from `(V3)` cancel pairwise using `(h^i)j=j(h^i)`:

- `[h^i|h]` cancels between the first and third bars;
- `-[j(h^i)|h]+[(h^i)j|h]=0`;
- `-[h^i|jh]+[h^i|hj]=0`.

The two telescoping sums are respectively

`[j|h^p]-[j|h]=-[j|h]`

and

`[h|j]-[h^p|j]=[h|j]`,

because normalized bars containing `1` vanish.  Adding the constant terms gives
`-p[j|h]+p[h|j]=0` in characteristic `p`.  Therefore

`partial Z(h,j)=0`.                                       `(V4)`

The claimant's normalized boundary and all three shuffle signs are correct.

## The scalar `(C4)` sign and `(E0)`

Associativity of `(x_ax_b)x_c=x_a(x_bx_c)` gives the right-action carry identity

`u_(ab,c) alpha_c(u_(a,b))=u_(a,bc)u_(b,c)`.              `(V5)`

Its vector row is

`r_(ab,c)+L_c r_(a,b)=r_(a,bc)+r_(b,c)`.

Its scalar row, with no terms moved or reordered, is

`s_(ab,c)+s_(a,b)+ell_c(r_(a,b))`

` +(1/2)beta(r_(ab,c),L_c r_(a,b))`

`=s_(a,bc)+s_(b,c)+(1/2)beta(r_(a,bc),r_(b,c))`.          `(V6)`

Consequently

`Q(a,b,c)=ell_c(r_(a,b))`

` +(1/2)beta(r_(ab,c),L_c r_(a,b))`

` -(1/2)beta(r_(a,bc),r_(b,c))`

equals

`s_(b,c)-s_(ab,c)+s_(a,bc)-s_(a,b)=(delta s)(a,b,c)`.    `(V7)`

Thus the coboundary sign agrees exactly with `(V2)`.

Evaluating `(V7)` on `Z` independently gives the following eight-term summand:

`ell_h(r_(j,h^i))-ell_h(r_(h^i,j))+ell_j(r_(h^i,h))`

` +(1/2)beta(r_((h^i)j,h),L_h(r_(j,h^i)-r_(h^i,j)))`

` -(1/2)beta(r_(j,h^(i+1)),r_(h^i,h))`

` +(1/2)beta(r_(h^i,jh),r_(j,h))`

` +(1/2)beta(r_(h^(i+1),j),L_j r_(h^i,h))`

` -(1/2)beta(r_(h^i,hj),r_(h,j))`.                       `(V8)`

Every action and every sign in the submitted row agrees with `(V8)`.  Since
`Q=delta s` and `partial Z=0`,

`Omega(h,j)=<Q,Z>=<s,partial Z>=0`.                       `(E0)`

This is exact, but its zero value comes from the coboundary/cycle pairing itself.

## Polynomial and adjoint identities used by ordered `(C5)`

The following identities account for the terms that can otherwise be lost in a
power collection:

`sum_(m=0)^(p-1)L^m=(L-I)^(p-1)=N`,                      `(V9)`

`sum_(m=0)^(p-1)E_m=(L-I)^(p-2)=M`.                      `(V10)`

For the ordered quadratic sum, symplecticity gives

`beta(L^m a,L^n a)=beta(L^(m-n)a,a)` for `m>n`.

There are `p-d` pairs with `m-n=d`, hence

`sum_(0<=n<m<=p-1) beta(L^m a,L^n a)`

`=beta((sum_(d=1)^(p-1)(p-d)L^d)a,a)`.

The polynomial in parentheses is exactly `M+N`.  Moreover `N` is self-adjoint:
from `L^*=L^(-1)` one gets

`N^*=(D^*)^(p-1)=L^(-(p-1))D^(p-1)=LN=N`,

where `D^p=0` is used in the last equality.  Therefore

`beta(Na,a)=beta(a,Na)=-beta(Na,a)=0`

because `p` is odd.  The complete ordered identity is consequently

`sum_(0<=n<m<=p-1) beta(L^m a,L^n a)=beta(Ma,a)`.         `(V11)`

This proves, rather than assumes, the quadratic simplification used both in the
section-power formula and in `(E2)`.

## Every right-action and ordered cyclic factor

Repeatedly moving a kernel factor past the next section representative uses

`k x_t=x_t alpha_t(k)`.

It yields the exact descending product

`x_t^p=u_(t^(p-1),t) alpha_t(u_(t^(p-2),t)) ...`

`       ... alpha_t^(p-2)(u_(t,t))`.                     `(V12)`

For `R_(t,i)=r_(t^i,t)` and `e_i=p-1-i`,

`alpha_t^(e_i)(R_(t,i),s_(t^i,t))`

`=(L_t^(e_i)R_(t,i),`

`  s_(t^i,t)+ell_t(E_(e_i)R_(t,i)))`.

Thus, writing these coordinates as `a_(t,i),b_(t,i)`, the factors occur from
left to right in the order `i=p-1,p-2,...,1`, and

`c_t=sum_(i=1)^(p-1)a_(t,i)`,

`d_t=sum_(i=1)^(p-1)b_(t,i)`

` +(1/2)sum_(1<=k<i<=p-1)beta(a_(t,i),a_(t,k))`.          `(E1)`

The inequality `k<i` is correct: the factor with the larger index `i` is earlier
in the actual product.  No action, `ell` term, or quadratic pair is omitted.

For commuting `h,j`, comparison of

`x_hx_j=x_(hj)u_(h,j)` and `x_jx_h=x_(hj)u_(j,h)`

gives

`x_h^(x_j)=x_hv`,

`v=u_(j,h)^(-1)u_(h,j)=(q,z)`,

`q=r_(h,j)-r_(j,h)`,

`z=s_(h,j)-s_(j,h)-(1/2)beta(r_(j,h),r_(h,j))`.          `(V13)`

The minus sign in `z` comes from multiplying the inverse of `u_(j,h)` first.

The right-action power is again descending:

`(x_hv)^p=x_h^p alpha_h^(p-1)(v) ... alpha_h(v)v`.        `(V14)`

Since

`alpha_h^m(q,z)=(L_h^m q,z+ell_h(E_mq))`,

the increment after `x_h^p=(c_h,d_h)` has vector

`sum_(m=0)^(p-1)L_h^m q`

and scalar

`p z+sum_(m=0)^(p-1)ell_h(E_mq)`

` +(1/2)sum_(0<=n<m<=p-1)beta(L_h^m q,L_h^n q)`

` +(1/2)beta(c_h,sum_(m=0)^(p-1)L_h^m q)`.               `(E2)`

This independently reproduces all four central contributions and their order.

Now `(V9)`--`(V11)` turn the first three contributions into

`N_h q`, `ell_h(M_h q)`, and `(1/2)beta(M_h q,q)`.

Also `D_h c_h=0`, since `x_h^p` commutes with `x_h`.  By self-adjointness of
`N_h`,

`beta(c_h,N_h q)=beta(N_h c_h,q)=0`.                      `(V15)`

The `p z` term is zero in characteristic `p`; it was not silently discarded.

## Independent derivation of `(E3)`--`(E7)`

The group identity

`(x_h^p)^(x_j)=(x_h^(x_j))^p`

has left side

`alpha_j(c_h,d_h)=(L_j c_h,d_h+ell_j(c_h))`.

Comparing it with `(E2)` after the preceding simplifications gives

`D_j c_h=N_h q`,                                          `(E3)`

`ell_j(c_h)=ell_h(M_h q)+(1/2)beta(M_h q,q)`.             `(E4)`

Swapping `h,j` changes `q` to `-q`, so

`D_h c_j=-N_j q`.                                         `(E3')`

Next, `alpha_t^p` is conjugation by `x_t^p=(c_t,d_t)`.  Its central coordinate,
using `(V9)`, gives for every `a in A`

`ell_t(N_t a)=beta(a,c_t)`.                               `(E5)`

Applying `(E5)` first with `(t,a)=(j,c_h)` and then with
`(t,a)=(h,c_j)`, and using `(E3)`, `(E3')`, and commutation of `D_h,D_j`, gives

`beta(c_h,c_j)=ell_j(M_j N_h q)=ell_h(M_h N_j q)`.        `(E6)`

Both signs are correct.  In the second equality,
`beta(c_h,c_j)=-beta(c_j,c_h)` cancels the minus sign in `(E3')`.

For completeness, the right-action identity

`x_hx_j=x_jx_hv`

implies

`alpha_j alpha_h=iota_v alpha_h alpha_j`.

Its scalar coordinate is

`ell_j(D_h a)-ell_h(D_j a)=beta(L_h L_j a,q)`.            `(V16)`

At `a=M_j M_h q`, the two left-hand terms are the equal values in `(E6)`, so

`beta(L_h L_j M_j M_h q,q)=0`.                            `(E7)`

This is one scalar orthogonality relation.  It does not say that `M_h N_j q` is in
the radical of `beta`, and it does not make either scalar in `(E6)` zero.

## Complete arbitrary normalized-section transformations

Let

`x'_t=x_tk_t`, `k_t=(eta_t,z_t)`, and `k_1=1`.

From `(V1)`,

`alpha'_t=iota_(k_t)alpha_t`,

`L'_t=L_t`,

`ell'_t(a)=ell_t(a)+beta(L_t a,eta_t)`.                   `(V17)`

The descending ordered power

`(x_tk_t)^p=x_t^p alpha_t^(p-1)(k_t)...alpha_t(k_t)k_t`

and `(V9)`--`(V11)` give

`c'_t=c_t+N_t eta_t`,

`d'_t=d_t+ell_t(M_t eta_t)`

`       +(1/2)beta(M_t eta_t,eta_t)`.                     `(V18)`

Here `p z_t=0`.  The apparent cross term
`(1/2)beta(c_t,N_t eta_t)` is zero by `(V15)` with `q` replaced by `eta_t`.

Direct reassociation of `x'_ax'_b` gives

`u'_(a,b)=k_(ab)^(-1)u_(a,b)alpha_b(k_a)k_b`.             `(V19)`

Therefore

`r'_(a,b)=-eta_(ab)+r_(a,b)+L_b eta_a+eta_b`.             `(V20)`

For the scalar coordinate retain the actual four vector factors

`v_1=-eta_(ab)`, `v_2=r_(a,b)`, `v_3=L_b eta_a`, `v_4=eta_b`.

Then

`s'_(a,b)=-z_(ab)+s_(a,b)+z_a+ell_b(eta_a)+z_b`

` +(1/2)sum_(1<=m<n<=4)beta(v_m,v_n)`.                    `(V21)`

Equations `(V17)`--`(V21)` contain every central coordinate, action, `ell` term,
and ordered quadratic pair.  They agree with the submission.

For the commuting pair,

`q'=q+D_j eta_h-D_h eta_j`,

`c'_t=c_t+N_t eta_t`.                                    `(V22)`

## Covariance and `(E8)`--`(E9)`

Both sections satisfy the associativity identity `(V5)`, hence

`Q'=delta s'` and `Q'-Q=delta(s'-s)`.

Using `(V4)` gives

`Omega'-Omega=<delta(s'-s),Z>=0`.                         `(V23)`

Thus `(E0)` is genuinely invariant under every normalized section change.

For `(E3)`, use commutation of `D_h,D_j` and `D_h^p=0`:

`N_h q'=N_h q+N_h D_j eta_h-N_h D_h eta_j`

`      =D_j c_h+D_j N_h eta_h`

`      =D_j c'_h`.                                        `(V24)`

The primed version of `(E4)` follows independently from the same section-free
identity

`(x'_h{}^p)^(x'_j)=(x'_h{}^(x'_j))^p`

and the already checked ordered formula `(V14)`.  This is not an appeal to the
claimant's formula: it rederives both primed coordinate rows from the group
identity, including the new central coordinate of `v'`; that coordinate is again
multiplied by `p` and vanishes.  The same comparison rederives primed `(E5)` and
hence primed `(E6)`.  Thus `(E3)`--`(E6)` are covariant, but their numerical
basepoint pairing need not be unchanged.

Bilinearity applied to `(V22)` gives exactly

`beta(c'_h,c'_j)-beta(c_h,c_j)`

`=beta(N_h eta_h,c_j)+beta(c_h,N_j eta_j)`

` +beta(N_h eta_h,N_j eta_j)`.                            `(E8)`

If only `eta_h` changes, self-adjointness of `N_h` and `(E3')` give

`beta(N_h eta_h,c_j)=beta(eta_h,N_h c_j)`

`=-beta(eta_h,M_h N_j q)`.                                `(E9)`

The sign, exponent, and factor order in `(E9)` are correct.

For an equality `Omega=beta(c_h,c_j)` to be section-independent without an added
correction, `(E9)` would have to vanish for every `eta_h`, which requires the new
statement `M_h N_j q in rad(beta)`.  Neither `(E3)`--`(E7)` nor `(E0)` proves that
statement.  In particular, `(E7)` is only one pairing with `q`, not radical
membership.

## Why the method does not reach `beta=0`

There are two logically separate gaps.

First, even for the frozen commuting pair, `(E6)` is an expression, not a
vanishing identity.  The invariant scalar `(E0)` is zero, but the submitted rows
do not identify it with the section-sensitive basepoint pairing.  Formula `(E9)`
exhibits the exact correction that any such identification must cancel.  A new
correction term or a new radical-membership lemma is therefore required.

Second, actual-value coverage has the form

`A=union_(t in H)(c_t+N_tA)`.                             `(V25)`

Since `N_tA` is contained in `D_tA`, hence in `[A,H]`, `(V25)` does make

`kappa(t)=c_t+[A,H]`

surjective onto `A/[A,H]`.  But a bilinear form descends to that quotient only if

`[A,H] <= rad(beta)`,

which is not established.  Nor does the union in `(V25)` show that two arbitrary
covered values admit roots whose quotient cosets form the one commuting pair
used above.  Even a new proof of the frozen pair's vanishing would therefore
still need a separate coverage-to-all-pairs bridge.

The certificate supports only the conservative statement that the frozen
argument, as written, stops here.  It does not prove that no stronger consequence
of the full group hypotheses exists, and it does not settle Problem 21.137.

- It is not proved that the full hypotheses can never yield the missing radical
  or coverage lemma by a different argument.
- Nothing is established about the general powerfulness clause or the `p=2`,
  exponent-8 clause.

## What would upgrade it

To advance the target, a later argument would need at least both of the following:

1. a section-invariant corrected pairing or a proved vanishing such as
   `M_h N_j q in rad(beta)` that actually forces the scalar in `(E6)` to be zero;
2. a proof that actual-value coverage transports the resulting pairwise
   statement to every pair in `A`, for example by establishing the needed radical
   containment and a valid commuting-root bridge.

Any universal target claim would then require a fresh full-scope review and the
human gate before `status/proven` could be assigned.
