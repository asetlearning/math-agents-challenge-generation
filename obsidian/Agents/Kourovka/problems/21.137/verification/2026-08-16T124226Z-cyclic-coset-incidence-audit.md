# Verdict

**PASS as a structural PARTIAL_RESULT.** The cyclic-coset lemma is a genuine strengthening of the accepted same-coset result, and the cross-pairing implication is exact. Neither result forces different cyclic pieces to commute or answers the active scope.

# Cyclic-coset lemma

Fix a nontrivial coset \(xQ\), put \(a=x^p\), let conjugation by \(x\) induce \(A=I+N\), and let

\[
W=\operatorname{im}N^{p-1}
\]

on the relevant quotient of \(Q\). For \(1\le j\le p-1\),

\[
(x^j)^p=a^j,\qquad
A^j-I=N\,U_j(N),
\]

where

\[
U_j(N)=jI+\binom j2N+\cdots .
\]

The constant coefficient \(j\) is nonzero in \(\mathbf F_p\), so \(U_j(N)\) is invertible; it is a polynomial in \(N\), hence commutes with \(N\). Consequently

\[
\operatorname{im}(A^j-I)^{p-1}
=\operatorname{im}\bigl(N^{p-1}U_j(N)^{p-1}\bigr)=W.
\]

The already checked affine formula therefore puts the projected power values rooted in \(x^jQ\) inside \(ja+W\). The union of values rooted over the cyclic subgroup \(\langle xQ\rangle\) lies in the linear span \(\langle a,W\rangle\). The same-coset audit proved that \(W\) is totally isotropic and \(a\perp W\); alternatingness also gives \(a\perp a\). Hence \(\langle a,W\rangle\) is totally isotropic. Adding elements of \(C\le Z(Q)\) does not change commutators. The identity coset contributes only \(1\). Thus any two actual power values commute whenever their root cosets lie in the same cyclic subgroup of \(H/Q\).

# Cross-pairing incidence

Let \(u=x^p\) and \(uv=z^p\). If \(zQ\in\langle xQ\rangle\), the cyclic-coset lemma says that \(u\) and \(uv\) commute. Since \(Q\) has class \(2\),

\[
[u,uv]=[u,v],
\]

up to the harmless inverse dictated by the chosen commutator convention; in either convention its triviality is equivalent to \([u,v]=1\). Therefore

\[
zQ\in\langle xQ\rangle\quad\Longrightarrow\quad [u,v]=1.
\]

The same argument with a root \(y\) of \(v\) gives the symmetric implication. Because the root choices were arbitrary, a nonzero pairing forces every chosen product-root coset outside the cyclic subgroups generated by the chosen root cosets for both factors.

# Material effect and boundary

The accepted same-coset piece \(a+W\) is enlarged to one common isotropic linear piece \(\langle a,W\rangle\) for all \(p-1\) nonidentity cosets in a cyclic subgroup of \(H/Q\). The incidence corollary constrains how roots for a product can be placed when two power values do not commute. Closure of the actual power set supplies a root for \(uv\), but does not put that root in either required cyclic subgroup. Hence these are useful structural restrictions, not a proof of global abelianness and not an improvement of the \(p^{2p+3}\) bound.
