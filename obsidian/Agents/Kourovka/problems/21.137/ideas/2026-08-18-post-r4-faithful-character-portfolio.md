# Ranked portfolio

## 1. `CHAR-GALOIS-WEYL` — selected proof representation

**Idea.**  In a hypothetical minimum counterexample, use cyclic `Z(G)` to
construct a faithful irreducible complex representation `rho`.  Choose
`a,b in P` with `n=[a,b]` generating `N`, and literal roots

`x^p=a`, `y^p=b`, `z^p=ab`.

If `rho(n)=zeta I`, with `zeta` a primitive `p`th root, then the matrices
`A=rho(a)` and `B=rho(b)` form a Weyl pair: `AB=zeta BA` (up to the frozen
commutator convention).  Let `sigma` be the cyclotomic automorphism
`xi -> xi^(1+p)` on `p^2`th roots.  Since `G` has exponent `p^2`,

`chi^sigma(g)=chi(g^(1+p))=chi(g g^p)`,

while `sigma` fixes all character values on the exponent-`p` subgroup `P`, so
`chi^sigma|P=chi|P`.  The first task is to compute the resulting Clifford-block
discrepancy exactly.  If it scalarizes to a linear character on the relevant
inertia quotient, compare the three roots and the Weyl multiplier: a quotient
linear character has trivial alternating multiplier, whereas `[A,B]` contributes
`zeta`.  The desired certificate is that this scalar cannot be absorbed by a
block permutation or root change.

**Why it might work.**  It retains the nonzero multiplication law in `P'` as a
scalar in every matrix block and uses all three *existing group elements*, not
an affine image or a chosen root section.  It is prime-uniform and does not
reuse the R4 kernel, quotient, action, factor system, shear orbit, central rows,
root multiplicities, or modular group-algebra sum.

**Most likely failure.**  `rho|P` may have several isotypic blocks.  The Galois
twist can then differ by a non-scalar matrix cocycle and a permutation of
blocks, allowing the Weyl scalar to be absorbed.  Equal restricted characters
alone do not license a global scalar twist.

**Cheap falsifier.**  Build the complete Clifford decomposition first.  Kill
if the discrepancy is genuinely matrix-valued, or exhibit a formal block
system satisfying the twist, root, and Weyl rows with `zeta != 1`.  Do not
continue by assuming multiplicity one.

## 2. `MAXCLASS-POWER-SHIFT` — prime-uniform structured-family proof

**Idea.**  Treat finite maximal-class odd-`p` groups of exponent `p^2` directly.
Derive, rather than cite, the two required lower-central statements:
`g^p in gamma_p(G)` for every `g`, and the exponent/order shift forcing
`gamma_(2p)(G)=1`.  Then every literal power lies in `gamma_p(G)` and
`[gamma_p(G),gamma_p(G)]<=gamma_(2p)(G)=1`, independently of closure.

**Why it might work.**  Power-depth in a single lower-central chain is a
different representation from root fibres and could give a clean substantial
family theorem plus a sharp boundary for any counterexample.

**Falsifier and likely failure.**  Kill on an explicit maximal-class pc law of
exponent `p^2` with nontrivial `gamma_(2p)`, or if either shift requires an
unavailable classification theorem.  Exceptional maximal-class groups may make
the proposed exponent/order implication false.  Cost: 40--50 hand minutes; no
compute.  This is ranked second because it is likely only a proper-family result.

## 3. `PCOVER-QUOTIENT-INHERITANCE` — finite counterexample certificate search

**Why it might work.**  Quotient inheritance prunes whole descendant branches
