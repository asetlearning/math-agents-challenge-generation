## Immediate reductions

Closure gives `P=G^p`, because the subgroup generated by all `p`th-power
values equals the value set itself.  Moreover every element of `P` is itself
some `g^p`, so exponent `p^2` of `G` gives `exp(P)|p` (this does not merely
check the generators of `P`).

Assume `P'` is nontrivial.  Choose the first lower-central grade `c` of `G`
on which `P'` is nonzero, and pass to `Q=G/gamma_{c+1}(G)`.  Then the surviving
image of `P'` lies in `gamma_c(Q)<=Z(Q)`, so the image of `P` has class at most
two and exponent dividing `p`.  The reviewed class-at-most-`p+1` theorem says
that any actual counterexample necessarily has class at least `p+2`; it does
not remove this central quotient.

## +8 exact gate: the coefficient-one transgression

Take actual values `A=x^p`, `B=y^p` with surviving central commutator
`C=[A,B]`, and use literal-image closure to choose `z` with `z^p=AB`.  With
the convention `[u,v]=u^-1 v^-1 u v`, one has the exact identity

`C=[A,AB]=[A,z^p]=h h^z ... h^(z^(p-1))`, where `h=[A,z]`.

Here `h` and all its conjugates lie in the normal subgroup `P`, hence have
order dividing `p`.  Hall collection of the displayed norm has linear part

`[A,z]^p [A,z,z]^binom(p,2) ... [A,{}_(p-1)z]^binom(p,p-1)
 [A,{}_p z]`.

All coefficients before the last are divisible by `p`, but the extreme term
`[A,{}_p z]` has coefficient `binom(p,p)=1`.  The remaining nonlinear Hall
factors contain at least two entries from `P` and therefore lie in the same
central layer `P'`; minimality does not kill them.  Thus the leading identity
is a *transgression formula* for `C`, not a vanishing formula.  Equivalently,
if `alpha` is conjugation by `z` on `P`, then

`alpha^p=Inn(z^p)=Inn(AB)` and `A^-1 alpha^p(A)=[A,B]=C`.

Modulo `P'`, `alpha^p=1`; the coefficient-one `p`-step displacement is allowed
to land nontrivially in `P'`.  Exponent `p` of `P` kills the `p`-divisible
terms but cannot kill this coefficient-one term.  This is the precise failure
of the proposed leading-term vanishing lemma.  The next work block will test
whether using the roots for *all* products imposes compatibility between these
pair-dependent transgressions; no bounded carrier or computation will be used.

## Exact linearization in the first central quotient

Keep the quotient `Q` and write `Pbar` for the image of `P`.  Since `p` is odd,
the Baer correspondence makes the class-two exponent-`p` group `Pbar` into an
`F_p`-Lie algebra `L`; every automorphism of `Pbar` is linear on `L`.  Let
`q` denote the Lie element corresponding to the group element `AB`, let `z^p=AB`,
let `alpha` be conjugation by `z` on `Pbar`, and put `D=alpha-I`.  Then

`alpha^p = conjugation by AB = I+ad(q)`

and the characteristic-`p` binomial identity gives the exact equation

`D^p=ad(q)`.                                                     (T)

This formula includes, rather than discards, every nonlinear Hall correction.
Because `z` centralizes `z^p`, `D(q)=0`.  If `C=[A,B]` is the chosen nonzero
central commutator, then

`D^p(A)=[A,q]=C` and `D(C)=0`.                                  (J)

Thus `A,D(A),...,D^p(A)=C` is a Jordan chain of length `p+1`.  The fixed
noncentral vector `q` is outside its span: the kernel of `D` on that one block
is exactly `<C>`, whereas `[A,q]=C != 0`.  Consequently

`dim_Fp L >= p+2`, hence `|Pbar| >= p^(p+2)` and `|G| >= p^(p+3)`.

The last order bound uses that `z^p=AB != 1`, so `z` has order `p^2` and lies
outside the exponent-`p` subgroup `Pbar`.  The reviewed class-at-most-`p+1`
theorem separately forces `class(Q)>=p+2`.

## What the three available roots actually say

Choose roots `x^p=A`, `y^p=B`, and `z^p=AB`, and put
`D_x=c_x-I`, `D_y=c_y-I`, `D_z=c_z-I` on `L`.  The entire leading compatibility
is

`D_x^p=ad(A)`, `D_y^p=ad(B)`,
`D_z^p=ad(A)+ad(B)=D_x^p+D_y^p`.

There is no licensed identity between `D_z` and `D_x,D_y`: closure supplies a
root of the product, not a multiplicative or additive root section.  This is
also visible without choices.  For

`U=Q/C_Q(Pbar) <= Aut(Pbar)`,

the literal set of `p`th-power values is exactly

`{u^p:u in U}=Inn(Pbar) ~= Pbar/Z(Pbar)`.

The forward inclusion follows from `(c_g)^p=c_(g^p)` and `g^p in Pbar`; the
reverse inclusion follows because every element of the literal subgroup
`Pbar` has a `p`th root in `Q`.  This image is already an abelian subgroup,
while the target layer `Pbar'` lies in the kernel of the action.  Hence ordinary
central-quotient induction loses exactly the commutator it is meant to kill.

## Sharpness of the single product-root equation

The obstruction is not just failure to estimate a coefficient.  On the
`F_p`-space with basis `e_0,...,e_p,q`, set `[e_0,q]=e_p` and all other basis
brackets to zero.  Let `D(e_i)=e_(i+1)` for `i<p`, `D(e_p)=D(q)=0`, and
`T=I+D`.  Then `T` preserves the bracket and

`T^p=I+D^p=I+ad(q)`.

Via the Baer correspondence this gives a class-two exponent-`p` group `P_0`
of order `p^(p+2)` and an automorphism `T`.  In the semidirect product by an
element `t` of order `p^2` acting as `T`, the element `t^p q^-1` is central;
quotienting by it gives an exponent-exactly-`p^2` extension with `z^p=q` and
the nonzero transgression `[e_0,q]=e_p`.  In BCH coordinates, take
`A=e_0` and `B=q-e_0-(1/2)e_p`; then `A*B=q` and `[A,B]=e_p` (the scalar
`1/2` exists because `p` is odd).  The quotient modulo `P_0` is cyclic of
order `p`, so every element has `p`th power in the exponent-`p` subgroup
`P_0`; hence the extension has exponent at most `p^2`, while `z^p=q != 1`
makes the exponent exactly `p^2`.

This is deliberately **not** a target witness: it does not make `A`, `B`, or
all of `P_0` literal `p`th-power values.  It shows sharply that exponent
`p^2`, a central surviving `P_0'`, and a root of the product do not by
themselves force the leading term to vanish.  Any repair must use a genuinely
coherent relation among roots of `A`, `B`, and `AB`; the hypothesis supplies
only their `p`th powers, as the quotient `U` identity makes exact.
