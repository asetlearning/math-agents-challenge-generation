# Exact statement and semantic check

I found no additional gap in the identity

\[
  G_4/G_4''\cong H_4,
  \qquad [G_4:G_4'']=2^{21}.
\]

The conditional-law semantics are handled in the correct direction. If
\(G_4=P_4/R_4\), \(K_4=P_4'\), and
\(N_4=K_4^2[K_4,K_4]\), admissibility of the marked quotient
\(H_4=P_4/N_4\) implies \(R_4\le N_4\). It does not require arbitrary
quotients of admissible groups to remain admissible. Since \(R_4\le K_4\),
\(G_4'=K_4/R_4\). The latter is generated by conjugates of the basic
commutators; those are involutions under the source law. For any
involution-generated group \(Y\), \(Y^2=Y'\), so

\[
  G_4''=(G_4')^2[G_4',G_4']
       =N_4/R_4.
\]

This supports the following terminology, with important qualifications.

- \(H_4\) is exactly the abstract metabelianization of the marked universal
  group \(G_4\), and it is itself admissible. It is consequently initial among
  marked **metabelian admissible** targets.
- It is not a proof that \(G_4\) is metabelian, finite, solvable, or of order
  \(2^{21}\). The number \(2^{21}\) is the finite index of \(G_4''\), even if
  both groups are infinite.
- For an arbitrary admissible target \(T\), the abstract quotient \(T/T''\) is
  a quotient of \(H_4\), but it need not remain admissible: quotienting may
  create new involutions. Thus one must not call every such quotient an object
  of the original conditional-law class, nor assert that every target has
  metabelianization of order exactly \(2^{21}\).

The known finite marked quotient \(E\) of order at least \(2^{34}\) also gives
an unconditional residual consequence: \(G_4''\) maps onto \(E''\), while
\(|E/E''|\le |H_4|=2^{21}\), hence

\[
  |E''|\ge 2^{13}.
\]

So \(G_4''\) has a nontrivial finite quotient of order at least \(2^{13}\),
whether or not \(G_4\) is finite. If \(G_4\) is finite, this specializes to the
recorded divisor/lower bound \(|G_4''|\ge2^{13}\).

# What an upper-bound route must now do

The exact metabelianization isolates the entire missing upper bound in

\[
  D:=G_4''=N_4/R_4.
\]

Producing further finite quotients can only strengthen lower bounds; it cannot
prove \(D\) finite. A viable upper-bound certificate must instead control the
derived descent of \(D\), or of the smaller normal closure reduction from the
same audit.

The first linear layer is legitimate:

\[
  D/D'
\]

is a finite elementary abelian group, and conjugation makes it an
\(\mathbf F_2[H_4]\)-module. One should derive the module relations forced by
the involution law and compute/bound this layer. It would be incorrect to treat
all of \(D\) itself as an \(H_4\)-module before proving it abelian.

The same procedure can then be iterated on

\[
  D^{(i)}/D^{(i+1)}.
\]

Every such layer is finite elementary abelian once the preceding finite-index
step is established. The decisive certificate is a uniform termination
\(D^{(r)}=1\), or another explicit finite bound on the residual kernel. Merely
computing several finite layers does not rule out a nontrivial perfect or
infinitely descending residual subgroup.

There is a potentially cheaper finiteness route already exposed by the audit.
With \(H=\langle a,b,c\rangle\) and
\(N=\langle d^G\rangle=\langle d^h:h\in H\rangle\), one has
\(|H|\le2^{11}\), so \(N\) is generated by at most \(2^{11}\) involutions;
all its derived factors are finite elementary abelian. Proving this \(N\)
solvable would prove \(N\), and hence \(G_4\), finite. Thus the clean next
upper-bound program is:

1. seek a hand identity or finite relative-presentation argument giving a
   derived-length bound for \(N\) (or directly for \(D\));
2. bound each elementary-abelian derived layer by module calculations;
3. only after finiteness is secured, combine those bounds with the exact
   factor \(2^{21}\) to constrain \(|G_4|\).

This separates the logical jobs cleanly: the metabelianization supplies the
known finite quotient, while solvability/termination of the residual normal
subgroup is the missing upper-bound theorem.
