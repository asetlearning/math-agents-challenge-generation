# Scope and sourcing

This addresses the four-generator universal object with the fourth-power law imposed on every pair of involutions. Everything below is **General knowledge, unverified** or a fresh hand assessment of the current clean run. No web or historical solution material was used.

# Structural lemma

For involutions \(a,b\),

\[
[a,b]=(ab)^2,qquad [a,b]^2=1,qquad
[[a,b],a]=[[a,b],b]=1.
\]

Thus every binary commutator of involutions is again an involution and centralizes its two entries. The logged polarization identity is also useful: if \(u,v,z\) are involutions and \([u,v]=1\), then

\[
[[u,z],[v,z]]=1.
\]

This is the right input for a normal-closure induction. If \(G=\langle H,t\rangle\), where \(H\) is generated by three involutions, then

\[
\langle t\rangle^G=\langle t^h:h\in H\rangle.
\]

The generating orbit consists of involutions whose pairwise commutators are involutions. A viable rank induction must bound this normal closure as an \(H\)-operator group; bounding only the six commutators of the marked generators is insufficient.

# Hidden semantic trap

The admissible class is not visibly quotient-closed: a quotient can create new involutions whose lifts have order four, so the original law says nothing about their products. Therefore an ordinary quotient induction or a presentation with only marked-generator pair relations is invalid.

The current-run universal construction by intersection of all admissible marked kernels avoids this problem. Equivalently, the iterative Horn closure

\[
N_{r+1}=\left\langle N_r,\ (uv)^4: u^2,v^2\in N_r\right\rangle^{P_4}
\]

and \(N_\omega=\bigcup_rN_r\) encode the exact conditional law. Any upper-bound argument should work with this saturation or an equivalent rewriting certificate.

# Assessment of the current lower bounds

I found no mathematical gap in the hand construction

\[
H_4=P_4/\bigl((P_4')^2[P_4',P_4']\bigr).
\]

The free kernel \(P_4'\) has rank 17, so \(|H_4|=2^{21}\); its derived group is elementary abelian and its exponent divides four. It is therefore an admissible quotient and a lower bound, universal only in the elementary-derived subfamily.

The later \(2^{33}\) fibre-product construction also appears internally coherent on hand inspection. The key isolation word lies in \(\Phi_2(P_{T_i}')\), dies in \(H_4\) and in the other three factors because a two-involution subgroup has elementary derived group, and realizes an arbitrary kernel element in the selected factor. This supports equality of the diagonal image with the fibre product. It remains only a lower bound and should go to Validator before being relied on; it gives neither finiteness nor an upper bound for \(G_4\).

# Ranked next strategies

## I1 — finite-image 2-core reduction

For a finite admissible image and an involutory marked generator \(s\), every \(\langle s,s^g\rangle\) is a 2-group of order at most eight. The Baer--Suzuki theorem should put \(s\) in \(O_2(G)\); the four generators then force the whole finite image to be a 2-group.

This is a strong reduction for finite candidates, but it needs an exact theorem citation before use. It does not show that the universal object is finite or residually finite.

Success certificate: a cited Baer--Suzuki application with the identity/degenerate cases stated. Kill criterion: do not infer anything about an infinite universal object from finite images alone.

## I2 — normal-closure filtration over \(G_3\)

Take \(H=\langle x_1,x_2,x_3\rangle\), a quotient of the source's finite \(G_3\), and \(K=\langle x_4\rangle^G=\langle x_4^h:h\in H\rangle\). Filter \(K\) by commutators with \(H\) and by squares. The exact involution identities make every basic outer commutator an involution; the target is a terminating finite module/commutator normal form for \(K\).

First bounded experiment: derive all weight-four consequences of the polarization identity for four distinct involutions and determine whether the weight-four layer is an explicit quotient of a finite \(\mathbf F_2H\)-module. The \(2^{21}\) quotient shows that a class-at-most-three truncation cannot be universal.

Success certificate: a finite list of normal-form parameters stable under conjugation by \(H\). Kill criterion: if weight five introduces a new unconstrained module of the same kind, switch to Horn saturation rather than extending the list informally.

## I3 — Horn-saturation upper approximation

Study \(P_4/N_r\) and prove either finite stabilization or a persistent nontrivial layer. Stabilization supplies the missing universal upper certificate; persistence may indicate infinitude. The membership-witness argument identifies \(N_\omega\) as the intended exact fixed point.

Success certificate: a terminating confluent rewrite system or a certified nontrivial quotient at every stage. Kill criterion: a finite quotient of one stage is only a lower bound and must not be identified with the fixed point.

# Self-critique

The local involution calculus is strong but does not presently bound the length of iterated commutators with distinct entries. The large admissible quotients show that aggressive low-class guesses are misleading. The best near-term result is the rigorous lower-bound package; the exact-order problem still needs a genuinely global upper mechanism.
