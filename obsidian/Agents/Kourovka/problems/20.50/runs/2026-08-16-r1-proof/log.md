### Exact involution calculus

Use \([a,b]=a^{-1}b^{-1}ab\). If \(a,b\) are involutions, then

\[
[a,b]=(ab)^2,\qquad [a,b]^2=1,
\qquad [[a,b],a]=[[a,b],b]=1.
\]

The first two assertions use the source law; the centralization follows because conjugation by either \(a\) or \(b\) inverts \(ab\), hence fixes \((ab)^2=(ab)^{-2}\). Inductively, every binary/outer commutator evaluated on involutions is either identity or an involution.

If \(u,v,z\) are involutions and \([u,v]=1\), then

\[
[[u,z],[v,z]]=1. \tag{polarization}
\]

Indeed, \([uv,z]=[u,z]^v[v,z]\). The left side and the two factors on the right are involutions or identity. A product of two involutions is an involution or identity exactly when the two commute. Thus \([u,z]^v\) commutes with \([v,z]\); the latter centralizes \(v\), so conjugating by \(v\) removes the superscript. This is an exact group identity under the stated hypotheses, not only a graded relation.

Consequences currently justified:

- every lower-central factor \(\gamma_k(G)/\gamma_{k+1}(G)\) is an elementary abelian 2-group, generated by simple commutators;
- the degree bounds through weight three are \(4,6,8\): repeated-letter triples vanish, and Jacobi leaves at most two triple commutators for each of the four 3-subsets;
- every derived term is involution-generated; since \(G/G'\) has order at most \(2^4\), Schreier induction shows that a solvable admissible four-generated group is finite of 2-power order;
- Baer–Suzuki shows every finite quotient of the universal group is a 2-group: for each canonical generator image \(q\), every \(\langle q,q^h\rangle\) is an image of a dihedral group of order at most 8, hence \(q\in O_2\), and these images generate the quotient.

These statements do **not** establish that the universal object is solvable or nilpotent.

### Universal-object semantics

Let \(P_m=C_2*\cdots*C_2\). The class defined by

\[
x^2=y^2=1\Longrightarrow (xy)^4=1
\]

is closed under subgroups and direct products, though not under arbitrary quotients. Intersect the kernels of all admissible marked epimorphisms from \(P_m\); the resulting quotient \(U_m\) embeds diagonally in their product, so it is itself admissible and maps onto every admissible marked object. The quotient \(C_2^m\) ensures that the canonical generators remain exact involutions. Thus the assigned “largest” object exists under the canonical universal interpretation despite quotient-created involutions.

Equivalently, define \(N_0=1\) and let \(N_{r+1}\) be the normal closure of \(N_r\) together with every \((uv)^4\) for which \(u^2,v^2\in N_r\). Then \(N_\omega=\bigcup_rN_r\) is already a fixed point: any two membership witnesses occur at a common finite stage. Hence \(U_m=P_m/N_\omega\). This gives a possible upper-approximation strategy, but no finite stopping stage is currently known.

## Personal proof audit I — the exact \(2^{21}\) quotient

This subsection is a fresh derivation by the rostered Problem agent; no delegated output is cited or used.

Let

\[
P_4=\langle x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4\mid x_i^2=1\ (1\le i\le4)\rangle
   =C_2*C_2*C_2*C_2,
\]

let \(\epsilon:P_4\to E_4=C_2^4\) be abelianization, and put \(K_4=\ker\epsilon=P_4'\).

### Rank of \(K_4\)

The standard graph of groups for \(P_4\) is a star with one trivial central vertex, four leaf vertex groups of order 2, and four trivial edge groups. Its Euler characteristic is

\[
\chi(P_4)=1+4\cdot\frac12-4=-1.
\]

The subgroup \(K_4\) has index \(|E_4|=16\). It is torsion-free: every finite-order element of a free product is conjugate into a factor, while a nonidentity conjugate of \(x_i\) still has nonzero image \(e_i\) in the abelian quotient and hence cannot lie in \(K_4\). By the subgroup theorem for free products, \(K_4\) is therefore free. Multiplicativity of graph-of-groups Euler characteristic gives

\[
\chi(K_4)=16\chi(P_4)=-16.
\]

A free group of rank \(r\) has Euler characteristic \(1-r\), so \(r=17\).

### Quotient, exact order, and admissibility

Define

\[
N_4=K_4^2[K_4,K_4]=\Phi_2(K_4),\qquad H_4=P_4/N_4,
\]

where \(K_4^2\) denotes the subgroup generated by all squares. The subgroup \(N_4\) is characteristic in \(K_4\), and \(K_4=P_4'\) is characteristic in \(P_4\), so \(N_4\triangleleft P_4\). Since \(K_4\) is free of rank 17,

\[
K_4/N_4\cong C_2^{17}.
\]

Consequently

\[
1\longrightarrow C_2^{17}\longrightarrow H_4
 \longrightarrow C_2^4\longrightarrow1,
\qquad |H_4|=2^{17+4}=2^{21}.
\]

Each marked image \(\bar x_i\) has order dividing 2 by the presentation and is nonidentity because its further image in \(E_4\) is \(e_i\ne0\). Thus all four have exact order 2 and generate \(H_4\). Moreover

\[
H_4'=K_4/N_4\cong C_2^{17}.
\]

For any \(h\in H_4\), the quotient \(H_4/H_4'\cong C_2^4\) gives \(h^2\in H_4'\), and elementary abelianness of \(H_4'\) gives \(h^4=1\). Hence \(H_4\) has exponent at most 4. In particular, \((xy)^4=1\) for every pair of involutions—and indeed for every pair of elements—so \(H_4\) is admissible.

### Exact limited universality

If \(Q\) is any marked four-involution-generated group with \(Q'\) elementary abelian, the marked epimorphism \(P_4\twoheadrightarrow Q\) maps \(K_4=P_4'\) onto \(Q'\) and therefore kills \(K_4^2[K_4,K_4]=N_4\). It factors through \(H_4\). Conversely, \(H_4'\) is elementary abelian. Thus \(H_4\) is precisely the largest marked object in this **restricted subfamily**.

Because \(H_4\) itself satisfies the full all-involutions condition, the full universal \(G_4\) maps onto it. This proves only that \(G_4\) has an admissible quotient of order \(2^{21}\), and that \(2^{21}\mid |G_4|\) if \(G_4\) is finite. It does not supply an upper bound or identify \(G_4\).

Audit result for proposition 1: **passes**.

## Personal proof audit II — the exact \(2^{33}\) fiber-product quotient

This is again a fresh personal derivation. Let \(U_3\) denote the source's universal \(G_3\), whose source-stated order is \(2^{11}\).

### The largest elementary-derived quotient of \(U_3\)

Set \(P_3=C_2*C_2*C_2\), \(K_3=P_3'\), and \(N_3=K_3^2[K_3,K_3]\). Here

\[
\chi(P_3)=1+3/2-3=-1/2,
\]

and \([P_3:K_3]=8\). The same torsion-free/Euler-characteristic argument gives \(\chi(K_3)=-4\), hence \(K_3\) is free of rank 5. Therefore

\[
H_3:=P_3/N_3,\qquad |H_3|=2^{5+3}=2^8.
\]

As above, \(H_3\) is an admissible exponent-at-most-four group. Write \(U_3=P_3/R_3\). Universality of \(U_3\) gives epimorphisms to both \(H_3\) and \(C_2^3\), so

\[
R_3\subseteq N_3\subseteq K_3.
\]

Thus \(U_3'=K_3/R_3\), and

\[
\Phi_2(U_3')=(U_3')^2[U_3',U_3']
   =N_3/R_3.
\]

It follows that

\[
U_3/\Phi_2(U_3')\cong P_3/N_3=H_3.
\]

Every quotient of \(U_3\) whose derived subgroup is elementary abelian must kill \(\Phi_2(U_3')\), so \(H_3\) is its largest elementary-derived quotient. In particular,

\[
|\Phi_2(U_3')|=|U_3|/|H_3|=2^{11-8}=2^3. \tag{1}
\]

### Four marked factors

For each \(i\in\{1,2,3,4\}\), put \(T_i=\{1,2,3,4\}\setminus\{i\}\), choose any bijection \(\sigma_i:T_i\to\{1,2,3\}\), and define

\[
Q_i=U_3\times C_2=U_3\times\langle z_i\rangle.
\]

Define a marked epimorphism \(\varphi_i:P_4\twoheadrightarrow Q_i\) by

\[
x_j\longmapsto (u_{\sigma_i(j)},1)\quad(j\in T_i),
\qquad x_i\longmapsto(1,z_i),
\]

where \(u_1,u_2,u_3\) are the canonical exact involutions of \(U_3\). These four images are exact involutions and generate \(Q_i\). The direct product \(Q_i\) is admissible because the all-involutions implication is checked coordinatewise. Its order is \(2^{11+1}=2^{12}\).

The largest elementary-derived quotient of \(Q_i\) is

\[
A_i=H_3\times C_2,qquad |A_i|=2^9,
\]

because \(Q_i'=U_3'\times1\) and

\[
L_i:=\ker(Q_i\twoheadrightarrow A_i)
     =\Phi_2(U_3')\times1
\]

has order \(2^3\) by (1). Since \(A_i\) is a marked elementary-derived quotient of \(P_4\), the restricted universality of \(H_4\) supplies a marked epimorphism \(\alpha_i:H_4\twoheadrightarrow A_i\). Write \(\beta_i:Q_i\twoheadrightarrow A_i\) for the quotient map.

### Full fiber product and its order

Form

\[
F=\{(h,q_1,q_2,q_3,q_4):
       \alpha_i(h)=\beta_i(q_i)\text{ for every }i\}
   \le H_4\times\prod_{i=1}^4Q_i.
\]

Projection \(F\to H_4\) is onto. Over each \(h\), the coordinate \(q_i\) has exactly \(|L_i|=2^3\) choices, independently. Therefore

\[
|F|=|H_4|\prod_{i=1}^4|L_i|
    =2^{21+4\cdot3}=2^{33}. \tag{2}
\]

The diagonal marked map \(\delta:P_4\to H_4\times\prod_iQ_i\) has image \(D\subseteq F\). It is immediate that \(D\) is generated by the four diagonal marked involutions and projects onto \(H_4\). The only nontrivial issue is whether \(D=F\).

### Kernel isolation, hence generation of the full fiber product

Fix \(i\) and \(\ell\in L_i\). The restriction of \(\varphi_i\) to

\[
P_{T_i}:=\langle x_j:j\in T_i\rangle\cong P_3
\]

maps onto \(U_3\times1\). Epimorphisms carry \(S^2[S,S]\) onto the corresponding subgroup of the image, so there is a word

\[
w\in\Phi_2(P_{T_i}')=(P_{T_i}')^2[P_{T_i}',P_{T_i}']
\]

with \(\varphi_i(w)=\ell\).

First, \(P_{T_i}'\le P_4'=K_4\), so

\[
\Phi_2(P_{T_i}')\le K_4^2[K_4,K_4]=N_4.
\]

Thus \(w\) maps to identity in \(H_4\).

Next fix \(k\ne i\). Among the three generators indexed by \(T_i\), the map \(\varphi_k\) sends \(x_k\) to the central factor \((1,z_k)\) and the other two to two canonical involutions in \(U_3\times1\). Hence \(\varphi_k(P_{T_i})\) is the direct product of \(C_2\) with a group generated by two involutions of \(U_3\). In an admissible group, two involutions generate a dihedral group of order dividing 8, because their product has order dividing 4. Its derived subgroup has order at most 2 and is therefore elementary abelian. Consequently

\[
\Phi_2(\varphi_k(P_{T_i})')=1.
\]

Since \(w\in\Phi_2(P_{T_i}')\), it follows that \(\varphi_k(w)=1\) for every \(k\ne i\).

Therefore \(\delta(w)\) is precisely the element \(\ell\) in the \(i\)-th kernel coordinate and identity in \(H_4\) and every other \(Q_k\). Hence \(D\) contains each coordinate subgroup \(L_i\) independently.

Finally, for any \(f\in F\), choose \(d\in D\) with the same \(H_4\)-coordinate (possible because \(D\to H_4\) is onto). Then \(fd^{-1}\in\prod_iL_i\), and every coordinate factor of this product lies in \(D\) by the isolation argument. Thus \(f\in D\), so

\[
D=F.
\]

Together with (2), the diagonal group \(D\) has exact order \(2^{33}\) and is generated by four exact involutions.

### Admissibility and scope status

Each factor \(H_4,Q_1,Q_2,Q_3,Q_4\) is admissible.
