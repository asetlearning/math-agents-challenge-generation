# Active target

Determine the order of the largest group \(G_4\) generated by four involutions and satisfying \((xy)^4=1\) for every pair of involutions \(x,y\in G_4\).

# Partial claim

The exact universal metabelianization is \(G_4/G_4''\cong H_4\) of order \(2^{21}\). In addition, \(G_4\) has an explicitly defined admissible quotient of exact order \(2^{33}\), and a further hand argument constructs an admissible diagonal quotient of order at least \(2^{34}\). Every finite quotient of \(G_4\) is a 2-group. Consequently, \(|G_4|\) is either countably infinite or \(2^n\) for some \(n\ge34\); in the finite case \(|G_4''|\ge2^{13}\).

This is **not** a claim to know \(|G_4|\), to prove \(G_4\) finite, or to identify the quotient with \(G_4\).

The subsequent QORBIT-CLOSURE experiment ended in `QORBIT_FAIL`: the proposed uniform closure \((d^h)^d\in d^H\) is false in an admissible four-marked image. This exhausts that finiteness strategy but does not change the exact-order status.

# Construction and proof outline

Let \(P_m\) be the free product of \(m\) copies of \(C_2\), let \(K_m=P_m'\), and set

\[
H_m=P_m/(K_m^2[K_m,K_m]).
\]

The index-\(2^m\) subgroup \(K_m\) is free of rank \(1+2^{m-1}(m-2)\). Thus \(|H_4|=2^{21}\) and \(|H_3|=2^8\). The derived subgroup of \(H_m\) is elementary abelian, so \(H_m\) has exponent at most 4 and is admissible. Moreover it is the largest marked quotient with elementary-abelian derived subgroup.

For the full universal object, \(G_4'\) is involution-generated, hence \((G_4')^2=G_4''\). Comparing kernels in \(P_4\) gives \(G_4''=(K_4^2[K_4,K_4])/R_4\), so \(G_4/G_4''\cong H_4\) exactly. This reduction does not assume that an arbitrary quotient remains admissible.

Independently, \(H_4'\) is the mod-2 cycle space of the 4-cube. Its augmentation filtration has dimensions \(6,8,3\), giving lower-central dimensions \(4\mid6\mid8\mid3\), exact class 4, and a 21-bit collected normal form.

Using the source fact \(|G_3|=2^{11}\), the kernel of the largest elementary-derived quotient \(G_3\twoheadrightarrow H_3\) has order \(2^3\).

For each three-subset \(T_i=\{1,2,3,4\}\setminus\{i\}\), take

\[
Q_i=G_3\times C_2,
\]

marking the three indices in \(T_i\) by the three canonical generators of \(G_3\) and marking the missing index by the generator of the central \(C_2\). Its largest elementary-derived quotient is \(A_i=H_3\times C_2\), with kernel \(L_i\) of order \(2^3\). Form

\[
F=H_4\times_{A_1}Q_1\times_{A_2}Q_2
       \times_{A_3}Q_3\times_{A_4}Q_4.
\]

Then \(|F|=2^{21}(2^3)^4=2^{33}\).

The key generation step is not assumed: for \(\ell\in L_i\), choose a preimage word

\[
w\in (P_{T_i}')^2[P_{T_i}',P_{T_i}'].
\]

It dies in \(H_4\). In every \(Q_k\) with \(k\ne i\), its three input generators map into a group generated by two involutions together with a central involution; that group's derived subgroup is elementary abelian, so \(w\) dies there too. In \(Q_i\), it realizes \(\ell\). Hence the four diagonal marked involutions isolate every coordinate kernel \(L_i\), and their diagonal image is the entire fiber product \(F\).

The full line-by-line proof is in `Agents/Kourovka/problems/20.50/runs/2026-08-16-r1-proof/log.md` under “Personal proof audit I” and “Personal proof audit II.”

The same log's “Personal strictness proof” shows \(U_3''\) has order \(2^3\). Hence two commutator generators of \(U_3'\) fail to commute. Marking their four involutory inputs gives an admissible subgroup \(Q\le U_3\) on which the four-support word \([[x_1,x_2],[x_3,x_4]]\) is nontrivial, whereas that word vanishes in \(D\). The diagonal image in \(D\times Q\) therefore has order at least \(2^{34}\).

# Universal upper-side reduction

For a normal subgroup \(N\triangleleft P_4\), set

\[
\mathcal T(N)=\langle\!\langle N\cup\{(uv)^4:u^2,v^2\in N\}\rangle\!\rangle,
\]

and define \(N_0=1\), \(N_{r+1}=\mathcal T(N_r)\), \(N_\omega=\bigcup_rN_r\). A direct fixed-point argument gives the exact universal kernel

\[
R_4=N_\omega,
\qquad G_4=P_4/N_\omega.
\]

If \(G_4\) is finite, \(N_\omega\) is finite-index and finitely generated, so a finite generating set is contained in one stage and the Horn chain must stabilize there. Conversely, a stabilized stage is already the universal fixed point; a finite normal form for it would certify the exact order. No finite stage is shown to stabilize in this run.

For every arbitrary admissible \(G=\langle a,b,c,d\rangle\), the three-generator subgroup \(H=\langle a,b,c\rangle\) has order at most \(2^{11}\), and the normal closure of \(d\) is generated by the finite orbit \(\{d^h:h\in H\}\). Its abelianization is finite elementary abelian. Thus any infinite target would require a finitely generated nonsolvable normal closure with an infinite derived descent, every successive derived quotient being finite elementary abelian.

The final hand-only upper-side block gives a strict centralizer refinement. If \(N\) is that normal closure and \(q_r=|G/N^{(r)}|\), then every \(q_r\) is a finite power of two and

\[
[N^{(r)},{}_{q_r}G]\le N^{(r+1)}.
\]

In particular, \(q_0\le2^{11}\) gives \([N,{}_{2048}G]\le N'\). The proof views \(N^{(r)}/N^{(r+1)}\) as an \(\mathbf F_2[G/N^{(r)}]\)-module and uses the hand-proved bound \(I^{|P|}=0\) for the augmentation ideal of any finite 2-group \(P\). Hence every finite derived truncation has a finite central refinement. The unresolved gap is nonuniformity: \(q_r\) may grow with \(r\), so this does not force a terminal derived term, finiteness, solvability, or nilpotence of \(G\).

Validator's corrected verdict passes the personal Lie-style argument \(U_3'''=1\), \(U_3''\cong C_2^3\), and its stated downstream bounds. Hence the existing exact fiber quotient has \(D''\cong C_2^{12}\), and the strictness quotient gives a further independent second-derived bit. In particular,

\[
13\le\dim_{\mathbf F_2}(G_4''/G_4''')\le983041,
\]

where the upper bound comes from the largest elementary-derived group on the 17 involutions generating \(G_4'/G_4'''\). Equivalently, \(|G_4/G_4'''|=2^m\) for some \(34\le m\le983062\). This reviewed finite-truncation statement does not imply \(G_4'''=1\).

# QORBIT-CLOSURE failure certificate

In the canonical admissible \(U_3=\langle d,e,f\rangle\), put

\[
\alpha=[d,e],\qquad \beta=[d,f],\qquad z=[\alpha,\beta].
\]

The personal class-four argument and the corrected Validator verdict give \(U_3''\cong C_2^3\). The three basic commutators of \([d,e],[d,f],[e,f]\) generate \(U_3''\), so \(z\ne1\). Pair-centrality and polarization give the exact universal identity

\[
(d^{ef})^d=d^{ef}z.
\]

In \(U_3/U_3''=H_3\), the cube-cycle model proves that \(\langle\bar e,\bar f\rangle\cong D_8\) has trivial stabilizer on \(\bar d\). The pair subgroup \(\langle e,f\rangle\) embeds in this quotient. Therefore an equality \(d^h=d^{ef}z\) with \(h\in\langle e,f\rangle\) would force \(h=ef\), and then force \(z=1\), a contradiction.

Mark \(U_3\) by the four distinct exact generating involutions

\[
(a,b,c,d)=(e,f,[e,f],d).
\]

Then \(H=\langle a,b,c\rangle=\langle e,f\rangle\), and the explicit word \(h=ab=ef\) satisfies

\[
(d^h)^d\notin d^H.
\]

Thus no uniform generator-extension rewrite exists. Had orbit closure held uniformly, the four conjugacy-class bounds would give \([G:Z(G)]\le2^{44}\); Schur's theorem and \(|G/G'|\le16\) would then imply finiteness. The counterexample invalidates that antecedent, so it supplies no finite upper bound and no exact order.

# What this does not establish

- No finite upper bound for \(G_4\).
- No proof that \(G_4\) is finite, solvable, or nilpotent.
- No factorization of every admissible marked group through the finite quotient \(F\).
- No equality \(F\cong G_4\).
- No proof that \(G_4'''=1\); the reviewed finite-truncation bounds do not terminate the derived series.
- No finiteness conclusion from QORBIT: the required orbit-closure antecedent is false.

# How this could be wrong

1. The rank computation for \(P_m'\) could misuse graph-of-groups Euler characteristic; Validator should reconstruct the index-cover calculation.
2. The assertion that \(H_3\) is the largest elementary-derived quotient of \(G_3\) depends on the kernel inclusion \(R_3\subseteq K_3^2[K_3,K_3]\); Validator should check the quotient orientation.
3. The fiber product could be larger than the diagonal image if the kernel-isolation word did not vanish in every off-target factor; the proof reduces this to the elementary derived subgroup of every two-involution group.
4. Product/subgroup inheritance could be misapplied if “involution” were not interpreted coordinatewise; the proof explicitly uses that a subgroup involution is also an involution in the ambient direct product.
5. The Horn fixed-point proof could reverse the kernel inclusions; Validator should check both induction into every admissible kernel and admissibility of the union quotient.
6. The second-derived refinements and QORBIT separator use the personal graded proof that the finite \(U_3\) has class at most 4 and central \(U_3''\cong C_2^3\). Validator's corrected verdict passes it, but an independent reconstruction should still check the degree-five vanishing and the cube-cycle stabilizer argument. (The basic \(2^{34}\) strictness bound needs only \(|U_3''|=2^3\), not centrality.)
