### 2. The four marks are exact, distinct, and generating

Let \(U=U_3=\langle d,e,f\rangle\), and put

\[
a=e,\qquad b=f,\qquad c=[e,f]=(ef)^2.
\]

The canonical generators \(d,e,f\) are exact involutions. Their images form the three distinct nonzero basis vectors in the abelian quotient \(C_2^3\), so they are pairwise distinct.

The exact metabelian quotient

\[
H_3=P_3/(K_3^2[K_3,K_3])
\]

has derived subgroup equal to the mod-two cycle space of the 3-cube. The word \((ef)^2\) traces the nonzero \(e\)-\(f\) square. Hence the image of \(ef\) in \(H_3\) has order four. Since \((ef)^4=1\) already in \(U_3\), \(ef\) has exact order four in \(U_3\), and \(c=(ef)^2\) is a nonidentity involution.

The element \(c\) has zero abelianization, whereas \(d,e,f\) have distinct nonzero abelianizations. Therefore \(c\) is distinct from each of them. Finally, the displayed four marks generate because \(d,e,f\) are among them. Thus they are four exact, pairwise distinct, generating involutions. Also

\[
\langle a,b,c\rangle=\langle e,f\rangle=:B.
\]

### 3. The reviewed \(U_3''\) result forces a nonzero correction

The permitted prerequisite note established by hand

\[
\gamma_5(U)=1,qquad U''\cong C_2^3.
\]

Set

\[
\alpha=[d,e],\qquad \beta=[d,f],\qquad \gamma=[e,f].
\]

The derived subgroup \(U'\) is normally generated by \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\). A conjugate of one of these weight-two elements differs from it by an element of \(\gamma_3(U)\). In a commutator of two such conjugates, every correction lies in

\[
[\gamma_3,\gamma_2]\,[\gamma_3,\gamma_3]
\le \gamma_5\gamma_6=1.
\]

Consequently

\[
U''=\langle[\alpha,\beta],[\alpha,\gamma],[\beta,\gamma]\rangle. \tag{1}
\]

Each displayed generator has order at most two: \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\) are themselves involutions or identity, and the all-involutions law makes the commutator of two nontrivial ones an involution or identity. The group in (1) has exact order eight and is elementary abelian. Three elements of order at most two cannot generate it if one is trivial or dependent. Hence all three are nonzero and independent. In particular,

\[
z:=[\alpha,\beta]=[[d,e],[d,f]]\ne1. \tag{2}
\]

This inference uses no quarantined probe.

### 4. Polarization, collection, and the sign

Use

\[
[x,y]=x^{-1}y^{-1}xy,\qquad x^g=g^{-1}xg.
\]

First, if commuting involutions \(u,v\) and an involution \(t\) lie in an admissible group, then

\[
[[u,t],[v,t]]=1. \tag{3}
\]

Indeed, \(uv\) is an involution (or identity), and

\[
[uv,t]=[u,t]^v[v,t]
\]

is an involution (or identity). Its two displayed factors are involutions (or identity), so they commute. Conjugating their commutation relation by \(v\), which centralizes \([v,t]\), gives (3). This is the polarization step.

Put

\[
p=d^e,\qquad q=d^f,\qquad r=d^{ef}=p^f.
\]

Pair centrality gives \([d,p]=[d,q]=[p,r]=1\), and conjugating \([d,p]=1\) by \(f\) gives \([q,r]=1\). Applying (3) to the commuting pair \(d,p\) and the involution \(f\) yields

\[
[[d,f],[p,f]]=1.
\]

Since \([d,f]=dq\) and \([p,f]=pr\), this is

\[
dqpr=prdq.
\]

Using only the four commutations just listed gives

\[
qp\,dr=pq\,rd,
\]

or, with \(A=pq\) and \(B=dr\),

\[
A^{-1}B=AB^{-1}.
\]

Multiplication on the left by \(A\) and on the right by \(B\) gives

\[
(dr)^2=(pq)^2. \tag{4}
\]

Now \(p=d\alpha\) and \(q=d\beta\), while \(d\) centralizes \(\alpha,\beta\). Hence \(pq=\alpha\beta\), so (4) becomes

\[
[d,r]=(dr)^2=(\alpha\beta)^2=[\alpha,\beta]=z. \tag{5}
\]

For the stated commutator convention,

\[
r^d=[d,r]r=zr.
\]

The reviewed class-four result puts \(z\in U''\le\gamma_4(U)\le Z(U)\), so \(zr=rz\). Therefore the exact identity, including its side/sign, is

\[
(d^{ef})^d=d^{ef}[[d,e],[d,f]]. \tag{6}
\]

### 5. Cube-cycle centralizer and orbit separation

The prerequisite identifies

\[
\overline U:=U/U''=H_3,
\qquad
\overline U'=K_3/(K_3^2[K_3,K_3])
   \cong H_1(Q_3,\mathbf F_2),
\]

where \(Q_3\) is the 3-cube. A closed kernel word maps to the mod-two edge chain traced by its cube path; a nonzero cycle chain is therefore a nonzero element.

Let \(\overline B=\langle\bar e,\bar f\rangle\). The nonzero \(e\)-\(f\) square represented by \((\bar e\bar f)^2\) shows that \(\bar e\bar f\) has order four. The relation \((ef)^4=1\) makes any two-involution subgroup a quotient of \(D_8\), so

\[
\overline B\cong D_8. \tag{7}
\]

Now take \(1\ne\bar h\in\overline B\), and let \(v\in\langle e,f\rangle_{\mathbf F_2}\) be its abelianization. The cube path of

\[
[\bar d,\bar h]=\bar d\bar h^{-1}\bar d\bar h
\]

contains two \(d\)-edges. If \(v\ne0\), they are the distinct edges

\[
\{0,d\},\qquad\{v,d+v\}.
\]

The two \(h\)-segments use only \(e\)- and \(f\)-edges, so neither \(d\)-edge can cancel. The cycle is nonzero.

If \(v=0\), the only nonidentity possibility in \(D_8\) is

\[
\bar h=(\bar e\bar f)^2.
\]

The two \(d\)-edges then cancel, but the remaining chain is the \(e\)-\(f\) square in the \(d\)-layer plus the parallel square in the zero layer. Their edge sets are disjoint, so their sum is nonzero. Thus

\[
C_{\overline B}(\bar d)=1. \tag{8}
\]

The orbit map \(\bar h\mapsto\bar d^{\bar h}\) is consequently injective. Also \(B=\langle e,f\rangle\) has order at most eight, whereas its image \(\overline B\) has order eight by (7). Hence \(B\to\overline B\) is an isomorphism.

Suppose that the value in (6) were \(d^h\) for some \(h\in B\). Modulo \(U''\), where \(z\) dies, one would have

\[
\bar d^{\bar h}=\bar d^{\overline{ef}}.
\]

Injectivity and the embedding of \(B\) force \(h=ef\). The original equality would then read \(rz=r\), contrary to (2). Therefore

\[
(d^{ef})^d\notin d^B. \tag{9}
\]

### 6. Descent from the uniform \(U_4\) assertion

The tuple

\[
(a,b,c,d)=(e,f,[e,f],d)
\]

consists of four exact distinct involutions, generates the independently admissible group \(U_3\), and has \(\langle a,b,c\rangle=B\). By the universal marked property there is a marked epimorphism

\[
\pi:U_4\twoheadrightarrow U_3.
\]

If (Q) held in \(U_4\), then at \(h=ab\) there would be some \(k\in\langle a,b,c\rangle\) such that

\[
(d^{ab})^d=d^k.
\]

Applying \(\pi\) would put \((d^{ef})^d\) in \(d^B\), contradicting (9). Thus the uniform QORBIT-CLOSURE assertion itself is false in \(U_4\). This descent does not assume that arbitrary quotients preserve the semantic law; the particular image was checked independently to be the source-defined \(U_3\).
