# Verdict

**PASS as a PARTIAL_RESULT only.** The fibre product \(D\) has exact order \(2^{33}\), is generated by four specified elements of exact order \(2\), and satisfies the law for all involutions. The strictness construction gives a further admissible four-generated quotient \(E\) with \(|E|\ge2^{34}\). Consequently,

\[
G_4\text{ finite}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 2^{34}\mid |G_4|.
\]

No exact value for \(|E|\) is proved, and neither construction is a universality certificate or an upper bound for \(G_4\).

# 1. Free-derived ranks and elementary-derived quotients

For

\[
P_m=C_2*\cdots*C_2,\qquad K_m=P_m',
\]

the graph-of-groups Euler characteristic is

\[
\chi(P_m)=1-\frac m2.
\]

The subgroup \(K_m\) has index \(2^m\). It is torsion-free because every nonidentity torsion element of a free product is conjugate into a factor and retains a nonzero abelianization coordinate. Hence \(K_m\) is free, and

\[
\operatorname{rank}K_m
=1-\chi(K_m)
=1-2^m\left(1-\frac m2\right)
=1+2^{m-1}(m-2).
\]

Thus \(\operatorname{rank}K_4=17\) and \(\operatorname{rank}K_3=5\). With

\[
N_m=K_m^2[K_m,K_m],\qquad H_m=P_m/N_m,
\]

one has \(K_m/N_m\cong C_2^{\operatorname{rank}K_m}\). Therefore

\[
|H_4|=2^{17+4}=2^{21},\qquad |H_3|=2^{5+3}=2^8.
\]

The derived subgroup of \(H_m\) is \(K_m/N_m\), so it is elementary abelian; \(H_m/H_m'\cong C_2^m\), hence every element of \(H_m\) has fourth power \(1\). The marked generators remain exact involutions by projection to \(C_2^m\). Thus these are admissible groups, and \(H_m\) is exactly the largest marked quotient in the restricted elementary-derived subfamily.

# 2. The \(G_3\) kernel and its orientation

Write the source universal three-generator group as \(U_3=P_3/R_3\), with source-given order \(2^{11}\). Since \(H_3=P_3/N_3\) is admissible, universality supplies \(U_3\twoheadrightarrow H_3\). The kernel orientation is therefore

\[
R_3\subseteq N_3\subseteq K_3,
\]

not the reverse. Consequently

\[
U_3'=K_3/R_3,\qquad
\Phi_2(U_3')=(U_3')^2[U_3',U_3']=N_3/R_3,
\]

and

\[
U_3/\Phi_2(U_3')\cong P_3/N_3=H_3.
\]

The exact source order now gives

\[
|\Phi_2(U_3')|=\frac{2^{11}}{2^8}=2^3.
\]

# 3. All four fibre kernels and the order of \(F\)

For \(T_i=\{1,2,3,4\}\setminus\{i\}\), let

\[
Q_i=U_3\times C_2.
\]

Mark the variables in \(T_i\) by the three canonical generators of \(U_3\), and mark \(x_i\) by the central \(C_2\)-generator. These four exact involutions generate \(Q_i\). Its largest elementary-derived quotient is

\[
A_i=H_3\times C_2,
\]

and the coordinate kernel

\[
L_i=\Phi_2(U_3')\times1
\]

has order \(2^3\), for each of the four values of \(i\).

Restricted universality gives compatible marked epimorphisms \(H_4\twoheadrightarrow A_i\). In the simultaneous fibre product

\[
F=H_4\times_{A_1}Q_1\times_{A_2}Q_2
\times_{A_3}Q_3\times_{A_4}Q_4,
\]

projection to \(H_4\) is onto and every fibre has \(\prod_i|L_i|\) elements. Thus

\[
|F|=2^{21}(2^3)^4=2^{33}.
\]

# 4. Kernel-isolating words and diagonal generation

Fix \(i\) and \(\ell\in L_i\). The restriction \(P_{T_i}\to U_3\times1\) is onto, so verbal-subgroup functoriality supplies

\[
w\in\Phi_2(P_{T_i}')=(P_{T_i}')^2[P_{T_i}',P_{T_i}']
\]

mapping to \(\ell\) in \(Q_i\). Since \(P_{T_i}'\le K_4\), this word lies in \(N_4\) and dies in \(H_4\).

For \(k\ne i\), the same three variables map in \(Q_k\) to one central involution and two involutions generating a quotient of \(D_8\). That image has elementary-abelian derived subgroup, so its \(\Phi_2\) of the derived subgroup is trivial. Hence \(w\) dies in every off-target \(Q_k\).

The diagonal image \(D\) of \(P_4\) therefore contains each \(L_i\) as an independently isolated coordinate subgroup. It also surjects onto \(H_4\). Given \(f\in F\), cancel its \(H_4\)-coordinate using an element of \(D\); the remainder belongs to \(\prod_iL_i\), which lies in \(D\). Hence

\[
D=F,\qquad |D|=2^{33}.
\]

The four diagonal generators have order exactly \(2\), as is already witnessed by their nontrivial images in \(H_4\).

# 5. The all-involutions law

Every factor \(H_4,Q_1,\ldots,Q_4\) is admissible. The law is inherited by direct products and subgroups. If a coordinate of an ambient involution is the identity rather than an exact involution, the fourth-power conclusion in that coordinate is immediate; otherwise it is the factor's defining law. Thus for all involutions \(x,y\in D\),

\[
(xy)^4=1.
\]

This checks the source's all-involutions quantifier, not merely the pairs of marked generators.

# 6. Strictness and the \(2^{34}\) bound

If a group \(Y\) is generated by involutions, then \(Y^2=Y'\): its abelianization has exponent \(2\), while the quotient by the square verbal subgroup has exponent \(2\) and is therefore abelian. The group \(U_3'\) is generated by conjugates of basic commutators of the canonical involutions; each such commutator is itself an involution under the source law. Hence

\[
\Phi_2(U_3')=(U_3')^2[U_3',U_3']=U_3''.
\]

Section 2 gives \(|U_3''|=2^3\), so \(U_3'\) is nonabelian. Two members \(p,q\) of its involutory generating set must therefore fail to commute. Write

\[
p=[s_1,s_2],\qquad q=[s_3,s_4]
\]

with all \(s_i\) involutions in \(U_3\), and put \(Q=\langle s_1,s_2,s_3,s_4\rangle\). The four-support word

\[
z=[[x_1,x_2],[x_3,x_4]]
\]

maps to \([p,q]\ne1\) in \(Q\). It maps to \(1\) in \(D\): it lies in \([P_4',P_4']\le N_4\), and in each \(Q_i\) one of the two inner commutators has a central input.

Let \(E\) be the diagonal image in \(D\times Q\). It is generated by the four marked diagonal involutions, which are exact by projection to \(D\), and it is admissible by product/subgroup inheritance. Projection \(E\twoheadrightarrow D\) has the nonidentity element \((1,[p,q])\) in its kernel. Since all groups here are finite \(2\)-groups,

\[
|E|\ge2|D|=2^{34}.
\]

This proves strictness of \(D\) and the conditional divisibility bound, but not equality \(|E|=2^{34}\).

# 7. Subsidiary centrality check

The optional log refinement \(U_3''\cong C_2^3\le Z(U_3)\) is also consistent. The lower-central graded argument has elementary-abelian factors because they are generated by simple commutators that are involutions. The polarization identity gives \([L_3,L_2]=0\); Jacobi and the sandwich relations then give \(L_5=0\). Finiteness and nilpotence force \(\gamma_5(U_3)=1\), hence \(U_3''\le\gamma_4(U_3)\le Z(U_3)\). Being involution-generated and central, the order-eight subgroup is elementary abelian.

This subsidiary fact is not needed for the \(2^{34}\) lower bound. Even with it, the kernel over \(D\) is only bounded between orders \(2\) and \(2^3\), so the current proof gives \(2^{34}\le|E|\le2^{36}\), not an exact order.

# Scope boundary

The constructions certify finite admissible quotients of the universal object. They provide no reverse factorization, no finite upper bound, and no proof that \(G_4\) is finite. The universal-object and determine-order rows remain open.
