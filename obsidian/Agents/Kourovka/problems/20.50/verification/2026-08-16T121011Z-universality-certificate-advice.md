# Answer

A terminating power-commutator normal form, an independently built same-order model, and exhaustive involution classification are **not sufficient by themselves**. They can prove that a particular finite presented group (P) is internally admissible and has the claimed order. Universality additionally requires a factorization certificate showing that every admissible four-involution-generated group is a quotient of (P).

## Precise sufficient package

Let (F=C_2*C_2*C_2*C_2) with marked generators (a_1,\dots,a_4), and let (\mathcal C) be the marked quotients generated by the four involution images and satisfying ((xy)^4=1) for every involution pair in the whole group. A finite candidate (P=F/R) is certified as (G_4) if all of the following are proved:

1. **Upper bound:** a complete, terminating and confluent rewriting/consistent power-commutator certificate gives at most (N) normal forms for (P).
2. **Lower bound and presentation fidelity:** an independently reconstructed group (M) of order (N) is generated by four exact involutions satisfying every defining relator of (P). The resulting epimorphism (P\twoheadrightarrow M) then proves (P\cong M) and (|P|=N).
3. **Internal admissibility:** the normal forms give a complete classification of all involutions of (P), and every pair in that complete list satisfies ((xy)^4=1). The four marked generators retain exact order 2 and generate (P).
4. **Universal factorization:** every defining relator in (R) is proved, in an arbitrary marked (H\in\mathcal C), from the all-involutions condition. Equivalently, for every admissible generating tuple there is an epimorphism (P\twoheadrightarrow H) sending (a_i) to that tuple. Together with (P\in\mathcal C), this gives both kernel containments against the intersection of kernels of all admissible marked quotients and proves (P) is the largest object.

Each rewrite rule used for item 4 needs a derivation valid in arbitrary admissible (H). If the derivation invokes the involution law on words (u,v), it must first prove that their values are involutions in every such (H), not merely in the candidate (P).

## Pitfalls

- Exhausting involutions of (P) proves only (P\in\mathcal C); it does not prove that (P) maps onto every member of (\mathcal C).
- The all-involutions condition is conditional and need not be inherited by arbitrary quotients: a quotient can acquire new involutions. Generator-pair relators or candidate-specific involutions therefore do not define the universal object automatically.
- A same-order permutation model validates the presented group only when the direction and surjectivity of the presentation map are explicit.
- A finite quotient, p-quotient, or consistent pc presentation remains only a lower-bound model unless the universal-factorization item is supplied.

With all four items and independently checkable transcripts, the certificate is sufficient in principle. No candidate exists yet, so this is certificate design rather than a verdict.
