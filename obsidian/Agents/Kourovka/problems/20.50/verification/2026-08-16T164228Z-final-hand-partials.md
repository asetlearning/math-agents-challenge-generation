## 1. Independent attack on the (U_3) premise

### 1.1 Exact identities and the graded algebra

For involutions (x,y) in an admissible group,

\[
[x,y]=(xy)^2,
\qquad [x,y]^2=1,
\qquad [[x,y],x]=[[x,y],y]=1. \tag{1}
\]

If involutions (u,v) commute and (z) is an involution, then

\[
[[u,z],[v,z]]=1. \tag{2}
\]

Indeed, ([uv,z]=[u,z]^v[v,z]) is an involution. Its two displayed factors are involutions, so they commute; conjugating by (v), which centralizes ([v,z]), gives (2).

Every simple commutator in the three canonical involutions is therefore an involution or identity. The lower-central factors of (U_3) are generated by such commutators, hence have exponent two. Thus

\[
L=\bigoplus_{n\ge1}\gamma_n(U_3)/\gamma_{n+1}(U_3)
\]

is a graded Lie algebra over (\mathbf F_2).

Write its degree-one generators as (a,b,c), and put

\[
p=[a,b],\quad q=[a,c],\quad r=[b,c],
\]

\[
A=[p,c],\quad B=[q,b],\quad C=[r,a].
\]

Repeated-letter triples vanish by (1), so (A,B,C) span (L_3). Graded Jacobi gives

\[
A+B+C=0. \tag{3}
\]

This is used only in (L_3); no ungraded product identity is assumed.

The element (A=[p,c]) centralizes (p,c) by (1). Since (p) centralizes (a,b), equation (2) also makes (A) centralize (q=[a,c]) and (r=[b,c]). Cyclically, (B) centralizes (p,q,r,b), and (C) centralizes (p,q,r,a). Hence

\[
[L_3,L_2]=0. \tag{4}
\]

Jacobi applied to (L_2,L_2,L_1), together with (4), also gives

\[
[[L_2,L_2],L_1]=0. \tag{5}
\]

### 1.2 Degree-five vanishing

Since (L) is generated by (L_1), (L_5) is spanned by

\[
[T,d,e]=[[T,d],e],
\qquad T\in L_3, d,e\in L_1.
\]

Jacobi and (4) make this expression symmetric in (d,e). For each of the spanning elements (T=A,B,C), exact pair centrality gives ([T,d,d]=0); bilinearity extends this to all (T\in L_3).

Because (A) centralizes (c), only ([A,a,b]) can remain. Similarly the only candidates from (B,C) are ([B,a,c]) and ([C,b,c]). Bracket (3) successively by the pairs ((a,b)), ((a,c)), and ((b,c)). In each equation the two unwanted terms vanish by the listed centralizers and symmetry, so the remaining candidate vanishes. Therefore

\[
L_5=0,
\qquad \gamma_5(U_3)=\gamma_6(U_3). \tag{6}
\]

The source fact |(U_3)|=(2^{11}) makes (U_3) a finite 2-group and hence nilpotent. Equality at (6) would persist at all subsequent lower-central terms, so nilpotence forces

\[
\gamma_5(U_3)=1. \tag{7}
\]

Thus (U_3) has class at most four.

### 1.3 Exact structure of (U_3'')

Let (P_3=C_2*C_2*C_2), (K_3=P_3'), and (N_3=K_3^2[K_3,K_3]). The index-eight subgroup (K_3) is free of rank

\[
1+2^{3-1}(3-2)=5.
\]

Therefore (H_3=P_3/N_3) has order (2^{3+5}=2^8). Its derived subgroup is elementary abelian, so it has exponent at most four and is admissible. If (U_3=P_3/R_3), universality gives (R_3\subseteq N_3) and

\[
U_3/\Phi_2(U_3')\cong H_3.
\]

Consequently

\[
|\Phi_2(U_3')|=2^{11-8}=2^3. \tag{8}
\]

The subgroup (U_3') is generated by conjugates of basic commutators of the canonical involutions; these generators are involutions by (1). For every involution-generated group (Y), (Y^2=Y'): the abelianization has exponent two, and the quotient by the square subgroup has exponent two and is abelian. Hence

\[
\Phi_2(U_3')=(U_3')^2[U_3',U_3']=U_3''. \tag{9}
\]

Equations (7)–(9) put (U_3''\le\gamma_4(U_3)\le Z(U_3)) and give |(U_3'')|=(2^3). The subgroup (U_3'') is itself involution-generated, so centrality makes it elementary abelian. Therefore

\[
U_3''\cong C_2^3,
\qquad U_3'''=1. \tag{10}
\]

The submitted rank-three premise passes. Its proof does not rely on any quarantined probe.

## 2. Independent reconstruction of the centralizer descent

Let (G=\langle a,b,c,d\rangle) be arbitrary in the active class, let (H=\langle a,b,c\rangle), and let (N=\langle d^G\rangle). Since (H) is a quotient of (U_3),

\[
|H|\le2^{11}.
\]

As (G=HN), one has (G/N\cong H/(H\cap N)). Moreover

\[
N=\langle d^h:h\in H\rangle,
\]

so (N) is generated by at most (2^{11}) involutions. Thus (N/N') is finite elementary abelian. Schreier induction and involution-generation of every derived term show that each

\[
N^{(r)}/N^{(r+1)}
\]

is finite elementary abelian. It follows that

\[
P_r:=G/N^{(r)}
\]

is a finite 2-group for every finite (r): its normal subgroup (N/N^{(r)}) and quotient (G/N) both have 2-power order. Put (q_r=|P_r|).

### 2.1 Augmentation-ideal lemma

For every finite 2-group (P), if (I) is the augmentation ideal of (\mathbf F_2[P]), then

\[
I^{|P|}=0. \tag{11}
\]

This has a direct induction. For nontrivial (P), choose a central involution (z). Modulo the central ideal ((z-1)\mathbf F_2[P]), the group algebra is (\mathbf F_2[P/\langle z\rangle]). Induction gives

\[
I^{|P|/2}\subseteq(z-1)\mathbf F_2[P].
\]

The ideal on the right is square-zero because (z) is central and ((z-1)^2=0) in characteristic two. Squaring the inclusion proves (11).

### 2.2 Action factorization and commutators

Set

\[
A_r=N^{(r)}/N^{(r+1)}.
\]

Conjugation makes (A_r) an (\mathbf F_2[G])-module. Every element of (N^{(r)}) acts trivially because

\[
[N^{(r)},N^{(r)}]=N^{(r+1)}.
\]

Hence the action factors through the finite 2-group (P_r=G/N^{(r)}). Modulo (N^{(r+1)}), successive group commutators correspond to successive multiplication by augmentation elements. Thus (11) gives

\[
[N^{(r)},{}_{q_r}G]\le N^{(r+1)}. \tag{12}
\]

For (r=0),

\[
q_0=|G/N|\le |H|\le2048.
\]

Once the (q_0)-th augmentation power is zero, every higher power is zero as well. Therefore

\[
[N,{}_{2048}G]\le N'. \tag{13}
\]

Both containments pass exactly as stated.

## 3. Downstream partials that now survive

The independently established (10) removes the former conditional label from the following bounded consequences:

1. In the previously checked exact fibre quotient (D), the four coordinate kernels are (L_i=U_3''\cong C_2^3). Since (D/L\cong H_4) is metabelian, (D''\le L=\prod_iL_i). Conversely, a preimage in (P_{T_i}'') isolates every element of (L_i) and lies in (P_4''); it dies in the off-target two-involution factors. Hence
   
   \[
   D''=L\cong C_2^{12},
   \qquad D'''=1.
   \]

2. For the strict diagonal quotient (E\le D\times Q), the earlier four-support word gives a nonidentity element in the kernel of (E''\twoheadrightarrow D''). Since (Q\le U_3), equation (10) gives (Q'''=1), and hence
   
   \[
   E'''=1,
   \qquad |E''|\ge2^{13}.
   \]

   The kernel of (E\twoheadrightarrow D) lies in (Q''\le U_3''), so the exact order of (E) is still unknown but is bounded by
   
   \[
   2^{34}\le|E|\le2^{36}.
   \]

3. The quotient (G_4'/G_4''') is generated by 17 involutions modulo its Frattini subgroup and is a quotient of the elementary-derived group (H_{17}). Therefore
   
   \[
   \dim_{\mathbf F_2}(G_4''/G_4''')\le
   1+2^{16}(17-2)=983041.
   \]

   The quotient (E) supplies the lower bound 13. Thus
   
   \[
   13\le\dim_{\mathbf F_2}(G_4''/G_4''')\le983041,
   \]

   and
   
   \[
   |G_4/G_4'''|=2^m
   \quad\text{for some }34\le m\le983062.
   \]

These are finite derived-truncation statements. They do not assert (G_4'''=1).

## Why the active assignment remains unanswered

The augmentation bound (12) uses (q_r=|G/N^{(r)}|), which can grow with (r). There is no (r)-independent Loewy length, no terminal derived layer, and no proof that (N), (G), or (G_4) is solvable, nilpotent, or finite. The rank-three conclusion (10) does not propagate to arbitrary four-support commutators.

The groups (D) and (E) remain finite admissible quotients. No reverse factorization identifies either with the largest/universal object. The exact order of (E) is not known, and no finite upper bound or exact value for |(G_4)| is established.
