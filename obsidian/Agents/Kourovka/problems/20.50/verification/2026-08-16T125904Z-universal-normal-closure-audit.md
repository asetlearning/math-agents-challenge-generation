# Verdict

**PASS as a universal structural PARTIAL_RESULT.** The normal-closure reduction and

\[
G_4/G_4''\cong H_4,\qquad [G_4:G_4'']=2^{21}
\]

are exact. The index statement remains meaningful when \(G_4\) and \(G_4''\) are infinite; the proof does not establish their finiteness or determine \(|G_4|\).

# Finite normal-closure reduction

Let \(G=\langle a,b,c,d\rangle\) be any admissible target and \(H=\langle a,b,c\rangle\). The subgroup \(H\) is admissible and three-involution generated, so it is a quotient of the source universal \(G_3\); hence

\[
|H|\le2^{11}.
\]

Set

\[
N=\langle d^G\rangle,\qquad M=\langle d^h:h\in H\rangle.
\]

The group \(H\) normalizes \(M\). Also \(d\in M\), so conjugation by \(d\) normalizes \(M\). Since \(G=\langle H,d\rangle\), \(M\triangleleft G\); it contains \(d\), while all its generators lie in \(N\). Thus

\[
N=M=\langle d^h:h\in H\rangle,
\]

and \(N\) is generated by at most \(2^{11}\) involutions.

Its abelianization is therefore elementary abelian of rank at most \(|H|\):

\[
|N/N'|\le2^{|H|}\le2^{2048}.
\]

Moreover \(G=HN\), so \(G/N\cong H/(H\cap N)\) is finite. Hence \(G\) is finite exactly when \(N\) is finite.

If a group is generated by involutions, its derived subgroup is generated by conjugates of commutators of those involutions; every such commutator is an involution under the source law. Inductively every derived term \(N^{(r)}\) is involution-generated. Since \(N/N'\) is finite, Schreier makes \(N'\) finitely generated. Its abelianization is finitely generated of exponent \(2\), hence finite elementary abelian. Iterating proves that every

\[
N^{(r)}/N^{(r+1)}
\]

is finite elementary abelian. Therefore

\[
N\text{ solvable}\Longrightarrow N\text{ finite}
\Longrightarrow G\text{ finite}.
\]

The converse \(G\) finite implies \(N\) finite is immediate. The argument does not prove solvability and explicitly leaves open an infinite nonsolvable derived descent.

# Exact universal metabelianization

Write

\[
G_4=P_4/R_4,\qquad K_4=P_4',\qquad
N_4=K_4^2[K_4,K_4].
\]

The already checked \(H_4=P_4/N_4\) is admissible. By the universal-kernel definition, its marked quotient forces

\[
R_4\subseteq N_4\subseteq K_4.
\]

Consequently

\[
G_4'=K_4/R_4.
\]

For any group \(Y\) generated by involutions,

\[
Y^2=Y'.
\]

Indeed \(Y/Y'\) is generated by elements of order at most \(2\), so \(Y^2\le Y'\); and \(Y/Y^2\) has exponent \(2\), hence is abelian, so \(Y'\le Y^2\).

The subgroup \(G_4'\) is generated by conjugates of the basic commutators of the four canonical involutions. Those commutators are themselves involutions because

\[
[x_i,x_j]=(x_ix_j)^2,\qquad (x_ix_j)^4=1.
\]

Applying the lemma to \(Y=G_4'\) gives

\[
(G_4')^2=(G_4')'=G_4''.
\]

Passing the square and commutator verbal subgroups through \(K_4/R_4\) now gives

\[
G_4''
=(G_4')^2[G_4',G_4']
=\frac{K_4^2[K_4,K_4]}{R_4}
=N_4/R_4.
\]

Therefore

\[
G_4/G_4''\cong(P_4/R_4)/(N_4/R_4)
\cong P_4/N_4=H_4.
\]

Since the independently checked free-derived rank calculation gives \(|H_4|=2^{21}\), the index is exactly \(2^{21}\). This computation does not require \(G_4\) to be finite and does not assume that arbitrary quotients of admissible groups remain admissible.

# Consequences and boundary

The finite quotient \(E\) of order at least \(2^{34}\) yields, conditionally on finite \(G_4\),

\[
|G_4''|=\frac{|G_4|}{2^{21}}\ge2^{13}.
\]

Also \(G_4''\) is finite-index in the four-generated \(G_4\), so it is finitely generated even if infinite; the involution-generation induction shows it is involution-generated. Proving this subgroup finite remains precisely the missing upper-bound step. No finiteness, exact-order, solvability, or nilpotency conclusion is certified.
