# Verdict

**PASS as two bounded PARTIAL_RESULT exclusions.** The outer-action lists are exhaustive, all possible restrictions of the factor set to the two coordinate preimages are covered, and the center calculations separate \(K\) from \(L\) in every case. This says nothing about abelian order-eight kernels, larger auxiliary groups, or witnesses outside the sufficient equivariant template.

# Common extension mechanism

In either family,

\[
1\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow P\longrightarrow
C_4\times V_4\longrightarrow1,
\]

and an order-three lift extends the outer action to \(C_4\times A_4\). The \(V_4\)-restriction is outer-trivial. For its preimage \(K\), choose lifts centralizing \(N\). Then

\[
K=N\,C_K(N),\qquad N\cap C_K(N)=Z(N),
\]

and

\[
K\cong(N\times E_V)/\Delta Z(N),
\]

where \(E_V\) is a central extension of \(V_4\) by \(Z(N)\cong C_2\).

The order-three lift preserves \(C_K(N)\), cycles the three nonzero quotient elements, and fixes its central kernel. Therefore the class of \(E_V\) lies in

\[
H^2(V_4,C_2)^{C_3}.
\]

This fixed space is one-dimensional. Its zero class gives \(E_V\cong C_2^3\). Its unique nonzero class has quadratic form nonzero on all three nonzero vectors and gives \(E_V\cong Q_8\). The three \(D_8\)-type classes form a nontrivial orbit and cannot be fixed. Thus no \(V_4\)-restriction is omitted.

For an outer-trivial \(C_4\)-restriction, the same centralizer argument gives

\[
L\cong(N\times E_C)/\Delta C_2.
\]

A central extension of cyclic \(C_4\) by \(C_2\) is generated by a lift \(t\) and the central kernel. The two classes are \(t^4=1\), giving \(E_C\cong C_4\times C_2\), and \(t^4=z\), giving \(E_C\cong C_8\).

For a nontrivial outer \(C_4\)-restriction, changing the lift by an element of \(N\) normalizes conjugation to a chosen order-two representative \(\theta\). Every extension then has

\[
tnt^{-1}=\theta(n),\qquad t^4=z^\varepsilon,
\qquad\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}.
\]

These two values overcover all possible factor sets if an additional global lift condition removes one; they miss none. Mixed cocycle terms between \(C_4\) and \(V_4\) do not alter either restricted extension and therefore cannot change the center comparison.

# Quaternion kernel

Here \(\operatorname{Out}(Q_8)\cong S_3\). A homomorphism \(A_4\to S_3\) has image \(1\) or \(C_3\). If it has image \(C_3\), the commuting \(C_4\)-image is trivial; if it is trivial, the \(C_4\)-image is \(1\) or \(C_2\). These are exactly the three logged outer-action types.

The two possible \(K\) groups are

\[
Q_8\times V_4,\qquad Z(K)\cong C_2^3,
\]

and

\[
Q_8\circ Q_8,\qquad Z(K)\cong C_2.
\]

For outer-trivial \(C_4\), the two possible \(L\) groups have centers

\[
Z(Q_8\times C_4)\cong C_2\times C_4,
\qquad
Z(Q_8\circ C_8)\cong C_8.
\]

For nontrivial outer action, \(\theta^2=1\), so \(t^2\) and \(z\) are central. A central element in an odd \(t\)-coset would induce the nontrivial outer class on \(Q_8\), which is impossible. An element in an even coset centralizes \(Q_8\) only when its \(Q_8\)-component is central. Hence

\[
Z(L)=\langle z,t^2\rangle\cong
\begin{cases}
C_2^2,&t^4=1,\\
C_4,&t^4=z.
\end{cases}
\]

The possible center orders of \(K\) are \(8,2\); those of this \(L\) are \(4\), while the outer-trivial centers have order \(8\) but exponent \(4\) or \(8\), rather than exponent \(2\). No match exists.

# Dihedral kernel

For \(D_8\) of order eight,

\[
\operatorname{Out}(D_8)\cong C_2.
\]

Every homomorphism \(A_4\to C_2\) is trivial, while the \(C_4\)-image is \(1\) or \(C_2\). This is exhaustive. The displayed representative

\[
\theta:r\mapsto r^{-1},\qquad s\mapsto rs
\]

is an automorphism of order \(2\) and lies outside \(\operatorname{Inn}(D_8)\).

The two possible \(K\) groups are

\[
D_8\times V_4,\qquad Z(K)\cong C_2^3,
\]

and

\[
D_8\circ Q_8,\qquad Z(K)\cong C_2.
\]

The outer-trivial \(L\) centers are again \(C_2\times C_4\) and \(C_8\). The nontrivial outer calculation is identical to the quaternion case and gives \(C_2^2\) or \(C_4\). Thus the center order/type never matches that of \(K\).

# Boundary

The global order-three lift may eliminate some listed restricted classes, but cannot add another one; retaining an impossible class only makes the exclusion argument stronger. Since every surviving full factor set restricts to one case above and mixed terms do not affect the restrictions, the coverage is complete for exactly these two order-\(128\) families.
