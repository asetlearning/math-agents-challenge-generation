# What makes math problems hard for reinforcement learning: a case study

## Abstract

> "Using a long-standing conjecture from combinatorial group theory, we explore, from multiple perspectives, the challenges of finding rare instances carrying disproportionately high rewards. Based on lessons learned in the context defined by the Andrews-Curtis conjecture, we propose algorithmic enhancements and a topological hardness measure with implications for a broad class of search problems. As part of our study, we also address several open mathematical questions. Notably, we demonstrate the length reducibility of all but two presentations in the Akbulut-Kirby series (1981), and resolve various potential counterexamples in the Miller-Schupp series (1991), including three infinite subfamilies."

## TL;DR

The paper treats AC-trivialisation of balanced presentations as an RL environment with long horizons and very sparse rewards. It benchmarks BFS, greedy search, PPO and a Transformer on 1190 Miller–Schupp presentations, and proposes two hardness notions: an operational one (solved only at a higher compute level N, with long paths; §5.2) and a global, topological one (the persistent-H₀ barcode of the length-filtered AC graph; §7). Side results include AC-triviality of three infinite MS subfamilies, a length reduction of AK(n) from 2n+7 to n+11, and six length-14 holdouts that greedy search could not solve.

## Problem

The question is how to measure and exploit *hardness* in search problems where solutions are rare and paths are long ("ultra-sparse rewards", §1). The Andrews–Curtis (AC) conjecture is the testbed: the action space is small and finite, each instance has an intrinsic size (total relator length), and known lower bounds on AC-path length range "from linear to superexponential [Bri15; Lis17]" (§1).

## Approach

- **Move set (§2).** AC′ moves: r_i → r_i r_j^{±1} (i ≠ j), and r_i → g r_i g^{-1} for a generator g. Inversion is folded into concatenation because "an inversion transformation leaves the length invariant providing no signal to the search process". Relator lengths are capped at 2 × max(2n+3, length(w)+1) + 2 so that the search space is finite.
- **Dataset (§2).** MS(n, w) = ⟨x, y | x⁻¹yⁿx = yⁿ⁺¹, x = w⟩ with n ≤ 7 and length(w) ≤ 7. After cyclic de-duplication this gives "170 choices of presentations for each fixed n, resulting in a dataset of 7 × 170 = 1190 presentations".
- **Classical search (§3).** BFS and greedy best-first search, with priority (presentation length, path length). Both are capped at 10⁶ visited nodes.
- **RL (§4).** PPO with a solved/unsolved replay mix of 1/4 and 3/4. Horizons are either constant (T = 200, 400, 2000) or a schedule 200 → 400 → 800 → 1200 over 100M environment interactions (§4.5).
- **Supermoves and adaptive action space (§5).** Frequent subsequences of successful PPO paths are added as composite actions. Algorithm 3 adjusts the action space from the *hard instances* found at each compute level N.
- **Language modelling (§6, App. D).** A decoder-only Transformer is trained on about 1.8M presentations, generated by applying random AC′ moves to the 1190 MS seeds (Algorithm 6).
- **Global hardness (§7).** Γ_k is the induced subgraph of the AC graph on presentations of length ≤ k. The barcode of reduced persistent H₀ of the filtration (Γ_k) is the proposed hardness multiset: "the absence of infinite bars in this barcode is equivalent to the AC conjecture" (§7.2.3).
- **Local analysis (§8).** Uses 5-step neighbourhood sizes and random forests to predict PPO-solved versus PPO-unsolved.

## Key result

- **Greedy beats BFS (§3.3).** "Out of the 1190 presentations in the Miller–Schupp series with n ≤ 7 and length(w) ≤ 7, greedy search solved 533 while BFS solved only 278." Greedy solved all presentations of length < 14. "At length 14, there are six presentations that greedy search could not solve." Four of them (MS(2, x⁻²y⁻¹x²y^{±1}) and MS(3, y^{±1}x²y^{±1})) are stated to be AC-equivalent to AK(3). The other two, MS(2, yx²y^{±1}x⁻²), "could be related neither to AK(3) nor to the trivial presentation through any path involving only presentations with both relators having length less than 20."
- **Longest greedy path (§3.3).** ⟨x, y | x⁻¹y⁵x = y⁶, x = yx²y⁻¹⟩ needed "a sequence of 344 AC-moves". Along greedy solutions the maximum length increase was at most 5.
- **Theorem 2 / Theorem 3 / Corollary 9 (§3.4–3.5).** MS(1, w) is AC-trivial for all w. MS(n, w⋆) with w⋆ = y⁻¹xyx⁻¹ is AC-trivial for all n. Every member of MS(2, w_k) and P(2, k) is AC-trivial.
- **Theorem 4 (§3.5).** "For every n ≥ 2, AK(n) is AC-equivalent to the presentation ⟨x, y | x⁻¹yx = xyx⁻¹y, xy^{n−1}x = yxy⟩, of length n + 11. This gives reduction in length of AK(n) for all n ≥ 5." (AK(n) has length 2n + 7.)
- **PPO (§4.4).** With constant horizon T = 200 and T = 400, "the best performing PPO agents could solve 431 and 402 presentations respectively", always a subset of the greedy-solved set. T = 2000 solved 219 (not converged). A separate PPO run used in §8.1 solved 417 (the "PPO-solved" set; 773 unsolved).
- **Theorem 10 (§4.5).** With the variable horizon, PPO AC-trivialised ⟨x, y | x⁻¹y³xy⁻⁴, x⁻¹y⁻¹xy⁻³x⁻¹y⁻¹⟩ and ⟨x, y | x⁻¹y⁴xy⁻⁵, x⁻¹yxy⁻¹x⁻¹y⁻³⟩, two presentations greedy search had failed on. The raw paths of 205 and 1085 moves were pruned to 195 and 381 moves. Initial lengths 17 and 19 rose to maxima of 29 and 30. The first is one letter away from MS(3, w₋₃), which is AC-equivalent to AK(3).
- **Operational hardness (§5.2).** Hard instances are "those which the model is able solve at the current setting of N and not with the lower value of the resource N", restricted to long paths. At N = 8 × 10⁷, five seeds solved 354, 337, 330, 328 and 323 presentations. Only 5 presentations were solved by all five agents (paths 47, 31, 22, 14, 13), while 36 were solved by at least one (paths up to 200).
- **Language model (§6.3).** Validation loss reached 0.7337, against ≈ 1.7918 at initialisation. t-SNE embeddings cluster GS-solved and GS-unsolved presentations even though the training set "contained no information about the ease of solvability". The authors infer an invariant that "survives application of thousands of AC-moves".
- **Barcode statistics (§7.3).** For L = 11…16, the number of nodes is 350,356; 791,140; 3,238,052; 7,199,908; 29,243,812; 64,623,652 (the same under both move sets). With AC moves, the number of bars of length i is h₁ = 4; 16; 72; 144; 508; 1034, h₂ = 0; 0; 4; 4; 52; 88 and h₃ = 0; 0; 0; 0; 8; 20. With AC′ moves it is h₁ = 19; 67; 243; 483; 1819; 3923, h₂ = 0; 0; 16; 16; 136; 208 and h₃ = 0; 0; 0; 0; 32; 80. The AC/AC′ edge ratio is ≈ 1.53, and the h-ratios are "fairly constant" (h₁ ratios 0.2105–0.2981). Up to L = 16 there are no bars of length > 3.
- **Neighbourhood sizes (§8.1).** 5-step neighbourhood sizes lie between 72,964 and 89,872. The largest size covers 2.4% of PPO-solved but 48.3% of PPO-unsolved presentations. Only twelve sizes occur among unsolved presentations, against all 131 among solved ones.
- **Stable AC / unknots (§9, App. F).** The paper finds a misprint in [MMS02, p.10] that undermines a 20-year-old claim that a certain presentation is stably AC-trivial.

## Assumptions

- Hardness is measured relative to a capped search: 10⁶ nodes for BFS and greedy, relator-length masks, and a finite horizon. "GS-unsolved" means unsolved by this specific engine, not AC-nontrivial.
- The AC′ move set generates the same group of transformations as the classical AC moves (§2, footnote 6). "Trivial presentation" is widened to every presentation of length n.
- The barcode experiments truncate the AC graph to nodes admitting a trivialisation path of length ≤ L ≤ 16, a limit set by compute (§7.3).

## Limitations / scope

- The six length-14 holdouts are reported with no move sequences for the four "AC-equivalent to AK(3)" claims. [[carreras-2026-ac-certificates]] later supplied public certificates for the two MS(3) claims and reports that the two MS(2) claims remain uncertified.
- PPO never strictly dominated greedy search with a constant horizon. The authors attribute this to small networks (2 × 512) and limited compute (§4.4, footnote 15).
- The supermove gains were "rather modest" (§5.1). Algorithm 3 is proposed but not evaluated end to end.
- The barcode is computed only on small finite truncations. The authors do not claim it is computable in general (it would decide AC).
- The Transformer result is qualitative (t-SNE clusters), with no classifier accuracy reported.

## Replication evidence

Partial. Code and a verification notebook for the RL paths are public (AC-Solver). Carreras 2026 cross-validated the *Two-Hump* follow-up's solutions at the elementary-move level and independently re-derived its bounded component counts. For this paper's length-14 claims, it confirmed the MS(3) ⇒ AK(3) equivalences with explicit certificates but found no public ledgers for any of the four claims.

## Why this paper matters

This is the reference RL treatment of the AC conjecture, and the starting point of the Gukov-group line that continues in the "Two-Hump" campaign (Fagan et al. 2026, arXiv:2606.21611, AC-19 dataset). Its lasting contributions are methodological. It shows that greedy length-ordered search is a strong baseline that small PPO agents do not beat. It shows that hardness concentrates in a small, structurally distinct tail: a few neighbourhood sizes, and distinct Transformer embedding clusters. It also gives two definitions of instance hardness for path-to-base search problems, one operational (§5.2) and one topological (§7).

It also contains one of the clearest statements of the generic-easy, rare-hard picture in a mathematical search setting: "there is a distribution of problems such that generic instances are 'easy' and a small fraction is 'hard'" (§5).

**Relevance to challenge-gen:** Appendix D / Algorithm 6 is a working instance of *certified generation by reverse walk*. Starting from 1190 MS seeds, it applies phases of N = 1000 random AC′ moves under rising length caps (128 phases, l_max = 128) to produce about 1.8M presentations, each AC-equivalent to its seed with the move trace as certificate. §6.3 shows that a hardness signal inherited from the seed survives "thousands of AC-moves". That suggests generated instances keep the difficulty class of their seed rather than becoming generic, a hypothesis challenge-gen can test for B(2,5) words. Two hardness metrics can be reused directly: the §5.2 definition (solved only at a higher solver budget, with long paths) and the ℓ-increase / barcode notion (§7).

## Open questions surfaced

- Are the two MS(2) holdouts, MS(2, yx²y^{±1}x⁻²), AC-trivial or AC-equivalent to AK(3)? The paper excludes paths with both relators of length < 20. Carreras 2026 links each to a stated-AK(3) case but not to AK(3) itself.
- Can a learned priority function f(r₁, r₂) beat the length-ordered greedy queue (§3.6)?
- Does Algorithm 3 (adaptive supermoves keyed to hard instances) outperform fixed action spaces at scale?
- Which "linguistic" invariant separates GS-solved from GS-unsolved presentations, and is it AC-invariant (§6.3)?
- Does the barcode scaling between AC and AC′ persist beyond L = 16 (§7.3)?
