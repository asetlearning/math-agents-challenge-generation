## 1. The precise conditional statement

**Verbatim abstract** (confirmed via direct fetch of the arXiv abstract page, high confidence):

> "A pair $(G,T)$ is called a faithful odd transposition group if $T$ is a normal set of involutions generating the group $G$ and the product of any two distinct elements of $T$ has odd order. We introduce a special subclass of such groups, a generalized Moufang group of $p$-type (or $GM(p)$-type), in which the product of any two distinct involutions from $T$ has a fixed prime order $p$. For any such group $(G,T)$ and a scalar parameter $\eta$ in a field $\mathbb F$, we construct a non-associative, non-commutative algebra $A = A_{\mathbb F}(G,T,\eta)$. ... The Miyamoto group of $A$ with respect to $T$ is isomorphic to $G/Z(G)$. ... When $G$ is a free Burnside group of odd prime period $p$ extended by an involutory automorphism, the finiteness of $G$ is equivalent to the finite-dimensionality of $A_{\mathbb F}(G,T,\eta)$, providing a reformulation of the Burnside problem. For $p=5$ and $\eta=-1/3$, the algebra generated by two idempotents from $T$ is left-axial and satisfies the Monster-type fusion law $\mathcal M(4/3,-4/3)$."

**Central definitions** (from HTML full-text fetch, verbatim quotes):

- **Def. 1 (faithful odd transposition group)**: "$G$ is a periodic group and $T$ is a normal subset of involutions of $G$ such that $\langle T\rangle=G$, and $|ab|$ for any $a,b\in T$ is odd."
- **Def. 2 (GM(n)-type)**: "$(G,T)$ is called generalized Moufang type $n$ ... if order of the product of any two distinct elements of $T$ is $n$." The paper notes $n$ is forced to be prime (else a smaller-order pair can be extracted).
- **Def. 4 (the algebra)**: basis of $A$ = the set $T$ itself. Multiplication: $a*a=a$; for $b\ne a$, $a*b = b^a + \eta_n\sum_{x\in I(a,b)\setminus\{b^a\}} x$, where $n=|ab|$ and $I(a,b)$ is the relevant dihedral-orbit set. **Consequence I can state independently (not from the paper, elementary linear algebra): since the basis of $A$ IS the set $T$, $\dim A = |T|$ exactly — "finite-dimensional" and "$T$ finite" are the same statement by construction.**

**The load-bearing corollary** (verbatim, HTML fetch):

> **Corollary 1**: "Let $G=B(n,p).\langle t\rangle$, where $p$ is a prime greater than $2$ and $t$ is an automorphism of order $2$ of $B(n,p)$ that inverts free generators, $T=t^G$, $F$ be a field of good characteristic relative to $(p,\eta)$. Then $\mathrm{Miy}(A_F(G,T,\eta),T)\simeq G/Z(G)$, where $Z(G)$ is the center of $G$, and $G$ is finite if and only if $A_F(G,T,\eta)$ is finite-dimensional."

This rests on **Theorem 2**: "Let $(G,T)$ be an odd transposition group and let $G$ have a bounded period. If $G/Z(G)$ is a finite group and $T$ is a finite set, then $G$ is a finite group." I have **not** independently re-derived why (in this specific setup) $T$ finite forces $G/Z(G)$ finite — that appears to depend on Lemmas 2–42 (including an application of Glauberman's $Z^*$-theorem, Lemma 1) and the definiteness of the "symmetric left Frobenius form" mentioned in the abstract, neither of which I read in full. **This is the one piece of the argument I am relaying, not verifying.**

The exponent-5 fusion-law claim (**Proposition 1**, verbatim): "Let $(G,T)$ be a two generated group of $GM(5)$-type, $\eta=-1/3$ and $F$ is a field of good characteristic relative to $(5,\eta)$. Every idempotent in $T$ is a left-axis of $\mathcal M(4/3,-4/3)$-type." Per the extraction this is proved **unconditionally** for the 2-generated subalgebra via explicit Lemmas 6–9 — it does **not** depend on the open finite-dimensionality question.

**What's explicitly left open**, stated by the author (verbatim): "Question 1: For what $d$ and $p$ does the algebra $A_F(B(d,p).\langle t\rangle,T,\eta)$ ... have finite dimension? Question 2: For which $p$ does the algebra [of] GM(p,η)-type generated by $d+1$ elements[,] is finite dimensional?" The paper does not claim to resolve these — it proposes the algebraic reformulation and stops there.

## 2. Is the group object really free B(2,5), or a trap variant?

**Checked with the requested paranoia. Verdict: it is genuinely the free group, not a restricted/other variant — but it is not literally B(2,5) itself, it's an index-2 extension of it, and that extension step is trivial and harmless.**

- Corollary 1's $G$ is explicitly $B(n,p).\langle t\rangle$ — the **free** Burnside group $B(n,p)$ (rank $n$, exponent $p$; the paper's own text confirms this reading via "Let $G=\langle x_1,....,x_d\rangle$ be a free Burnside group of odd prime period $p$" immediately preceding the corollary in the body). For $n=2,p=5$ this is genuinely $B(2,5)$, extended by $t$ = the automorphism inverting the two free generators — **the same inversion automorphism already studied in this vault's Kuznetsov line** ([[kuznetsov-filippov-2010-sjim]], [[kuznetsov-safonov-2018]]), just applied to the free group instead of the restricted quotient $B_0(2,5)$.
- No restricted-quotient substitution anywhere in this corollary — unlike the 119-benchmark-words trap, there is no hidden "$B_0$ instead of $B$" swap here. Confirmed directly from the quoted text: "free Burnside group ... $B(n,p)$", not "$B_0(n,p)$" or "restricted Burnside group."
- **The one honest caveat, and it's benign**: the theorem is stated for $G=B(n,p).\langle t\rangle$ (the semidirect/split extension by $t$), not for $B(n,p)$ alone. I can close this gap myself with an elementary, independently-verifiable fact (not from the paper): for any group $N$ and finite-index extension $N\trianglelefteq G$ with $[G:N]<\infty$, $|G|=|N|\cdot[G:N]$, so **$G$ is finite iff $N$ is finite** — no group-theoretic subtlety, just Lagrange's theorem, since $[G:N]=2$ here (t has order 2, generates the quotient). So $B(2,5).\langle t\rangle$ finite $\iff$ $B(2,5)$ finite, unconditionally. Chaining this onto Corollary 1 gives a genuine (modulo the unread Lemmas 2–42) equivalence: **$B(2,5)$ finite $\iff$ $A_F(B(2,5).\langle t\rangle,T,\eta)$ finite-dimensional $\iff$ $T=t^G$ finite** (the last step by the "basis = T" observation above, itself elementary).
- **So: this is a real, non-trap reformulation of the actual open question (Kourovka 11.48-adjacent), modulo trusting the unread interior lemmas of a 5-month-old, zero-citation preprint.** That trust boundary is the real caveat, not a hidden restricted/free substitution.

## 3. Computability with our stack (GAP 4.15 + anupq/nq/lpres/ace/kbmag, KB infra, SAT)

Four concrete, cost-estimated items. None of these would resolve the open question — the point is testable, cheap sanity/heuristic computations, in the same spirit as the paper's own open Questions 1–2.

**(a) Small 2-generated axial-algebra sanity check (Proposition 1).** Build the explicit finite-dimensional algebra generated by two idempotents $a,b\in T$ with $|ab|=5$, $\eta=-1/3$, from the paper's own multiplication rule, and verify by direct linear algebra that it satisfies the Monster-type fusion law $\mathcal M(4/3,-4/3)$ (eigenvalue decomposition $\{1,4/3,-4/3\}$, fusion-rule closure) and carries a symmetric, well-defined Frobenius form. **This does not need our GAP/KBMAG stack at all** — it's a small (structurally analogous to the known Norton–Sakuma/Majorana 5A algebras, likely low single-digit dimension), self-contained linear-algebra computation, doable in plain Python/sympy or GAP's algebra package in well under an hour. Cheapest, most shovel-ready item; good Validator or Developer task, doesn't require Researcher's B(2,5)-specific infra.

**(b) Restricted-quotient tower analogue — heuristic only, explicitly NOT proof-relevant to Corollary 1.** Corollary 1 needs the free group; but we already have (via [[kuznetsov-filippov-2010-sjim]]) $|C_{B_0(2,5)}(t)|=5^{16}$ for exactly this inversion automorphism $t$ on the restricted quotient $B_0(2,5)$ (order $5^{34}$). By orbit-stabilizer, $|t^{B_0(2,5)}| = 5^{34}/5^{16} = 5^{18}$ — i.e. in the **restricted** analogue of Gorshkov's construction, $|T|=5^{18}\approx 3.8\times10^{12}$: already too large to build the algebra explicitly, but a well-defined number we get for free from data already in the vault, no new computation needed. Extending this to the full lower-central-series tower $B_k$ ($k=1..34$, same tower [[growth-functions-burnside]] already tracks) — computing $|t^{B_k}|=|B_k|/|C_{B_k}(t)|$ for each $k$ via GAP — is cheap (seconds per $k$ for small $k$, reuses existing infra) and would give an empirical growth curve. **Flag clearly to whoever picks this up: this says nothing about Corollary 1 or the free group's finiteness** — restricted quotients are finite by construction (Hall's theorem), so this curve can only ever look "finite," it cannot distinguish the free case. Useful only as a cheap warm-up / infra dry-run, not as evidence either way.

**(c) KBMAG extension of the existing B(2,5) presentation — the most direct, stack-native item.** Add a generator $t$ and relations $t^2=1$, $t\,a_i\,t^{-1}=a_i^{-1}$ ($i=1,2$) to the existing B(2,5) KB presentation, producing a KBMAG presentation for exactly $G=B(2,5).\langle t\rangle$, and run the project's standard KB/mixing pipeline on it. If it completes to a confluent finite rewriting system, that is (weak, non-proof) evidence of finiteness of $G$ (hence of $B(2,5)$ itself, by the Lagrange argument in §2); non-termination proves nothing either way (same standing caveat as every other KB attempt in this program, see [[b-exponent-5-adian-4.2b]]). Cost: minutes to set up (small presentation edit), running cost comparable to existing B(2,5) KB runs already in this program — this is the item that most directly reuses current infrastructure with near-zero marginal setup cost. Worth checking with Developer whether an equivalent presentation (inversion automorphism adjoined) has already been run anywhere in the Kuznetsov-inspired centralizer work.

**(d) SAT**: no obvious fit found. The paper's content is continuous/algebraic (eigenspace decomposition, bilinear-form definiteness), not a discrete constraint-satisfaction structure — I don't see a natural SAT encoding here and am not proposing one.
