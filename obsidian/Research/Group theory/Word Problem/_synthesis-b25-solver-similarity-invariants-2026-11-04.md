# Synthesis — similarity measures and invariants for steering trivial words towards a target in B(2,5)

> **Synthesis note, task I1-invariant-review** of [[b25-solver-stage1-plan-2026-11-04]] (project [[project-b25-solver]]). Written by the Researcher, 2026-11-04. Status draft; uncommitted.
>
> **How to read the labels.** Every claim carries one of: **[established]** (published result I opened or that is standard textbook algebra), **[derived+checked]** (my own short derivation, with a small numerical check run in a scratch directory, not in the repo), **[derived]** (my derivation, not checked), **[from memory]** (a reference or fact I know but did not open this session; verify before citing), **[open]**. Source status is listed per reference in the table at the end.

## The question

Both the target word w and a pool word u are trivial in B(2,5) = F₂/N, where F₂ is the free group on x, y and N = ⟨⟨v⁵ : v ∈ F₂⟩⟩ is the normal closure of all fifth powers. Which similarity measures between w and u (or invariants of w) can still carry information, what do they measure, what do they cost on words of length 100–1300, and do they vary inside N?

## Terms

- **Normal closure N, product of conjugates.** u ∈ N means u = ∏ gᵢ rᵢ^{±1} gᵢ⁻¹ in F₂ with each rᵢ a fifth power vᵢ⁵. The number of factors is the **area** of that expression (the number of cells in a van Kampen diagram).
- **Verbal subgroup.** N is generated by all values of the word map v ↦ v⁵, so it is invariant under **every endomorphism** φ of F₂ (not only automorphisms): w ∈ N ⇒ φ(w) ∈ N. [established, standard]
- **Free nilpotent quotient F/γ_c.** γ₁ = F, γ_{c+1} = [γ_c, F]. F/γ_{c} is the free nilpotent group of class c−1.
- **Magnus expansion μ over a ring A.** The ring map x ↦ 1+X, x⁻¹ ↦ 1−X+X²−… from F₂ into the units of the noncommutative power series A⟨⟨X,Y⟩⟩. [established: Magnus 1937]
- **Mod-5 dimension (Zassenhaus) subgroup D_n.** D_n = ∏_{i·5^j ≥ n} γ_i^{5^j}. For A = 𝔽₅: w ∈ D_n ⇔ μ(w) − 1 has no terms of degree < n. [established, Lazard 1954 / Jennings 1941; from memory]
- **B_k.** The finitely presented group ⟨x, y | v⁵ for all |v| ≤ k⟩ = F₂/N_k. N₁ ⊂ N₂ ⊂ … and ∪N_k = N. B_k maps onto B(2,5). [established, definition]
- **LCCF(w, u).** Length of the longest common cyclic factor: the longest word that is a factor of some cyclic rotation of w and of some cyclic rotation of u (u also taken inverted).
- **d(w, u).** The Lead's group distance: |free reduction of w·ρ(u)⁻¹|, minimised over rotations and the 16 images (8 letter automorphisms × inversion), as in the stage-1 plan.
- **R₂.₅.** The repo's 2.5-reduction (greedy, then cyclic exhaustive).

## A. Free-group images: Magnus expansion, nilpotent quotients, Fox derivatives

### A0. Why the quotients of B(2,5) are blind

Every invariant that factors through F₂/N takes the value 1 on w and on u. The finite nilpotent quotient B₀(2,5) (order 5³⁴, nilpotency class 12; [[havas-wall-wamsley-1974]]) is such a quotient, so for c ≥ 13 the quotient F/(γ_c N) is B₀(2,5) and tells nothing. [established] The informative objects are therefore quotients of F₂ by something **smaller** than N: γ_c, D_c, or a small piece of N.

### A1. Exponent sums over ℤ

For v ∈ F₂, (e_x, e_y)(v⁵) = 5·(e_x, e_y)(v), so every product of conjugates of fifth powers has exponent sums in 5ℤ². [established] The vector (e_x, e_y)(w)/5 ∈ ℤ² equals Σ ± (e_x, e_y)(vᵢ) over the relator factors. It is a lattice-point invariant of w, takes different values on different elements of N, costs O(n), and is invariant under rotation. It is a **necessary** condition: a candidate "shortened" word z with exponent sums not in 5ℤ² cannot be trivial. This is the "trivial abelianization" check already in the project profile.

### A2. Magnus expansion over 𝔽₅

**Claim 1 (all of N vanishes below degree 5).** Let Z = μ(v) − 1, a series with no terms below degree 1. Over 𝔽₅, (1+Z)⁵ = 1 + Z⁵ because 5 divides the binomial coefficients C(5,k) for 0 < k < 5. Hence μ(v⁵) ≡ 1 mod degree 5, and the same holds for conjugates and products. So **every word in N has μ(w) ≡ 1 mod degree 5 over 𝔽₅**; equivalently N ⊂ D₅(F₂). [established algebra; derived+checked: 300 random products of 1–4 conjugates of fifth powers, conjugators and roots up to length 5: none had a nonzero term below degree 5. Check script is in the Researcher scratch directory; not in the repo.]

Consequences:
- Below degree 5 the Magnus image is a **filter, not a score**: it is the same (trivial) for all words of N, and it detects most non-trivial words. On 300 random reduced words of length 12, the lowest nonzero degree was 1 in 292 cases and 2 in 7. [derived+checked]
- From degree 5 on, the leading term can differ between elements of N. If the minimal degree of Z = μ(v) − 1 is d, then μ(v⁵) − 1 has lowest term (lead Z)⁵, of degree 5d, and it is non-zero because the free associative algebra has no zero divisors. Conjugation by g does not change the lowest term (g·T·g⁻¹ has the same leading part as T, since μ(g) = 1 + higher terms). [derived]
- For a relator vⁱ with v of non-zero exponent sum mod 5, the lowest term is a fifth power of a nonzero linear form, in degree 5. The span of such terms is a proper subspace of the 32-dimensional degree-5 component. **A necessary condition on any product that equals w is that the degree-5 part of μ(w) lies in the span built from the relators used.** I did not compute the dimension of that span. [derived; open: whether it yields a usable guide]
- The same computation over ℤ/25 picks up the exponent sums divided by 5 at degree 1 (A1) and higher-degree information from the terms 5Z, 10Z², 10Z³, 5Z⁴ of (1+Z)⁵. [derived]

**Cost.** Multiplying a truncated series by (1+X) or (1+X)⁻¹ costs O(2^D) (a shift and an add, or a recurrence by degree). The number of noncommutative monomials of degree ≤ D in two variables is 2^{D+1} − 1. For n = 1300 and D = 12 this is ~10⁷ operations per word; D = 20 is ~3·10⁹ and 2 MB of coefficients per word (one byte each). [derived]

**What it measures.** The ultrametric d_Mag(w, u) = the lowest degree of μ(w) − μ(u) measures the commutator depth of w·u⁻¹ (how deep in the Zassenhaus filtration it lies), **not** its word length or its area. A short word z = [[x,y],y] already has high Magnus order, while a long word with exponent sum 1 has order 1. So I expect d_Mag to be uncorrelated with the shortening signal. [derived; open: no data]

References: Magnus 1937 (Crelle 177); Magnus–Karrass–Solitar, *Combinatorial Group Theory* (1966) ch. 5; Lazard 1954 (Ann. ENS 71); Passi, *Group Rings and Their Augmentation Ideals* (LNM 715, 1979). [all from memory]

### A3. Free nilpotent quotients and the Hall basis

F/γ_c is torsion-free nilpotent with a Mal'cev (Hall) basis of Witt-number size. For two generators the ranks per layer are 2, 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18, 30, 56, 99, 186, 335 (Witt's formula), cumulative 2, 3, 5, 8, 14, 23, 41, 71, 127, 226, 412, 747. [established, standard; computed here from Witt's formula]

- **What it measures:** the coordinates of w in the nilpotent group; for w ∈ N they lie in the subgroup K_c = Nγ_c/γ_c, of finite index (the index is |B₀(2,5)| = 5³⁴ once c ≥ 13).
- **Cost:** collection (rewriting a word to a Hall-basis normal form) is polynomial; the coordinates are polynomials in the letter counts of degree up to c, so they reach ~n^c, which needs big integers or reduction modulo a power of 5 for c ≳ 6 at n = 1300. [derived]
- **Varies in N:** yes. But the equality of images of w and u (w ≡ u mod γ_c) only gives a **coset test**. There is no known reason that "closer in F/γ_c" means "fewer relator conjugates apart".
- Subgroup membership in finitely generated nilpotent groups is decidable in polynomial time (Baumslag–Cannonito–Robinson–Segal 1991). [from memory]
- References: Hall 1957/1959 (Hall basis, nilpotent group notes); Chen–Fox–Lyndon 1958, *Free differential calculus IV: the quotient groups of the lower central series*, Ann. Math. 68, 81–95, doi:10.2307/1970044 [bibliographic data confirmed by web search; full text not opened]; Sims, *Computation with Finitely Presented Groups* (1994); Holt–Eick–O'Brien, *Handbook of Computational Group Theory* (2005). [the last three from memory]

### A4. Finite 5-group quotients (option, [open])

Instead of dividing by all of N, divide by γ_c·F^{5^k} (k ≥ 2): a finite 5-group Q_{c,k} whose p-quotient presentation the p-quotient algorithm computes (GAP ANUPQ; Havas–Newman 1980, vault: [[havas-newman-1980]]). The image of N is the subgroup generated by fifth powers inside Q_{c,k}, which is **non-trivial** when k ≥ 2. Words of length 1300 collect in a pc-presentation in O(n·poly) time. Then w and u are two non-trivial elements of the same subgroup, and the group distance in Q (word length of w̄·ū⁻¹ in the generators of the subgroup) is a computable proxy with a finite, searchable target. Whether it correlates with closeness in F₂ is untested. [derived; open]

### A5. Fox derivatives over ℤ₅ and the loop in a Cayley graph

The Fox derivative ∂/∂xⱼ on ℤ[F₂] is defined by ∂(ab) = ∂a + a·∂b, ∂xᵢ = δᵢⱼ. [established: Fox 1953]
- **Magnus coefficients = iterated Fox derivatives.** The coefficient of Xᵢ₁…Xᵢₖ in μ(w) equals ε(∂ᵏw/∂xᵢ₁…∂xᵢₖ), ε the augmentation. So the Magnus data is the Fox data collapsed to the trivial group. [established, Fox 1953 / Chen–Fox–Lyndon 1958; from memory]
- **Fundamental formula.** ∂(g r g⁻¹) = (1 − g r g⁻¹)∂g + g·∂r. If π: F₂ → Q kills r, then π(∂(grg⁻¹)) = π(g)·π(∂r). Hence for w = ∏ gᵢ rᵢ^{±1} gᵢ⁻¹ ∈ N and any quotient π with π(N) = 1 (Q a quotient of B(2,5)):
 π(∂w) = Σ ± π(gᵢ)·π(∂rᵢ), in ℤ₅[Q]². [derived]
- **Geometric meaning.** π(∂w) is the cellular 1-cycle of the closed path of w in the Cayley graph of Q: the coefficient at (vertex q, label xⱼ) counts, with sign mod 5, the passes of the path over that edge. The formula says this cycle is a sum of translated boundaries of relator cells. **Finding the shortest such sum over ℤ₅ is a linear-algebra / ℓ¹ problem, the homological relaxation of the area problem** ("homological area"). [derived; the idea that ℓ¹-norms of such classes give area lower bounds is Gersten's (Dehn functions and ℓ¹-norms of finite presentations, 1992/96) [from memory, not verified: a web search did not confirm the paper]]
- **Subadditivity.** The support size |supp π(∂w)| ≤ Σᵢ |supp π(∂rᵢ)| ≤ (number of factors)·5·max|vᵢ|, since the support of a sum is at most the sum of supports. Because |supp π(∂w)| ≤ n, this bounds the area from below only by ~n/(5·max|v|): **no stronger than the trivial letter-count bound.** [derived] The method gets teeth only by using the cycle itself (linear programming), not its size.
- **Cost:** O(n) multiplications in Q plus a hash map; the linear program needs |Q|·(number of relators) variables, so it is feasible only for small Q (|Q| ≲ 10⁵). [derived]
- Because π(∂w) vanishes for w ∈ [N,N]-type identities, two different words of N can have the same cycle; the cycle is a class in the relation module, not a complete invariant. [derived]

## B. Finitely presented approximations B_k

- **Definition and monotonicity.** B_k = F₂/N_k, N_k = ⟨⟨v⁵ : |v| ≤ k⟩⟩; every B_k maps onto B(2,5), so w ∈ N_k ⇒ w ∈ N. If B(2,5) is finite it is finitely presented, so B_k = B(2,5) for some k; if B(2,5) is infinite there is no such k. [established, elementary; relates to [[b25-finiteness-11.48-kostrikin]]]
- **B1, relator depth of a certificate (cheap).** A certificate (product of conjugated fifth powers) uses roots v of some maximal length; this is ρ_cert(w) ≥ ρ(w) := min{k : w ∈ N_k}. It varies within N and costs O(certificate). For the benchmark it is known from the recipes (the stage-1 plan states |v| ≤ 5 for the old pool). [derived]
- **B2, lower bounds on ρ(w).** If w is **non-trivial** in B_k, every proof of w = 1 must use a root longer than k. That is a statement about which relators the pool must contain. Deciding non-triviality in B_k needs a normal form: Knuth–Bendix (kbmag) or coset enumeration on B_k. B₁ = ℤ₅ ∗ ℤ₅ (infinite; normal form by free-product length). For k ≥ 2, whether B_k is infinite, whether it is hyperbolic or automatic, and whether KB completes are **not known to me**; I found no published study of this relator-length filtration for exponent 5 and did not search beyond the vault. [open]
- **Dehn-function reading.** Area over B_k (the minimal number of relators of root length ≤ k) is the Dehn function of a possibly infinite presentation. Kapovich (2026, arXiv:2606.01290, abstract only) shows that Dehn functions of ℤ² change growth type with the choice of an infinite relator set, with n log n for one family: **the area of a word depends on which relators are allowed**, so any "area" measure in this project is relative to the pool's relator set. [established for ℤ², abstract level; the transfer to B(2,5) is mine]
- **Cost:** KB on B_k is unbounded in the worst case; coset enumeration is not applicable if B_k is infinite. A minimal experiment: run kbmag on B₂, B₃ and reduce the 6 target words. [open; for Experimenter]

## C. Free-group distances and string similarity

### C1. What the Lead's distance d(w, u) actually is

**Claim 2.** For cyclically reduced w and u, min over rotations of w, rotations of u, and u ↔ u⁻¹ of |red(w·u′⁻¹)| equals |w| + |u| − 2·LCCF(w, u). [derived+checked: 150 random cyclically reduced pairs, lengths 5–10, brute force over all rotations and both orientations, 150/150 equal. The letter automorphisms are not part of the check; with them one replaces u by each of its images.]

Reason: rotate w to P·M and u′ to Q·M with M a common suffix; then w·u′⁻¹ reduces to P·Q⁻¹ and cancels exactly the common suffix; the common suffix is maximised by choosing M the longest common cyclic factor.

Consequences:
1. **d(w, u) < |w| ⇔ 2·LCCF > |u|.** A pool word that is as long as w must share a common cyclic factor of more than half its length with w (or with its inverse or an image). Closeness is therefore all-or-nothing on exact long matches; the gradient is sparse. [derived+checked]
2. **d(w, u) < |w| is a rewriting step.** If w = A·M·B and u ~ M·X (cyclically) then w ~ A·X⁻¹·B in B(2,5) and the new length is |w| − |M| + |X|. The pool acts as a dictionary of rewrite rules "M → X⁻¹" taken from its trivial words (the same view as the Mixer rule bank in [[_synthesis-b25-beat-beam-search]]). [derived]
3. **Cost:** LCCF by a suffix automaton on w·w costs O(n); 16 images and both orientations ×16 → ~30 passes, microseconds to milliseconds at n = 1300. The brute-force form (all rotations × reduction) is O(n³) and unnecessary. [derived]

### C2. Reduction-aware and smoothed variants

- **Reduction-aware distance:** |R₂.₅(w·ρ(u)⁻¹)| replaces free reduction by the repo's reducer. Because R₂.₅ shortens by the known short relators, it also recognises near-matches (differences of the form v⁵ with short v). Cost: R₂.₅ per pair. Already in the hybrid scorer. [derived]
- **Edit distance** (Levenshtein 1966; Wagner–Fischer 1974, O(nm); Myers 1999 bit-parallel; Landau–Vishkin 1989 for bounded distance): about 1.7·10⁶ cells at n = 1300, tens of microseconds with bit-parallelism. The **cyclic edit distance** (Maes 1990, O(nm log m)) removes the rotation dependence. [all from memory] Edit distance is **not invariant under free equality** (inserting x x⁻¹ costs 2 but changes nothing), and not under the automorphisms; it is a heuristic. The project profile already notes both points.
- **k-mer and BPE overlaps** (spectrum kernel, Leslie–Eskin–Noble 2002; BPE profiles from [[_synthesis-b25-patternboost-tokenization]]): O(n) per word, give a smooth similarity, no guarantee. [from memory for the spectrum kernel]
- In [[charton-2024-patternboost]] the local-search objective is problem-specific and the transformer only sees top constructions; nothing in the paper supplies a similarity measure for group words. [established, vault note]

### C3. The verbal subgroup and the Aut(F₂)/End(F₂) orbit [potentially the largest lever]

N is invariant under every endomorphism of F₂ (see Terms). Two consequences:

1. **Equivalent targets.** For any automorphism φ, w ∈ N ⇔ φ(w) ∈ N, and a proof of φ(w) ∈ N becomes a proof of w ∈ N by applying φ⁻¹ to every factor (the relators are all fifth powers, so the image of a relator is again a relator). So a **shorter φ(w)** is an easier equivalent target. The Whitehead algorithm finds a minimal-length (cyclic) element in the Aut(F₂) orbit; Roig–Ventura–Weil (2007) give a polynomial algorithm (IJAC; bibliographic data confirmed by web search, abstract level). The 16 images of the plan are a small finite subgroup of Aut(F₂). Whether the benchmark words are already Whitehead-minimal is **not known; a one-off computation settles it**. [established: Whitehead 1936, Lyndon–Schupp Ch. I §4; derived: the transfer to the certificates]
2. **Substitution instances.** If w = t(a, b) for words a, b and a shorter word t, a proof for t(x, y) gives a proof for w by substitution (the "scheme C" of challenge-gen). Detecting this needs endomorphism-preimage tools: Stallings foldings (Stallings 1983; Kapovich–Myasnikov 2002) [from memory]; the earlier T-028 grammar-compression discussion ([[_synthesis-t028-literature-2026-10-20]]) is the same question.

### C4. Dehn-function- and diagram-aware measures

- **Depth versus area.** Myasnikov–Ushakov ([[myasnikov-ushakov-2011-random-van-kampen]]) show that random van Kampen diagrams over a fixed group are shallow, and that a closure solver exponential in depth is generically polynomial. A target built by nesting (as the benchmark words are) is deep by design. The depth of a diagram is known only if the recipe is known, so it is available for pool words (T-in, T-out) and not for the target. [established, vault]
- **Sampling distribution.** Elder–Rechnitzer–Janse van Rensburg ([[elder-2015-random-trivial-words]]) give a Metropolis sampler of trivial words with the distribution (|w|+1)^α β^{|w|}, with moves "conjugate by a generator" and "insert a cyclic permutation of a relator". It controls the length distribution of the pool, not the similarity to a given w. [established, vault]
- **Peaks.** The filling-length literature ([[gersten-riley-2002]], [[olshanskii-2011-space-functions]]) measures the maximum length along a rewriting path. A target with a high peak cannot be shortened monotonically by any rewriting; a pool word that cancels a long piece of w "in one step" avoids that peak. The Researcher's T-028 synthesis states the move-set caveat. [established, vault]
- **Area lower bounds.** See A5; the practical lower bound is the letter count.

### C5. Learned distances and learned rewriting (closest published analogues)

All are in the vault except one abstract-only item:
- [[miasnikov-1999-ac-genetic]]: relator length as the fitness of a genetic search over Andrews–Curtis moves, Whitehead automorphisms and conjugations: the baseline "length is the signal" setting. [established]
- [[gukov-2020-learning-to-unknot]]: a policy that emits rewrite moves on braid words. [established]
- [[chervov-2025-cayleypy-rl]]: a learned distance-to-identity guiding beam search on Cayley graphs; the closest to a learned d(·). [established]
- Shehper et al., *What makes math problems hard for reinforcement learning: a case study* (arXiv:2408.15332, 2024; v2 2025; NeurIPS 2025): rare high-reward instances, "algorithmic enhancements" and a topological hardness measure for Andrews–Curtis; abstract only; features, "supermoves" and the hardness measure were **not read**. Relevant because the benchmark words are also rare-event instances. [abstract level]
- [[carreras-2026-ac-certificates]]: replayable move ledgers verified by a small independent checker, as the model for certificate-first reporting. [established]
- **No source found** that defines a similarity measure for steering one trivial word towards another as a product of conjugates. I searched the vault and made 6 web searches (Shehper, Chen–Fox–Lyndon, Whitehead complexity, Gersten ℓ¹ norms, plus the two fetches); an exhaustive literature search was not attempted. [absence claim, limited search]

## What's settled

- N is a verbal (fully invariant) subgroup, so images of a trivial word under any endomorphism are trivial. [established]
- Every invariant that factors through B(2,5) or its quotients is constant on w and u; useful invariants live in F₂ or its quotients by subgroups of N. [established]
- Over 𝔽₅, every word of N has Magnus expansion ≡ 1 mod degree 5; below that level the Magnus image only filters. [established algebra, checked on 300 random products]
- d(w, u) is exactly |w| + |u| − 2·LCCF; shortening needs a common cyclic factor longer than |u|/2. [derived+checked]

## What's contested

- Whether closeness in any filtration-type metric (Magnus, γ_c, D_c) correlates with closeness as a product of conjugates. No source claims it; I expect it does not. [open]

## What's open

1. Are the benchmark words Whitehead-minimal in their Aut(F₂) orbit (C3)? One-off, polynomial.
2. Is B₂/B₃ tractable by kbmag, and is any of the 6 targets non-trivial there (B2)? This would set a necessary root length for the pool.
3. Does the degree-5 Magnus part of the 6 targets sit in the span predicted by the relators of a given pool (A2)? A check on the benchmark that costs seconds.
4. Is the homological area of w in a small Q (A5) close to the true area for pool words, whose area is known by construction?
5. The unread parts: Shehper et al. (features, supermoves), Gersten (ℓ¹-norms), Bridson–Riley (not ingested).

## Methodology notes

- The small numerical checks (A2 and C1) were run on short random words only and say nothing about words of length 100–1300; they confirm the algebra, not the usefulness.

## Recommendation (ranked for stage 1; the Math Expert reviews next)

1. **Use d(w, u) as defined, but know what it is** (LCCF): exact, O(n), and the |R₂.₅(w·ρ(u)⁻¹)| version for near-matches. Expect the signal to be sparse; the smoothed variants (edit distance, k-mers) are heuristics and should be reported as such.
2. **Run the Whitehead-minimality check on the 6 targets and on the registry** (C3). If a benchmark word is not minimal, its minimal image is an easier equivalent target with a transferable certificate.
3. **Add exponent-sum and Magnus-mod-5 (degree < 5) checks as filters** on pool words and on any claimed shortening: cheap, exact, and they catch non-trivial words early. Do not use them as scores.
4. **Record ρ_cert (B1) in the pool metadata**; run kbmag on B₂ and B₃ as a cheap experiment (B2).
5. **Defer** the finite-quotient proxy (A4), the homological area (A5) and learned distances (C6) until E0 reports.
