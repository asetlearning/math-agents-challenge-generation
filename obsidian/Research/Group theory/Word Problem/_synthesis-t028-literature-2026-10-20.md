## Q1. Filling length vs coset-enumeration space vs Knuth–Bendix (updated after full-text reading)
- **Established** ([[gersten-riley-2002]], arXiv v1 read). Gromov's FL(w) is the min over van Kampen diagrams of the max boundary length while collapsing (free reduction, based, plus relator substitution via cyclic permutations of relators). Theorem 1: $h_0(n)\preceq g(n)\log(f(n)+1)$ for an AD-pair $(f,g)$; also $g_0\le h_0\le 2Kf_0+n$ and $f_0\preceq\exp h_0$; asynchronously combable groups have linear FL. (Earlier note of this synthesis wrote the bound as $[g+1][\log(f+1)+1]$; the theorem is the cleaner $g\log(f+1)$, the longer form is Proposition 2 for single diagrams.)
- **Established, with a correction to the definition used in T-028** ([[olshanskii-2011-space-functions]]). The space of a rewriting is the max total length of the word-sequence; the choice of moves matters: FL (Gromov), FFL (adds cyclic conjugation), FFFL (adds splitting $uv\to(u,v)$); Olshanskii uses FFFL. For one group FFFL can be linear while FL and FFL are exponential (Bridson–Riley, quoted there). The proposal's moves (free insert/delete, relator replace on cyclic words) correspond to FFL, not to Gromov's FL; if D/KB-style solvers split words, FFFL is closer. State the move set explicitly when defining "peak".
- **Established.** Prop 1.1 of Olshanskii: space function = space of a nondeterministic TM accepting the trivial words; word-problem space $\le S_G$ but not conversely (Baumslag's one-relator group: space function beyond multi-exponential, word problem polynomial). So large filling length does not imply an intrinsically hard word problem; it only blocks rewriting-style solvers.
- **Established (negative).** No source read relates FL to coset-table size or to KB. Coset enumeration cost is measured by MaxCos $M$ (memory) and TotCos $T$ (time), which can differ by orders of magnitude, and Sims proved no polynomial bound of $T$ in $M$ (stated in [[havas-ramsay-2000]]). Neither is a word-length peak.
- **Open.** Any inequality between FL (or FFL/FFFL) and $M$, $T$ or KB rule-set size. For a finite group all of these are bounded; the asymptotic theorems do not apply to B(2,5) if it is finite.

## Q2. Known hard instances for Todd–Coxeter / Knuth–Bendix (updated)
- **Established, and directly relevant** ([[havas-ramsay-2000]], full text). Neumann's family $E_1,E_2,E_3,\dots$ of trivial-group presentations is built by substituting words for the generators of the previous presentation — verbal substitution, as in scheme C. Findings: $E_1$ is easy ($T=588$ by Felsch); $E_2$ defeated Sims' procedures (no coincidences within 100,000 cosets); original $E_2$ over the trivial subgroup needs $T\approx8.3\times10^8$, $M\approx3.4\times10^8$ (about 9 GB); randomised relator rotation/inversion plus HLT-heavy strategy brings it to $M\approx4.4\times10^6$ (about 4 MB). **Knuth–Bendix solves $E_2$ in 0.6 s**; the 3-generator $E_3$ takes KBMAG 1347 s with 14.8 M total rule length, and defeated coset enumeration unless guided by hand. So: substitution makes TC much harder at once, KB much less, and a second substitution round hurts both; also hardness is partly strategy-dependent (tuned solvers win).
- **Established.** Arbitrarily long enumerations for the trivial group: [[coleman-mitchell-smith-tsalakou-2022-todd-coxeter-semigroups]] §1 (example $\langle a,b\mid ab^n,a^nb^{n+1},b^nab^{-1}a^{-1}\rangle$, "likely" at least $n$ steps; their reference [17], not proved there).
- Baumslag–Gersten and Andrews–Curtis candidates: as before (presentation-hard; word problem of Baumslag's group polynomial per [[olshanskii-2011-space-functions]]).
- **Open.** A published hard family of *words* (not presentations) for exponent-5 groups.

## Q3. Exponent-5 identities usable as long relators
- **Key logical point (established).** A word identity holds in every exponent-5 group iff it holds in $B(\infty,5)$ iff it follows from the exponent law ($F^5$ fully invariant). So a *law* can be used as a relator for B(2,5) with a certificate over U = {u⁵}; something true only in the quotient $B_0(2,5)$ (or only in 2-generated quotients such as class-12 truncation) cannot be assumed, unless shown to follow.
- **Hall–Petresco (established).** In any group $x^m y^m=(xy)^m c_2^{\binom m2}c_3^{\binom m3}\cdots c_m$, $c_i\in\gamma_i(\langle x,y\rangle)$. For $m=5$, in exponent 5 the middle binomials $\binom5i$ (1<i<5) are multiples of 5, so one gets $c_5=1$ as a law, i.e. a specific element of $\gamma_5$ is trivial. I did **not** find the explicit words $c_i$ in a source (Wikipedia gives none); they can be computed by collection (GAP/pc-group) and certified by the U cells of $x^5,y^5,(xy)^5$. [plausible, ~3 cells per instance — short, not "long-period"]
- **Engel (established, with caveat).** The Lie ring of exponent-5 groups satisfies the 4-Engel identity (Higman route to restricted Burnside, arXiv 2410.11524); Vaughan-Lee (1997): 4-Engel groups of exponent 5 are locally nilpotent (Macedońska slides); a Cambridge abstract gives an identity in $[y,z,z,z,z]$ equivalent to local finiteness of exponent-5 groups. Hence the group-level 4-Engel identity is **not** a known law of exponent 5 (it would be tied to the Burnside problem) — **do not use it as a relator** [open]; it holds in $B_0(2,5)$ only as a quotient statement.
- **Plausible, more useful.** Long laws with free certificates come for free from the proposal's own mechanism: any substitution instance of $u^5$ with $|u|$ large (Q-scheme C), or conjugate products of such. Literature gives no list of *short-derivation named* exponent-5 laws beyond Hall–Petresco-type consequences.

## Q4. Compression attacks that could recover a substitution $t(x,y)$ (updated)
- **Established.** Smallest grammar is NP-hard, even to approximate within 8569/8568; Re-Pair is a standard heuristic; no polynomial algorithm with ratio $o(\log n)$ is known ([[charikar-et-al-2005-smallest-grammar]], **second-hand via arXiv 1908.06428; full text of Charikar et al. not obtained**); Re-Pair lower bound $\Omega(\log n/\log\log n)$ (Bannai et al., arXiv 1908.06428, intro read).
- **Established** ([[bartholdi-figelius-lohrey-weiss-2020]], intro). The compressed word problem is in P for f.g. nilpotent groups (NC2), hyperbolic and virtually special groups, P-complete for non-abelian free groups and non-solvable finite groups, in coRP for linear groups. This is about *given* SLPs; B(2,5) and $B_0(2,5)$ are finite 5-groups. **Attribution correction:** the polynomial compressed-WP result for free groups is Lohrey's (ref [47] there), not a contribution of Bartholdi et al.; and arXiv 2203.11148 (the id in Lead's decision) is Coleman–Mitchell–Smith–Tsalakou on Todd–Coxeter, not Bartholdi et al. Both papers are now ingested.
- **Plausible.** $W=t(x,y)$ has grammar size about $|t|+|x|+|y|$ for length about $|t|\cdot m$, so an inverse-aware Re-Pair detects blocks; worst-case ratio bounds do not constrain this structured case, so measure.
- **Plausible (new analogy).** Neumann's substitution family ([[havas-ramsay-2000]]) is the same idea one level up; there, the first substitution already hides the structure from Todd–Coxeter but not from Knuth–Bendix. Expect the analogous outcome for kbmag on $W$ after block recovery.
- **Open.** A recovery-hardness theorem for scrambled substitution instances.
- Side remark (unchanged): B(2,5) is relatively free, so every kbmag rule is a law and substitution carries the rule set over once $x,y$ are known.
