## 6.1

*Issue 6 (1978) · printed p. 18 · proposed by V. A. Antonov, N. F. Sesekin*

A subgroup H of an arbitrary group G is said to be C-closed if H = C 2 (H) = C(C(H)), and weakly C-closed if C 2 (x) ⩽ H for any element x of H. The structure of the finite groups all of whose proper subgroups are C-closed was studied by Gaschütz. Describe the structure of the locally finite groups all of whose proper subgroups are weakly C-closed.

## 6.2

*Issue 6 (1978) · printed p. 18 · proposed by V. A. Antonov, N. F. Sesekin*

The totality of C-closed subgroups of an arbitrary group G is a complete lattice with respect to the operations A ∧ B = A ∩ B, A ∨ B = C(C(A) ∩ C(B)). Describe the groups whose lattice of C-closed subgroups is a sublattice of the lattice of all subgroups.

## 6.3

*Issue 6 (1978) · printed p. 18 · proposed by R. Bieri*

A group G is called of type (F P )∞ if the trivial G-module Z has a resolution by finitely generated projective G-modules. The class of all groups of type (F P )∞ has a couple of excellent closure properties with respect to extensions and amalgamated products (R. Bieri, Homological dimension of discrete groups, Queen Mary College Math. Notes, London, 1976). Is every periodic group of type (F P )∞ finite? This is related to the question whether there is an infinite periodic group with a finite presentation.

## 6.5

## 6.10

## 6.11

*Issue 6 (1978) · printed p. 18 · proposed by J. D. Dixon*

Let L be the class of locally compact groups with no small subgroups (see D. Montgomery, L. Zippin, Topological transformation groups, New York, 1955; V. M. Glushkov, Uspekhi Matem. Nauk, 12, no. 2 (1957), 3–41 (Russian)). Study extensions of groups in this class with the objective of giving a direct proof of the following: For each G ∈ L there exists an H ∈ L and a (continuous) homomorphism ϑ : G → H with a discrete kernel and an image Im ϑ satisfying Im ϑ ∩ Z(H) = 1 (Z(H) is the center of H). This result follows from the Gleason–Montgomery–Zippin solution of Hilbert’s 5th Problem (since L is the class of finite-dimensional Lie groups and the latter are locally linear). On the other hand a direct proof of this result would give a substantially shorter proof of the 5th Problem since the adjoint representation of H is faithful on Im ϑ.

## 6.21

*Issue 6 (1978) · printed p. 19 · proposed by V. D. Mazurov*

G. Higman proved that, for any prime number p, there exists a natural number χ(p) such that the nilpotency class of any finite group G having an automorphism of order p without non-trivial fixed points does not exceed χ(p). At the same time he showed that χ(p) ⩾ (p2 − 1)/4 for any such Higman’s function χ. Find the best possible Higman’s function. Is it the function defined by equalities χ(p) = (p2 − 1)/4 for p > 2 and χ(2) = 1? This is known to be true for p ⩽ 7.

## 6.39

*Issue 6 (1978) · printed p. 20 · proposed by B. I. Plotkin*

A class of groups K is said to be radical if it is closed under taking homomorphic images and normal subgroups and if every group generated by its normal K-subgroups also belongs to K. The question proposed relates to the topic “Radical classes and formulae of Narrow (first order) Predicate Calculus (NPC)”. One can show that the only non-trivial radical class definable by universal formulae of NPC is the class of all groups. Recently (in a letter to me), G. M. Bergman constructed a family of locally finite radical classes definable by formulae of NPC. Do there exist similar classes which are not locally finite and different from the class of all groups? In particular, does there exist a radical class of groups which is closed under taking Cartesian products, contains an infinite cyclic group, and is different from the class of all groups?

## 6.45

*Issue 6 (1978) · printed p. 20 · proposed by J. Wiegold*

Construct a characteristic subgroup N of a finitely generated free group F such that F/N is infinite and simple. If no such exists, it would follow that d(S 2 ) = d(S) for every infinite finitely generated simple group S. There is reason to believe that this is false.
