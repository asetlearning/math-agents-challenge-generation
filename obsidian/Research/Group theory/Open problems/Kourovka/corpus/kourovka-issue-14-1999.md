## 14.5

*Issue 14 (1999) · printed p. 70 · proposed by V. I. Arnautov*

Let G be an infinite group admitting non-discrete Hausdorff group topologies, and L (G) the lattice of all group topologies on G. a) Is it true that for any natural number k there exists a non-refinable chain τ0 < τ1 < · · · < τk of length k of Hausdorff topologies in L (G)? (For countable nilpotent groups this is true (A. G. Topale, Deposited in VINITI, 25.12.98, no. 3849– V 98 (Russian)).) b) Let k, m, n be natural numbers and let G be a nilpotent group of class k. Suppose that τ0 < τ1 < · · · < τm and τ0′ < τ1′ < · · · < τn′ are non-refinable chains of Hausdorff topologies in L (G) such that τ0 = τ0′ and τm = τn′ . Is it true that m ⩽ n · k and this inequality is best-possible? (This is true if k = 1, since for G abelian the lattice L (G) is modular.) c) Is it true that there exists a countable G such that in the lattice L (G) there are a finite non-refinable chain τ0 < τ1 < · · · < τk of Hausdorff topologies and an infinite chain {τγ′ | γ ∈ Γ} of topologies such that τ0 < τγ′ < τk for any γ ∈ Γ? d) Let G be an abelian group, k a natural number. Let Ak be the set of all those Hausdorff group topologies on G that, for every topology τ ∈ Ak , any non- refinable chain of topologies starting T from τ and terminating at the discrete topology has length k. Is it true that Ak {τγ′ | γ ∈ Γ} 6= ∅ for any infinite non-refinable chain {τγ′ | γ ∈ Γ} of Hausdorff topologies containing the discrete topology? (This is true for k = 1.)

## 14.6

*Issue 14 (1999) · printed p. 71 · proposed by _(see statement)_*

A group Γ is said to have Property Pnai if, for any finite subset F of Γ \ {1}, there exists an element y0 ∈ Γ of infinite order such that, for each x ∈ F , the canonical epimorphism from the free product hxi ∗ hy0 i onto the subgroup hx, y0 i of Γ generated by x and y0 is an isomorphism. For n ∈ {2, 3, . . .}, does P SLn (Z) have Property Pnai ? More generally, if Γ is a lattice in a connected real Lie group G which is simple and with centre reduced to {1}, does Γ have Property Pnai ? Answers are known to be “yes” if n = 2, and more generally if G has real rank 1 (M. Bekka, M. Cowling, P. de la Harpe, Publ. Math. IHES, 80 (1994), 117–134). Comments of 2018: Property Pnai has been established for relatively hyperbolic groups (G. Arzhantseva, A. Minasyan, J. Funct. Anal., 243, no. 1 (2007), 345–351) and for groups acting on CAT(0) cube complexes with appropriate conditions (A. Kar, M. Sageev, Comment. Math. Helv., 91 (2016), 543–561). Some progress has been made in the preprint (T. Poznansky, http://arxiv.org/abs/0812.2486). P. de la Harpe

## 14.7

*Issue 14 (1999) · printed p. 71 · proposed by _(see statement)_*

Let Γg be the fundamental group of a closed surface of genus g ⩾ 2. For each finite system S of generators of Γg , let βS (n) denote the number of elements of Γg which can be written p as products of at most n elements of S ∪ S −1 , and let ω(Γg , S) = lim supn→∞ n βS (n) be the growth rate of the sequence (βS (n))n⩾0 . Compute the infimum ω(Γg ) of the ω(Γg , S) over all finite sets of generators of Γg . It is easy to see that, for a free group Fk of rank k ⩾ 2, the corresponding infimum is ω(Fk ) = 2k − 1 (M. Gromov, Structures métriques pour les variétés riemanniennes, Cedic/F. Nathan, Paris, 1981, Ex. 5.13). As any generating set of Γg contains a subset of 2g − 1 elements generating a subgroup of infinite index in Γg with abelianization Z2g−1 , hence a subgroup which is free of rank 2g − 1, it follows that ω(Γg ) ⩾ 4g − 3. P. de la Harpe

## 14.46

*Issue 14 (1999) · printed p. 75 · proposed by A. Camina, C. E. Praeger*

A finite group G is said to be almost simple if T ⩽ G ⩽ Aut(T ) for some nonabelian simple group T . By definition, a finite linear space consists of a set V of points, together with a collection of k-element subsets of V , called lines (k ⩾ 3), such that every pair of points is contained in exactly one line. Classify the finite linear spaces which admit a line-transitive almost simple subgroup G of automorphisms which acts transitively on points. A. Camina, P. Neumann and C. E. Praeger have solved this problem in the case where T is an alternating group. In (A. Camina, C. E. Praeger, Aequat. Math., 61 (2001), 221–232) it is shown that a line-transitive group of automorphisms of a fi- nite linear space which is point-quasiprimitive (i. e. all of whose non-trivial normal subgroups are point-transitive) is almost simple or affine. Remarks of 2005: The cases of the following groups, but not almost simple groups with this socle, were dealt with: P SU (3, q) (W. Liu, Linear Algebra Appl., 374 (2003), 291–305); P SL(2, q) (W. Liu, J. Combin. Theory (A), 103 (2003), 209–222); Sz(q) (W. Liu, Discrete Math., 269 (2003), 181–190); Ree(q) (W. Liu, Europ. J. Combin., 25 (2004), 311–325); sporadic simple groups were done in (A. R. Camina, F. Spiezia, J. Combin. Des., 8 (2000), 353–362). Remark of 2009: It was shown (N. Gill, Trans. Amer. Math. Soc., 368 (2016), 3017–3057) that for non-desarguesian projective planes a point-quasiprimitive group would be affine. Remark of 2013: The problem is solved for large-dimensional classical groups (A. Camina, N. Gill, A. E. Zalesski, Bull. Belg. Math. Soc. Simon Stevin, 15, no. 4 (2008), 705–731).

## 14.73

*Issue 14 (1999) · printed p. 78 · proposed by C. E. Praeger*

Conjecture: There is a function f on the natural numbers such that, if Γ is a finite, vertex-transitive, locally-quasiprimitive graph of valency v, then the number of automorphisms fixing a given vertex is at most f (v). (By definition, a vertex- transitive graph Γ is locally-quasiprimitive if the stabilizer in Aut(Γ) of a vertex α is quasiprimitive (see 14.46) in its action on the set of vertices adjacent to α.) To prove the conjecture above one need only consider the case where Aut(Γ) has the property that every non-trivial normal subgroup has at most two orbits on vertices (C. E. Praeger, Ars Combin., 19 A (1985), 149–163). The analogous conjecture for finite, vertex-transitive, locally-primitive graphs was made by R. Weiss in 1978 and is still open. For non-bipartite graphs, there is a “reduction” of Weiss’ conjecture to the case where the automorphism group is almost simple (see 14.46 for definition) (M. Conder, C. H. Li, C. E. Praeger, Proc. Edinburgh Math. Soc. (2), 43, no. 1 (2000), 129–138). Comment of 2013: These conjectures have been proved in the case where there is an upper bound on the degree of any alternating group occurring as a quotient of a subgroup (C. E. Praeger, L. Pyber, P. Spiga, E. Szabó, Proc. Amer. Math. Soc., 140, no. 7 (2012), 2307–2318). Comment of 2021: This has been proved in the case where the group induced on the neighbourhood of a vertex has an abelian regular normal subgroup (P. Spiga, Bull. London Math. Soc., 48, no. 1 (2016), 12–18.)
