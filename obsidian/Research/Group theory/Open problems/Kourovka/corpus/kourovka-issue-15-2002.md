## 15.2

*Issue 15 (2002) · printed p. 81 · proposed by I. M. Isaacs*

By a theorem of W. Burnside, if χ ∈ Irr(G) and χ(1) > 1, then there exists x ∈ G such that χ(x) = 0, that is, only the linear characters are “nonvanishing”. It is interesting to consider the dual notion of nonvanishing elements of a finite group G, that is, the elements x ∈ G such that χ(x) 6= 0 for all χ ∈ Irr(G). a) It is proved (I. M. Isaacs, G. Navarro, T. R. Wolf, J. Algebra, 222, no. 2 (1999), 413–423) that if G is solvable and x ∈ G is a nonvanishing element of odd order, then x belongs to the Fitting subgroup F(G). Is this true for elements of even order too? (M. Miyamoto showed in 2008 that every nontrivial abelian normal subgroup of a finite group contains a nonvanishing element.) b) Which nonabelian simple groups have nonidentity nonvanishing elements? (For example, A7 has.)

## 15.3

*Issue 15 (2002) · printed p. 81 · proposed by I. M. Isaacs*

Let α and β be faithful non-linear irreducible characters of a finite group G. There are non-solvable groups G giving examples when the product αβ is again an irreducible character (for some of such α, β). One example is G = SL2 (5) with two irreducible characters of degree 2. In (I. Zisser, Israel J. Math., 84, no. 1–2 (1993), 147–151) it is proved that such an example exists in an alternating group An if and only if n is a square exceeding 4. But do solvable examples exist? Evidence (but no proof) that they do not is given in (I. M. Isaacs, J. Algebra, 223, no. 2 (2000), 630–646).

## 15.9

*Issue 15 (2002) · printed p. 81 · proposed by V. G. Bardakov*

An automorphism ϕ of the free group Fn on the free generators x1 , x2 , . . . , xn is called conjugating if xϕ −1 i = ti xπ(i) ti , i = 1, 2, . . . , n, for some permutation π ∈ Sn and some elements ti ∈ Fn . The set of conjugating automorphisms fixing the product x1 x2 · · · xn forms the braid group Bn . The group Bn is linear for any n ⩾ 2, while the group of all automorphisms Aut Fn is not linear for n ⩾ 3. Is the group of all conjugating automorphisms linear for n ⩾ 3?

## 15.12

*Issue 15 (2002) · printed p. 82 · proposed by _(see statement)_*

Let G be a group acting faithfully and level-transitively by automorphisms on a rooted tree T . For a vertex v of T , the rigid vertex stabilizer at v consists of those elements of G whose support in T lies entirely in the subtree Tv rooted at v. For a non-negative integer n, the n-th rigid level stabilizer is the subgroup of G generated by all rigid vertex stabilizers corresponding to the vertices at the level n of the tree T . The group G is a branch group if all rigid level stabilizers have finite index in G. For motivation, examples and known results see (R. I. Grigorchuk, in: New horizons in pro-p groups, Birkhäuser, Boston, 2000, 121–179). Do there exist branch groups with Kazhdan’s T -property? (See A14.34.) L. Bartholdi, R. I. Grigorchuk, Z. Šuniḱ

## 15.41

*Issue 15 (2002) · printed p. 84 · proposed by M. R. Vaughan-Lee*

## 15.45

## 15.46

*Issue 15 (2002) · printed p. 85 · proposed by V. M. Levchuk*

Can the question 7.28 on conditions for admissibility of an elementary carpet A = {Ar | r ∈ Φ} be reduced to Lie rank 1 if K is a field? A carpet A of type Φ of additive subgroups of K is called addmissible if in the Chevalley group over K associated with the root system Φ the subgroup hxr (Ar ) | r ∈ Φi intersects xr (K) in xr (Ar ). More precisely, is it true that the carpet A is admissible if and only if the subcarpets {Ar , A−r }, r ∈ Φ, of rank 1 are admissible? The answer is known to be affirmative if the field K is locally finite (V. M. Levchuk, Algebra and Logic, 22, no. 5 (1983), 362–371).

## 15.47

## 15.76

*Issue 15 (2002) · printed p. 89 · proposed by B. I. Plotkin*

b) If Θ is a variety of groups, then let Θ0 denote the category of all free groups of finite rank in Θ. It is proved (G. Mashevitzky, B. Plotkin, E. Plotkin J. Algebra, 282 (2004), 490–512) that if Θ is the variety of all groups, then every automorphism of the category Θ0 is an inner one. The same is true if Θ is the variety of all nilpotent groups of class ⩽ d, for d ⩾ 2 (A. Tsurkov, Int. J. Algebra Comput., 17 (2007), 1273–1281). Is this true for the varieties of solvable groups? of metabelian groups? An automorphism ϕ of a category is called inner if it is isomorphic to the identity automorphism. Let s : 1 → ϕ be a function defining this isomorphism. Then for every object A we have an isomorphism sA : A → ϕ(A) and for any morphism of objects µ : A → B we have ϕ(µ) = sB µs−1 A .

## 15.77
