## 10.2

*Issue 10 (1986) · printed p. 38 · proposed by V. S. Anashin*

A mixed identity of a group G is, by definition, an identity of an algebraic system obtained from G by supplementing its signature by some set of 0-ary operations. One can develop a theory of mixed varieties of groups on the basis of this notion (see, for example, V. S. Anashin, Math. USSR Sbornik, 57 (1987), 171–182). Construct an example of a class of groups which is not a mixed variety, but which is closed under taking factor-groups, Cartesian products, and those subgroups of Cartesian powers that contain the diagonal subgroup.

## 10.3

*Issue 10 (1986) · printed p. 38 · proposed by V. S. Anashin*

Characterize (in terms of bases of mixed identities or in terms of generating groups) minimal mixed varieties of groups.

## 10.4

## 10.12

*Issue 10 (1986) · printed p. 38 · proposed by R. I. Grigorchuk*

Does there exist a finitely generated semigroup S with cancellation having non-exponential growth and such that its group of left quotients G = S −1 S (which exists) is a group of exponential growth? An affirmative answer would give a positive solution to Problem 12 in (S. Wagon, The Banach–Tarski Paradox, Cambridge Univ. Press, 1985).

## 10.13

*Issue 10 (1986) · printed p. 38 · proposed by R. I. Grigorchuk*

Does there exist a massive set of independent elements in a free group F2 on free generators a, b, that is, a set E of irreducible words on the alphabet a, b, a−1 , b−1 such that 1) no element w ∈ E belongs to the normal closure p of E \ {w}, and 2) the massiveness condition is satisfied: lim n |En | = 3, where En is the set of n→∞ all words of length n in E?

## 10.16

*Issue 10 (1986) · printed p. 39 · proposed by L. A. Kurdachenko*

A class of groups is called a direct variety if it is closed under taking sub- groups, factor-groups, and direct products (Yu. M. Gorchakov, Groups with Finite Classes of Conjugate Elements, Moscow, Nauka, 1978 (Russian)). It is obvious that the class of F C-groups is a direct variety. P. Hall (J. London Math. Soc., 34, no. 3 (1959), 289–304) showed that the class of finite groups and the class of abelian groups taken together do not generate the class of F C-groups as a direct variety, and it was shown in (L. A. Kurdachenko, Ukrain. Math. J., 39, no. 3 (1987), 255–259) that the direct variety of F C-groups is also not generated by the class of groups with finite derived subgroups. Is the direct variety of F C-groups generated by the class of groups with finite derived subgroups together with the class of F C-groups having quasicyclic derived subgroups?

## 10.31

*Issue 10 (1986) · printed p. 40 · proposed by Yu. I. Merzlyakov*

Suppose that G is an algebraic group, H is a closed normal subgroup of G and f : G → G/H is the canonical homomorphism. What conditions ensure that there exists a rational section s : G/H → G such that sf = 1? For partial results, see (Yu. I. Merzlyakov, Rational Groups, Nauka, Moscow, 1987, § 37 (Russian)).

## 10.32

*Issue 10 (1986) · printed p. 40 · proposed by Yu. I. Merzlyakov*

A group word is said to be universal on a group G if its values on G run over the whole of G. For an arbitrary constant c > 8/5, J. L. Brenner, R. J. Evans, and D. M. Silberger (Proc. Amer. Math. Soc., 96, no. 1 (1986), 23–28) proved that there exists a number N0 = N0 (c) such that the word xr y s , rs 6= 0, is universal on the alternating group An for any n ⩾ max {N0 , c · log m(r, s)}, where m(r, s) is the product of all primes dividing rs if r, s 6∈ {−1, 1}, and m(r, s) = 1 if r, s ∈ {−1, 1}. For example, one can take N0 (5/2) = 5 and N0 (2) = 29. Find an analogous bound for the degree of the symmetric group Sn under hypothesis that at least one of the r, s is odd: up to now, here only a more crude bound n ⩾ max {6, 4m(r, s) − 4} is known (M. Droste, Proc. Amer. Math. Soc., 96, no. 1 (1986), 18–22).

## 10.36

Soulé, in: Algebraic K-theory, Proc. Conf., Northwestern Univ., Evanston, Ill., 1976, (Lect. Notes Math., 551), Springer, Berlin, 1976, 164–169).

## 10.40

*Issue 10 (1986) · printed p. 41 · proposed by G. A. Noskov*

P(H. Bass). Let G be a group, e an idempotent matrix over ZG and let tr e = eg g, eg ∈ Z. Strong conjecture: for any non-trivial x ∈ G, the equation P g∈G P eg = 0 holds where ∼ denotes conjugacy in G. Weak conjecture: eg = 0. The g∼x g∈G strong conjecture has been proved for finite, abelian and linear groups and the weak conjecture has been proved for residually finite groups. H. Bass’ Comment of 2005: Significant progress has been made; a good up-to-date account is in the paper (A. J. Berrick, I. Chatterji, G. Mislin, Math. Ann., 329 (2004), 597–621).

## 10.49

*Issue 10 (1986) · printed p. 42 · proposed by B. Poizat*

Does there exist a group G satisfying the following four conditions: 1) G is simple, moreover, there is an integer n such that G = C n for any conjugacy class C, 2) all maximal abelian subgroups of G are conjugate in G, 3) every maximal abelian subgroup of G is self-normalizing and it is the centralizer of any of its nontrivial elements, 4) there is an integer m such that if H is a maximal abelian subgroup of G and a ∈ G \ H then every element in G is a product of m elements in aH? Comment of 2005: There is a partial solution in (E. Jaligot, A. Ould Houcine, J. Algebra, 280 (2004), 772–796).

## 10.50

*Issue 10 (1986) · printed p. 42 · proposed by A. V. Rukolaine*

Complex characters of a finite group G induced by linear characters of cyclic subgroups are called induced cyclic characters of G. Computer-aided computations (A. V. Rukolaine, Abstracts of the 10th All–Union Sympos. on Group Theory, Gomel’, 1986, p. 199 (Russian)) show that there exist groups (for example, S5 , SL2 (13), M11 ) all of whose irreducible complex characters are integral linear combinations of induced cyclic characters and the principal character of the group. Describe all finite groups with this property.

## 10.59

*Issue 10 (1986) · printed p. 43 · proposed by A. I. Sozutov*

Is a p′ -group G locally nilpotent if it admits a splitting automorphism ϕ of p−1 prime order p such that all subgroups of the form hg, g ϕ , . . . , g ϕ i are nilpotent? An 2 p−1 automorphism ϕ of order p is called splitting if gg ϕ g ϕ · · · g ϕ = 1 for all g ∈ G.

## 10.60

*Issue 10 (1986) · printed p. 43 · proposed by A. I. Sozutov*

## 10.61

*Issue 10 (1986) · printed p. 43 · proposed by A. I. Sozutov*

Does the set-theoretic union of the kernels of all Frobenius subgroups of G with complement hai constitute a subgroup? For definitions see 6.55; see also (A. I. ## 10.74

*Issue 10 (1986) · printed p. 44 · proposed by V. P. Shunkov*

Suppose that a group G contains an element a of prime order such that its centralizer CG (a) is finite and all subgroups ha, ag i, g ∈ G, are finite and almost all of them are soluble. Is G locally finite? This problem is closely connected with 6.56. The question was solved in the positive for a number of very important partial cases by the author (Abstracts on Group Theory of the Mal’cev Int. Conf. on Algebra, Novosibirsk, 1989, p. 145 (Russian)).

## 10.75

*Issue 10 (1986) · printed p. 44 · proposed by V. P. Shunkov*

Suppose that a group G contains an element a of prime order p such that the normalizer of every finite subgroup containing a has finite periodic part and all subgroups ha, ag i, g ∈ G, are finite and almost all of them are soluble. Does G possess a periodic part if p > 2? It was proved in (V. P. Shunkov, Groups with involutions, Preprints no. 4, 5, 12 of the Comput. centre of SO AN SSSR, Krasnoyarsk, 1986 (Russian)) that if a is a point, then the answer is affirmative; on the other hand, a group with a point a of order 2 satisfying the given hypothesis, which has no periodic part, was exhibited in the same works. For the definition of a point see (V. I. Senashov, V. P. Shunkov, Algebra and Logic, 22, no. 1 (1983), 66–81).

## 10.77

Oxford (2), 49, no. 196 (1998), 491–499) gave a positive answer to the question a) in the case where G is residually finite.
