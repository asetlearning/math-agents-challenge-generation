## 19.1

*Issue 19 (2018) · printed p. 129 · proposed by M. K. Azarian*

An element g of a group G is a non-near generator of G if for every subset S ⊆ G such that |G : hg, Si| < ∞ it follows that |G : hSi| < ∞. The set of all non-near generators of G forms a characteristic subgroup of G called the lower near Frattini subgroup of G, denoted by λ(G). A subgroup M ⩽ G is nearly maximal in G if it is maximal with respect to being of infinite index in G. The intersection of all nearly maximal subgroups forms a characteristic subgroup called the upper near Frattini subgroup of G, denoted by µ(G). In general, λ(G) ⩽ µ(G). If λ(G) = µ(G), then this subgroup is called the near Frattini subgroup of G, denoted by ψ(G). a) Is it true that ψ(G) = 1 if G is the knot group of any product of knots? b) Is it true that ψ(G) = 1 if G is a cable knot group?

## 19.8

*Issue 19 (2018) · printed p. 130 · proposed by V. G. Bardakov*

A word in an alphabet A = {a1 , a2 , . . . , an } is called a palindrome if it reads the same from left to right and from right to left. Let k a non-negative integer. A word in the alphabet A is called an almost k-palindrome if it can be transformed into a palindrome by changing ⩽ k letters in it. (So an almost 0-palindrome is a palindrome.) Let elements of a free group F2 = hx, yi be represented as words in the alphabet {x±1 , y ±1 }. Do there exist positive integers m and c such that every element in F2 is a product of ⩽ c almost m-palindromes? It is known that for m = 0 there is no such a number c.

## 19.19

*Issue 19 (2018) · printed p. 131 · proposed by P. J. Cameron*

A finite transitive permutation group G is said to have the road closure property if, given any orbit O of G on 2-sets, and any proper block of imprimitivity for G acting on O, the graph with edge set O \ B is connected. Such a group must be primitive, and basic (not contained in a wreath product with the product action); it cannot have an imprimitive subgroup of index 2. In addition, such a group cannot be one of the permutation groups arising from triality (whose socle is D4 (q) and intersects the point stabiliser in the parabolic subgroup corresponding to the three leaves in the Coxeter–Dynkin diagram for D4 ). Road closure conjecture: these necessary conditions are sufficient for the road closure property.

## 19.28

*Issue 19 (2018) · printed p. 133 · proposed by A. L. Fel’shtyn, E. V. Troitsky*

Let G be a group, and ϕ an automorphism of G. Elements x, y ∈ G are said to be ϕ-conjugate if x = z −1 yϕ(z) for some z ∈ G. The ϕ-conjugacy is an equivalence relation; the number of ϕ-conjugacy classes is denoted by R(ϕ). Conjecture: If a finitely generated residually finite group G has an automorphism ϕ such that R(ϕ) is finite, then G has a soluble subgroup of finite index. The conjecture is proved if ϕ has prime order (E. Jabara, J. Algebra, 320, no. 10 (2008), 3671–3679). If G is infinitely generated, then G does not have to be almost solvable (K. Dekimpe, D. Gonçalves, Bull. London Math. Soc., 46, no. 4 (2014), 737– 746).

## 19.42

*Issue 19 (2018) · printed p. 135 · proposed by I. Kapovich*

Suppose that H is a word-hyperbolic subgroup of a word-hyperbolic group G such that the inclusion of H to G extends to a continuous H-equivariant map j : ∂H → ∂G between their hyperbolic boundaries. If such an extension exists, it is unique and j is called the Cannon–Thurston map. a) Is it true that for every point p ∈ ∂G its full preimage j −1 (p) is finite? b) Moreover, is it true that there is a number N = N (G, H) < ∞ such that for every p ∈ ∂G the full preimage j −1 (p) consists of at most N points? The answer to both questions is “yes” in all the cases where the Cannon–Thurston map is known to exist and where it has been possible to analyze the multiplicity of this map. This includes the original set-up considered by Cannon and Thurston where H is the surface group, and G is the fundamental group of a closed hyperbolic 3-manifold fibering over the circle with that surface as a fiber. In this case, j : S1 → S2 is a uniformly finite-to-one continuous surjective ‘Peano curve’.

## 19.102

*Issue 19 (2018) · printed p. 144 · proposed by E. Ventura*

A subgroup H of a free group F is called inert if r(H ∩ K) ⩽ r(K) for every K ⩽ F ; and compressed if r(H) ⩽ r(K) for every H ⩽ K ⩽ F . Is it true that compressed subgroups are inert?

## 19.103

*Issue 19 (2018) · printed p. 144 · proposed by E. Ventura*

Is it true that an intersection of compressed subgroups is compressed? It is known that arbitrary intersections of inert subgroups are inert.

## 19.104

*Issue 19 (2018) · printed p. 144 · proposed by E. Ventura*

Is there an algorithm which decides whether a given subgroup of F is inert? An algorithm to decide whether a given H is compressed is known.

## 19.105

*Issue 19 (2018) · printed p. 144 · proposed by E. Ventura*

Is it true that fixed subgroups of endomorphisms of F are inert? It is easy to see that retracts of free groups are compressed, and that they are inert if and only if fixed subgroups of endomorphisms of free groups are inert. Therefore the question is equivalent to the following: are retracts of free groups inert?

## 19.106

*Issue 19 (2018) · printed p. 145 · proposed by F. Wagner*

Let G be a uniformly locally finite group (which means that there is a function f on the natural numbers such that any subgroup generated by n elements has size at most f (n)). Suppose that for any two definable subgroups H and K, the intersection H ∩ K has finite index either in H or in K. Is G necessarily nilpotent- by-finite? Or even finite-by-abelian-by-finite?

## 19.107

*Issue 19 (2018) · printed p. 145 · proposed by P. Wesolek*

Let A E be the smallest class of locally compact groups such that (1) A E contains the compact groups and the discrete amenable groups; (2) A E is closed under taking closed subgroups, Hausdorff quotients, directed unions of open subgroups, and group extensions. Is every amenable locally compact group an element of A E ? (A negative answer would follow from a positive answer to 19.21.)

## 19.109 `[ANSWERED]`

A maximal subgroup of a maximal subgroup is called second maximal.
