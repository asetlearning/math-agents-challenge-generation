# Kourovka Problem 12.15 — Normal-Closure Conjugacy and the Derived Subgroup

## Source statement

> 12.15. Suppose that, in a finite 2-group G, any two elements are conjugate whenever their normal closures coincide. Is it true that the derived subgroup of G is abelian? E. A. Golikova, A. I. Starostin

Visually checked against Notebook No. 21, PDF p. 58. The problem remains unstarred and has no editor or later comment in the current corpus.

## Status and known context

The configured July 2026 Notebook still lists the question as open. Exact-number and distinctive-phrase searches on 2026-08-14 found the current Notebook statement but no claimed solution. This is a preliminary Lead screen; the problem agent must repeat and document the mandatory staleness search.

## Tractability expectation

The hypothesis and conclusion are exact predicates on finite 2-groups. A bounded GAP search can either find a complete counterexample or establish a reproducible small-order floor and expose which normal-closure fibres force the condition. The falsifiable expectation for cycle 1 is a certified witness, a complete stated order range, or a structural reduction; otherwise the line should stop.

## First attack

Enumerate finite 2-groups by increasing order. Partition elements by normal closure, test whether every fibre is a single conjugacy class, and then test whether the derived subgroup is abelian. Save library identifiers and independently reconstruct any witness from a presentation.

## What an answer would require

A refutation is one explicitly presented finite 2-group with the normal-closure property and nonabelian derived subgroup, with both checks independently reproducible. A positive answer requires a proof that the fibre condition forces G'' = 1; finite computation alone cannot establish it.

## Tooling

GAP SmallGroups, exact conjugacy classes, normal closures, derived series, and a separate presentation-level verifier for any candidate.
