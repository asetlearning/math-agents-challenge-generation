# Kourovka Problem 20.31 — Isospectral Groups S10 J2

## Source statement

> 20.31. Let ω(G) denote the set of element orders of a finite group G, and h(G) the number of pairwise nonisomorphic finite groups H with ω(H) = ω(G). Find h(L), where a) L is the symmetric group of degree 10; b) L is the automorphism group of the simple sporadic Janko group J2 ; c) P SL(2, q) < L ⩽ Aut(P SL(2, q)). See the current status in (M. A. Grechkoseeva, V. D. Mazurov, W. J. Shi, A. V. Vasil’ev, N. Yang, Commun. Math. Stat., 11, no. 2 (2023), 169–194; Subsection 4.2). M. A. Grechkoseeva, A. V. Vasil’ev

## Why this is in the tractable 50

Parts (a) and (b) concern fixed named finite groups and can be separated from the parametric part (c). Spectrum comparison can be attacked with exact libraries and explicit candidate classification.

This is a falsifiable selection judgment: the first cycle should produce either a bounded search with meaningful coverage, a named reduction, or a concrete obstruction. Failure to do so is evidence to skip rather than extend.

## Proposed first attack

1. Formalize every hypothesis as an exact predicate and test it on hand-checked positive and negative examples.
2. Run the smallest complete family available, recording the exact library/version, object identifiers, and coverage bound.
3. Inspect the first near misses to conjecture a structural reduction; do not infer the universal statement from a clean finite range.
4. Seek a certificate independent of the discovery computation.

## What an answer would require

A concrete almost-simple witness or a reduction separating sporadic, alternating, and Lie-type families. A positive computational pass alone is only necessary evidence unless exhaustive coverage of the mathematical target is proved.
