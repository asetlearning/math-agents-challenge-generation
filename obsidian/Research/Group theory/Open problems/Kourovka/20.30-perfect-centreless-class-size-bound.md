## Why this is in the tractable 50

The problem is phrased in terms of finite counterexample search. It admits exact finite experiments that can either locate a counterexample or isolate a sharply stated structural lemma; that makes a three-hour first cycle informative even if it does not close the problem.

This is a falsifiable selection judgment: the first cycle should produce either a bounded search with meaningful coverage, a named reduction, or a concrete obstruction. Failure to do so is evidence to skip rather than extend.

## Proposed first attack

1. Formalize every hypothesis as an exact predicate and test it on hand-checked positive and negative examples.
2. Run the smallest complete family available, recording the exact library/version, object identifiers, and coverage bound.
3. Inspect the first near misses to conjecture a structural reduction; do not infer the universal statement from a clean finite range.
4. Seek a certificate independent of the discovery computation.

## What an answer would require

A smallest explicit counterexample with all hypotheses independently checked, or a lemma excluding a broad finite range. A positive computational pass alone is only necessary evidence unless exhaustive coverage of the mathematical target is proved.

## Tooling

GAP small-group and perfect-group libraries; SAT/constraint search only after a precise encoding. Every search must be bounded and every reported object must be reconstructible from saved commands.

## Main risks

- The finite search may address only a special case of the quantified statement.
- Library coverage or classification assumptions may be incomplete.
- A passing invariant may be necessary but not sufficient.
- The problem may have been resolved after the July 2026 source freeze; the fresh staleness check is therefore a gate.
