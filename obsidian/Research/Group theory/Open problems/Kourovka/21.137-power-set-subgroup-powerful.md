# Kourovka Problem 21.137 — Power Sets That Are Powerful Subgroups

## Source statement

> 21.137. If the p-th powers in a finite p-group form a subgroup, must that subgroup be powerful? That is, for p ≠ 2, if the p-th powers in a p-group of exponent p² form a subgroup, must that subgroup be abelian? For a 2-group of exponent 8, if the squares form a subgroup, must that subgroup be abelian? L. Wilson

Visually checked against Notebook No. 21, PDF p. 184. The problem is unstarred and has no attached editor comment.

## Status and known context

The configured July 2026 Notebook lists the problem as open. Williams's paper “Quasi-powerful p-groups” records the odd-prime question as Question 4.7 and proves nearby positive power-structure results under the stronger quasi-powerful hypothesis. The problem agent's mandatory 2026-08-14 staleness search then found an April 2026 public Lean artifact by Elias Judin / Aristotle (Harmonic) claiming an exact counterexample to the exponent-8 clause: `D8 wr C2`, of order 128, whose squares form a nonabelian subgroup. Work stopped and the artifact was routed to Validator; this note does not certify that external claim.

## Tractability expectation

All hypotheses are exact finite-group predicates. The exponent-p² odd case and exponent-8 case admit sharply bounded catalogue searches. The falsifiable cycle-1 target is a certified counterexample, a complete small-order floor split by prime, or a commutator/power reduction that materially narrows a proof.

## First attack

For each catalogue p-group in a declared range, compute the set of p-th powers, test closure directly, and when closed test whether it is powerful (and in the stated exponent cases, abelian). Start with exponent 8 at p = 2 and exponent 9 at p = 3; preserve identifiers and presentations for every near miss.

## What an answer would require

A refutation is a reconstructible finite p-group whose p-th-power set is a subgroup but is not powerful. A positive answer requires a general proof; bounded enumeration is only evidence and a lower bound on counterexample order.

## Tooling

GAP SmallGroups and polycyclic methods, explicit set-closure tests, exponent and commutator calculations, and independent presentation-level verification of any candidate.
