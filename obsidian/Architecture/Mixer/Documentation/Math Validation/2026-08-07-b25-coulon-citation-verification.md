# Coulon citation — verification

**Two findings: the draft cites the wrong paper, and the paraphrase overstates what the source says.**
Researcher's sweep could not trace the claim because it was looking in the cited work, where it does
not appear.

Method note: WebSearch was unavailable, so I fetched arXiv directly and extracted the PDF text with a
stdlib zlib decoder. The extraction strips inter-word spaces and mangles some math glyphs; the quotes
below are de-mangled by me and are reproducible by anyone repeating the extraction.

## Finding 1 — WRONG PAPER (blocking for the draft)

The draft's reference list (`infinite_b25/paper/e7_survives_g4.md:240`) says:

> **[Coulon2013]** R. Coulon, *Small cancellation theory and Burnside problem*, Internat. J. Algebra
> Comput. **24** (2014), no. 3, 251–345; arXiv:1302.6933.

Confirmed via arXiv that this is the correct title/author for 1302.6933 — and its abstract is about
the Delzant–Gromov geometric approach, with **no exponent bound**. The `n₀ > 100` chain and the cited
statement numbers are in a **different paper**:

> R. Coulon, *Partial periodic quotient of groups acting on a hyperbolic space*,
> Ann. Inst. Fourier (Grenoble) **66** (2016), no. 5, 1773–1857; arXiv:1311.0855.

Our own internal provenance already names the second paper (`math_expert_R2_full.md:35–36`), so the
records disagree with the draft's bibliography. **A referee checking "Proposition 6.1" in the cited
IJAC paper will not find it.** Also: the arXiv title is singular *quotient*, our notes say
*quotients*; and the label `[Coulon2013]` is ambiguous — both papers were arXiv-2013 while publishing
in 2014 and 2016. Relabel to `[Coulon2016]`.

## Finding 2 — the source steps DO exist, verbatim

In the correct paper, both locations check out:

- **Run-up to Proposition 6.1:** "The sequence (λ_n) converges to 0 as n approaches infinity.
  **Therefore there exists an integer n₀ > 100 such that for every n > n₀** [conditions (12)–(15)]."
- **Proposition 6.1:** "There exist positive constants ρ₀, δ₁, L_S such that for every integer κ₀
  there is an integer n₀ with the following properties. … **Let n₁ > n₀ and n > n₁ be an odd
  integer.**"
- **§5 standing hypothesis** (stronger support than the notes claimed): "we suppose that there exists
  a loxodromic element h ∈ N which is primitive as an element of N **and an odd integer n > 100**
  such that (i) H is the cyclic subgroup generated by h^n…"
- **Theorem 6.9:** "There is a critical exponent n₁ such that every odd integer n > n₁ which is a
  multiple of e(N;X) has the following property…"
- **Theorem 6.15:** same shape, "There exists a critical exponent n₁ such that every odd integer
  n > n₁ which is a multiple of e(N;X)…"

**Not confirmed:** that the relative version is *Theorem 6.20*. At that number the text reads as a
subsection heading "Amalgamated product", followed by Theorem 6.17. A relatively-hyperbolic version
does exist near the end ("Assume that G is hyperbolic relatively to {H₁,…,H_m} … critical exponent
n₁"), but I could not confirm its number. Drop or re-check the 6.20 citation.

## Finding 3 — the paraphrase is NOT faithful. Reword.

Three defects, in order of referee risk:

1. **"Requires" asserts a necessity the paper does not prove.** The `n₀ > 100` step is an *existence*
   claim from a convergence argument — λ_n → 0, therefore *some* n₀ works, and the author takes it
   above 100 as margin. Nothing there shows n ≤ 100 fails. The defensible claim is that the theorems
   **as proved do not cover** n = 5.
2. **The threshold is not 100.** The chain is n odd, `n > n₁ > n₀ > 100`, and **n₁ is never
   computed**. "n > 100" is a lower bound on a lower bound; it simultaneously understates the true gap
   and overstates our precision about it.
3. **Theorem-level hypotheses say n₁, not 100.** Theorems 6.9/6.15 are phrased with an unquantified
   critical exponent. The literal "odd n > 100" lives in the §5 small-cancellation hypotheses and in
   the n₀ choice — cite it there, not at theorem level.

**Recommended draft wording:**

> …and Coulon's partial periodic quotient theorems [Coulon2016] apply only to odd exponents n above an
> unquantified critical exponent n₁, the construction fixing n₀ > 100 and requiring n > n₁ > n₀
> (Prop. 6.1; Thms 6.9, 6.15), with the small-cancellation hypotheses of §5 likewise assuming an odd
> n > 100. Exponent 5 therefore lies outside the range these theorems cover, by a margin that is not
> merely numerical but unquantified.

Same fix applies to `FINAL_REPORT.md:142` ("formally requires n > 100" — "formally" helps, "requires"
still overclaims) and `LADDER.md:47`.
